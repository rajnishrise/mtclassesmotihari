📌 Chapter snapshot

  • A current-carrying wire behaves like a magnet (Oersted, 1820). Electricity and magnetism are linked.
  • Field lines go N → S outside a magnet and S → N inside; they are closed curves, never intersect, and are crowded where the field is strong.
  • Straight wire: concentric circles; direction by the right-hand thumb rule. B increases with current and decreases with distance.
  • Circular loop: lines almost straight at the centre; field ∝ number of turns. Solenoid: like a bar magnet, uniform field inside; with a soft iron core it is an electromagnet.
  • Force on a conductor: largest when current ⊥ field, zero when parallel. Direction by Fleming's left-hand rule (Field, Current, Motion).
  • Domestic circuits: live (red), neutral (black), earth (green), 220 V; separate 15 A and 5 A circuits; fuse/MCB protect against overloading and short circuits; earthing prevents shocks.
  • Not in the rationalised syllabus: electric motor, electromagnetic induction and generator. Skip them.
  • CBSE weightage: unit Effects of Current (Ch 11 and 12) carries 13 marks. Field-line drawings and the two hand rules come every year.
12.1

Magnetic field and field lines

In Activity 12.1 a compass needle placed near a copper wire deflects when current flows. In 1820 Hans Christian Oersted discovered exactly this: electricity and magnetism are linked. The unit of magnetic field strength, the oersted, is named after him (the SI unit is the tesla).

A compass needle is a small bar magnet. Its north-seeking end is its north pole. Like poles repel, unlike poles attract. The region around a magnet where its force can be detected is its magnetic field. A magnetic field has both size and direction.

Properties of magnetic field lines

  • They emerge from the north pole and merge at the south pole outside the magnet; inside, they go from S to N. So they are closed curves.
  • The relative strength of the field is shown by how close the lines are: crowded = stronger (near the poles).
  • No two field lines ever cross. If they did, a compass at the crossing would point in two directions at once, which is impossible (CBSE 2024, 2025).

🧪 Try itDraw field lines with a compass

  1. Place a bar magnet on a sheet of white paper fixed to a board and mark its outline.
  2. Put a small compass near the north pole. Mark the two ends of the needle with dots. Move the compass so its south end sits on the last dot, and mark again.
  3. Join the dots to get one field line. Repeat from other starting points. Do your lines ever cross?

12.2

Magnetic field due to a current-carrying conductor

In Activity 12.4, a compass under a straight wire deflects one way; when the current is reversed, it deflects the other way. So the direction of the magnetic field depends on the direction of the current.

12.2.1 Straight wire and 12.2.2 Right-hand thumb rule

Right-hand thumb rule: hold the wire in your right hand with the thumb along the current; your curled fingers show the direction of the field.
  • The field lines are concentric circles centred on the wire, in a plane perpendicular to it.
  • The field is stronger when the current increases (B ∝ I) and weaker farther from the wire (B ∝ 1/r). So the circles get farther apart as you move away (CBSE 2026).
  • This rule is also called Maxwell's corkscrew rule.

12.2.3 Circular loop

The field at the centre is proportional to the current and to the number of turns n: a coil of n turns gives n times the field of one turn, because the fields of all turns add up.

🏆 Clock-face rule for a loop

Look at the face of the loop. If the current flows clockwise, that face is a South pole (field goes into the face). If it flows anticlockwise, that face is a North pole (field comes out).

12.2.4 Solenoid and electromagnet

A coil of many circular turns of insulated copper wire wrapped closely in the shape of a cylinder is a solenoid.

Bar magnet (permanent)Electromagnet
Magnetism is permanentMagnetic only while current flows
Strength is fixedStrength can be changed (current, turns, core)
Poles are fixedPoles reverse when current is reversed
Made of steel / alloys like alnicoSoft iron core inside a solenoid

🧪 Try itBuild a nail electromagnet (with a teacher)

12.3

Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field

A current produces a magnetic field that exerts a force on a nearby magnet. The French scientist André-Marie Ampère suggested the reverse must also be true: a magnet exerts an equal and opposite force on a current-carrying conductor.

  • The direction of the force depends on the direction of both the current and the magnetic field.
  • The force (displacement) is largest when the current is at right angles to the field, and zero when the current is parallel to the field (CBSE 2023).
  • The force increases if the current is increased, a stronger magnet is used, or a longer rod lies in the field.
Fleming's left-hand rule: Forefinger → Field, Middle finger → Current, Thumb → Motion (force)

⚡ Moving charges count as currents

  • A beam of positive particles (protons, alpha particles) is a current in the direction they move.
  • A beam of electrons is a current in the opposite direction to their motion.
  • The magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it changes the direction of motion (velocity, momentum) but not the speed or mass.

🏥 Magnetism in medicine

Weak ionic currents along nerve cells produce tiny magnetic fields in our body; the heart and brain are the main sources. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) uses strong magnetic fields to make images of the inside of the body for diagnosis. Devices such as electric motors, loudspeakers and measuring instruments also use the force on a current in a magnetic field.

  3. Reverse the battery, then turn the magnet over. Did your predictions come true?

12.4

Domestic electric circuits

Electricity reaches our homes from an overhead pole or underground cable through the mains. In India the potential difference between the live and neutral wires is 220 V (alternating current of frequency 50 Hz).

WireInsulation colourJob
Live (positive)red (new code: brown)at 220 V; switches and fuses go on this wire
Neutral (negative)black (new code: light blue)at 0 V; completes the circuit
Earthgreen (or green-yellow)joined to a metal plate deep in the earth; safety wire
🔌

Two separate circuits

15 A circuit for high-power appliances (geyser, AC, heater, iron). 5 A circuit for bulbs and fans.

🔀

Parallel connection

Every appliance gets 220 V and has its own switch, so each can be used independently.

🌍

Earthing

The metal body of an iron, toaster or fridge is joined to the earth wire. Any leakage current goes to the earth at almost zero potential, so the user does not get a severe shock.

Safety: overloading, short circuit, fuse and MCB

  • Short circuit: the live wire and neutral wire touch directly (damaged insulation or a fault). The resistance becomes almost zero, so the current increases heavily.
  • Overloading: too many appliances on one socket, or an accidental rise in supply voltage, make the current exceed the safe limit.
  • Fuse: a thin wire of low melting point in series with the circuit. Joule heating (H = I²Rt) melts it when the current exceeds its rating, breaking the circuit and protecting appliances from damage and fire.
  • Never replace a fuse with a thicker wire or a higher rating: the circuit would no longer be protected.
  • MCB (miniature circuit breaker) switches off automatically on overload and can simply be switched on again.
ExA 2 kW electric oven is used on a 220 V domestic circuit with a current rating of 5 A. What will happen?

I = P/V = 2000/220 = 9.1 A, more than 5 A. The circuit is overloaded: the fuse will blow (or the MCB trip). The oven should be used on a 15 A circuit.

🧪 Try itSafety walk at home (with an adult)

  1. Find the main switch, the MCB box and the electricity meter. Note the ratings written on the MCBs (6 A, 16 A, 32 A …).
  2. Find a three-pin plug. Which pin is longest and thickest? (Earth: it connects first and disconnects last.)
  3. Look for overloaded multi-plugs or damaged wires and suggest fixes. Never open a switchboard yourself.

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Using the right hand for force. Force needs the left hand (Fleming's); the right hand is for the field around a current.
  2. Forgetting that an electron beam means current in the opposite direction.
  3. Drawing field lines that cross, or that stop at the poles. They are closed loops that never intersect.
  4. Drawing the field inside a solenoid as curved. Inside, it is uniform: parallel, equally spaced lines.
  5. Saying the earth wire is the live wire. Green = earth, red/brown = live, black/blue = neutral.
  6. Saying a fuse is connected in parallel. It is in series, on the live wire.
  7. Saying a magnetic force changes the speed of a particle. It changes only the direction (velocity and momentum).
  8. Studying the electric motor, generator and electromagnetic induction. They are not in the rationalised CBSE syllabus.

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Science (086) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 39 questions: Section A has 20 one-mark questions (16 MCQs and 4 assertion–reason), Section B 6 two-mark, Section C 7 three-mark, Section D 3 five-mark, and Section E 3 four-mark case studies. ✍️ How marks are given

  • Field-line diagrams: correct shape ½ to 1, arrows on lines ½, labels (N, S, current direction) ½.
  • Rules: state the rule fully (which hand, which finger stands for what) for 1 mark; a half statement gets ½.
  • Direction questions: the final direction ½, the reason (rule used, current opposite to electron flow) ½.

🏆 Answer-writing tips

  1. Always put arrows on field lines. Lines without arrows lose marks.
  2. For solenoids, show uniform parallel lines inside and bar-magnet-like loops outside.
  3. In domestic circuit answers, use the words "in series with the live wire" for a fuse and "low resistance path to earth" for earthing.

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q36 · 3 marks(a) Describe an activity to show that a current-carrying conductor experiences a force in a magnetic field. (b) An electron beam moving horizontally from the back wall towards the front wall of a chamber is deflected to the right by a magnetic field. What is the direction of the field?

(a) Hang a small aluminium rod horizontally between the poles of a horseshoe magnet (N pole below and S pole above, so the field points upward), connected in series with a battery, key and rheostat. On passing current, the rod is displaced; reversing the current or the poles reverses the displacement. 2

(b) The current is opposite to the electron flow, i.e. from the front wall to the back wall. Applying Fleming's left-hand rule with the force to the right, the field is vertically downward. 1

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q37 · 3 marks(a) On what does the pattern of the magnetic field produced by a current depend? (b) Points X, Y and Z are at increasing distances from a long straight current-carrying wire. At which point is the field strongest? Justify. (c) How can the field at a point be increased?

(a) On the shape of the conductor (straight wire, loop, solenoid). 1

(b) At X, the nearest point: the field of a straight wire decreases as the distance increases (the concentric circles spread out). 1

(c) By increasing the current through the wire. 1

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q19 · 1 mark (A–R)Assertion: Magnetic field lines never intersect each other. Reason: If they did, a compass needle at the point of intersection would point in two directions at once, which is impossible.

(A). Both are true and the Reason correctly explains the Assertion.

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q14 · 1 markThe magnetic field lines inside a long current-carrying solenoid are (a) parallel straight lines (b) circles (c) radial lines from the centre (d) zero, as there is no field

(a). The field inside is uniform, shown by parallel straight lines.

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q26 · 2 marks(a) Why do two magnetic field lines never intersect? (b) How is a uniform magnetic field represented?

(a) At the point of intersection, the field would have two directions (a compass would point two ways), which is not possible. 1

(b) By parallel, equally spaced straight lines (with arrows). 1

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q32 · 3 marksState (a) the right-hand thumb rule and (b) Fleming's left-hand rule. What does each rule help us find?

(a) Hold a current-carrying straight conductor in your right hand so that the thumb points in the direction of the current; the curled fingers then give the direction of the magnetic field lines. Used to find the direction of the field. 1½

(b) Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular. If the forefinger points along the field and the middle finger along the current, the thumb points in the direction of the force (motion) on the conductor. 1½

CBSE 2023 · 31/1/1 · Q32 · 3 marks(a) State Fleming's left-hand rule. (b) An electron enters a magnetic field. When is the force on it (i) maximum (ii) minimum? OR Draw the field lines of a current-carrying solenoid and compare them with those of a bar magnet.

(a) Stretch the thumb, forefinger and middle finger of the left hand mutually perpendicular: forefinger = field, middle finger = current, thumb = force. 1

(b) (i) Maximum when the electron moves perpendicular to the field. 1 (ii) Minimum (zero) when it moves parallel (or antiparallel) to the field. 1

OR Draw closed loops from one end to the other outside and parallel lines inside. 1½ The pattern is like a bar magnet's; one end acts as N and the other as S. But the field inside a solenoid is uniform, and the poles reverse if the current is reversed. 1½

C3A proton beam moves towards the north in a uniform magnetic field directed upward. In which direction is it deflected? What would happen to an electron beam moving north in the same field?

An electron beam moving north is a current towards the south, so it is deflected west. 1

A fuse or MCB, connected in series on the live wire. 1

NCERT

All textbook questions answered

In-text questions

1Why does a compass needle get deflected when brought near a bar magnet?
The compass needle is a small magnet. The magnetic field of the bar magnet exerts forces on its poles (like poles repel, unlike poles attract), which turn the needle along the field.
2Draw magnetic field lines around a bar magnet.
Closed curves from the N pole to the S pole outside the magnet, crowded near the poles, with arrows; inside, from S to N. See the bar magnet figure in 12.1 and try the Field Line Explorer.
3List the properties of magnetic field lines.
(i) Outside a magnet they go from N to S; inside from S to N, forming closed curves. (ii) They are crowded where the field is strong. (iii) No two field lines cross each other. (iv) The tangent at any point gives the direction of the field there.
4Why don't two magnetic field lines intersect each other?
At the point of intersection there would be two directions of the field, so a compass placed there would point in two directions at once, which is impossible.
5A circular loop of wire lies in the plane of the table, and current flows clockwise through it. Use the right-hand rule to find the direction of the field inside and outside the loop.
Applying the right-hand thumb rule to each part of the loop: inside the loop the field is directed into the table (vertically downward); outside the loop it is directed out of the table (vertically upward).
6The magnetic field in a given region is uniform. Draw a diagram to represent it.
Draw parallel straight lines that are equally spaced, all with arrows in the same direction (see the "uniform field" tile in 12.1).
7The magnetic field inside a long straight current-carrying solenoid (a) is zero (b) decreases towards its end (c) increases towards its end (d) is the same at all points
(d) is the same at all points (uniform).
8Which property of a proton can change while it moves freely in a magnetic field? (a) mass (b) speed (c) velocity (d) momentum
(c) velocity and (d) momentum. The force is perpendicular to the motion, so it changes the direction but not the speed or mass.
9In Activity 12.7, how will the displacement of rod AB be affected if (i) the current is increased (ii) a stronger horseshoe magnet is used (iii) the length of the rod is increased?
In all three cases the force on the rod increases, so its displacement increases.
10A positively charged particle (alpha particle) projected towards west is deflected towards north by a magnetic field. The direction of the field is (a) towards south (b) towards east (c) downward (d) upward
(d) upward. Current = west (positive particle), force = north. By Fleming's left-hand rule the forefinger points upward.
11Name two safety measures commonly used in electric circuits and appliances.
(i) An electric fuse (or MCB) in series on the live wire. (ii) Earthing of metallic appliances.
12An electric oven of 2 kW is operated in a domestic circuit (220 V) that has a current rating of 5 A. What result do you expect? Explain.
I = P/V = 2000/220 = 9.09 A, which is more than 5 A. The circuit is overloaded, so the fuse will melt (or the MCB will trip) and the oven will switch off.
13What precaution should be taken to avoid overloading of domestic electric circuits?
Do not connect too many appliances to one socket (avoid multi-plug adaptors), use high-power appliances on the 15 A circuit, and use fuses or MCBs of the correct rating.

Exercise questions

1Which correctly describes the magnetic field near a long straight wire? (a) straight lines perpendicular to the wire (b) straight lines parallel to the wire (c) radial lines from the wire (d) concentric circles centred on the wire
(d).
2At the time of a short circuit, the current in the circuit (a) reduces substantially (b) does not change (c) increases heavily (d) varies continuously
(c) increases heavily.
3True or false: (a) The field at the centre of a long circular coil carrying current will be parallel straight lines. (b) A wire with green insulation is usually the live wire.
(a) True. (b) False: green is the earth wire; the live wire is red (brown).
4List two methods of producing magnetic fields.
(i) Using a permanent magnet (bar or horseshoe magnet). (ii) Passing current through a conductor such as a straight wire, a coil or a solenoid (electromagnet).
5When is the force experienced by a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field largest?
When the direction of the current is perpendicular to the magnetic field.
6You sit in a chamber with your back to one wall. An electron beam moving horizontally from the back wall towards the front wall is deflected by a strong magnetic field to your right. What is the direction of the field?
The current is opposite to the electron flow: from the front wall towards the back wall. The force is to the right. By Fleming's left-hand rule, the field is vertically downward.
7What is the role of a fuse used in series with an electrical appliance? Why should a fuse of defined rating not be replaced by one of a larger rating?
A fuse protects the circuit and appliance from overloading and short circuits: it melts when the current exceeds the safe value and breaks the circuit. A higher-rated fuse would not melt at the dangerous current, so the appliance and wiring could be damaged or catch fire.
8When does an electric short circuit occur?
When the live wire comes in direct contact with the neutral wire (because of damaged insulation or a fault in an appliance). The resistance becomes very small and the current very large.
9What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it necessary to earth metallic appliances?
The earth wire provides a low-resistance path for current to the earth. If the live wire touches the metal body of an appliance, the current leaks to the earth instead of through the user, keeping the body at earth potential and preventing a severe shock.
