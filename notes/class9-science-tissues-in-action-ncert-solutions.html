Pause and Ponder

5 questions
1
Coconut husk fibres are hard and brittle, but coriander leaf stalks are soft and flexible. Why?
Key ideaThe type of supporting tissue decides hardness or flexibility
  1. Step 1
    Coconut husk is made of sclerenchyma: dead cells with very thick walls of lignin
  2. Step 2
    Lignin makes the cells hard and stiff, so the husk is hard and brittle
  3. Step 3
    A coriander leaf stalk has collenchyma (and parenchyma): living cells thickened only at the corners with pectin
  4. Step 4
    Pectin is flexible, so the stalk bends easily
  5. AnswerHusk = sclerenchyma (dead, lignified); stalk = collenchyma (living, pectin at corners)
2
Why is a thick cuticle useful for a desert plant but a problem for a plant living underwater?
  1. Step 1
    Desert: water is scarce. A thick waxy cuticle reduces water loss by transpiration
  2. Step 2
    Underwater: there is no risk of drying. The plant absorbs water, minerals and dissolved gases directly through its surface
  3. Step 3
    A thick cuticle would block this absorption and gas exchange
  4. AnswerThick cuticle saves water in deserts but blocks absorption underwater, so water plants have a thin or no cuticle
3
How do the “dead” xylem cells work with the living leaf cells to keep water moving up against gravity?
  1. Step 1
    Xylem vessels and tracheids are dead, hollow tubes with strong lignified walls, joined end to end from root to leaf
  2. Step 2
    Living leaf cells lose water by transpiration through stomata
  3. Step 3
    This creates a transpiration pull that drags the water column up, since water molecules stick together
  4. Step 4
    The strong dead walls do not collapse under this pull (root pressure also helps a little)
  5. AnswerLiving cells give the pull; dead xylem gives a clear, strong pipe
4
What would happen if there were no stomata in the epidermis of stems or leaves?
  1. Step 1
    No gas exchange: CO2 could not enter, so no photosynthesis and no food
  2. Step 2
    No transpiration: no transpiration pull, so water and minerals would not rise well from the roots
  3. Step 3
    No cooling effect from evaporation, so leaves would overheat
  4. AnswerThe plant would starve, wilt and finally die
5
In the dance poses of Fig. 3.17, which joints are used and what movement does each allow?
  1. Step 1
    Shoulders and hips (ball and socket): arms raised and swept in circles, legs lifted sideways or turned out
  2. Step 2
    Elbows, knees and fingers (hinge): bending in mudras and the bent-knee half-sitting stance
  3. Step 3
    Neck (pivot): side-to-side head movements
  4. Step 4
    Wrists and ankles (gliding / hinge-like): turning the hands, standing on toes or heels
  5. Step 5
    Skull (fixed): no movement; protects the brain
  6. AnswerBall and socket, hinge, pivot, gliding and fixed joints all work together

Revise, Reflect, Refine

15 questions
1
Meristematic tissues divide repeatedly. What property of their cells allows them to do this? (i) thick walls for protection (ii) large vacuoles that store nutrients (iii) thin walls, dense cytoplasm and a large prominent nucleus (iv) functionally differentiated cells
  1. Step 1
    Dividing cells need a big active nucleus and dense cytoplasm, and thin walls that can stretch
  2. Step 2
    Thick walls, large vacuoles and differentiation are features of permanent cells that no longer divide
  3. Answer(iii) Thin walls, dense cytoplasm and a large prominent nucleus
2
If a plant is unable to transport food from leaves to roots, which tissue is malfunctioning? (i) Xylem (ii) Phloem (iii) Epidermis (iv) Sclerenchyma
  1. Step 1
    Xylem carries water and minerals upward; phloem carries food from leaves to other parts
  2. Answer(ii) Phloem
3
Why are the epithelial tissues that line an animal’s internal organs usually only one or a few cells thick? (i) to store food (ii) to provide maximum strength (iii) to allow quick exchange of materials (iv) to reduce friction
  1. Step 1
    A thin layer means a short distance for diffusion (for example, oxygen into blood in the lungs)
  2. Answer(iii) To allow quick exchange of materials across them
4
Compare a straight-leg jump (knees and ankles stiff) with a normal jump (knees and ankles bent). How did your ankle, knee and hip positions differ?
  1. Step 1
    Straight-leg jump: ankles, knees and hips stay nearly straight and locked; only the toes and calves push, so the jump is low
  2. Step 2
    Landing is jarring because the joints cannot absorb the shock
  3. Step 3
    Normal jump: first ankles, knees and hips all bend (a crouch), then they straighten together with force
  4. Step 4
    Muscles get a longer push, so the jump is higher; on landing the joints bend again to cushion the impact
  5. AnswerIn a normal jump the ankle, knee and hip bend and straighten together (hinge-type action); in a straight-leg jump they stay locked
5
Which type of joint is involved when you bend your knees and ankles? (i) Ball and socket (ii) Hinge (iii) Pivot
  1. Step 1
    Knees and ankles bend back and forth in one plane, like a door hinge
  2. Answer(ii) Hinge
6

R: It has multiple layers of tall cells that slow diffusion. B. A: Cardiac muscle contracts continuously without fatigue. R: Its cells have many mitochondria and an abundant blood supply. C. A: Tendons connect bone to bone and allow joint movement. R: Tendons are tough connective tissue that transmits force from muscle to bone. D. A: In a hinge joint movement is mainly in one plane. R: The bone ends are shaped to slide in all directions.
  1. A
  2. Step 1
    A is true; but lung epithelium is a single layer of thin, flat cells, so R is false
  3. Answer(iii)
  4. B
  5. Step 1
    Both true; many mitochondria and rich blood supply give constant energy and oxygen, which explains no fatigue
  6. Answer(i)
  7. C
  8. Step 1
    A is false (tendons join muscle to bone; ligaments join bone to bone); R is true
  9. Answer(iv)
  10. D
  11. Step 1
    A is true; bone ends of a hinge joint allow movement in one plane only, so R is false
  12. Answer(iii)
7
Plot a graph of the age of a teak tree (x-axis) against the diameter (DBH) and the number of annual rings (y-axis) using Table 3.7 (age 5, 10, 20, 25, 30, 40 years; DBH 4, 8, 24, 28, 32, 40 cm; rings 5, 10, 20, 25, 30, 40). (i) Interpret the diameter over time. (ii) What is the relation between diameter and annual rings? (iii) Which tissue is responsible for girth and where is it?
  1. Step 1
    Plot both lines on the same axes: rings form a straight line (rings = age); DBH rises but not evenly
  2. Step 2
    (i) Growth rate: 5 to 10 years about 0.8 cm/year; 10 to 20 years fastest, 1.6 cm/year; after 20 years about 0.8 cm/year
  3. Step 3
    (ii) Number of rings always equals the age (one ring a year). Diameter rises with rings but not in exact proportion, because some rings are wide (good years) and some narrow
  4. Step 4
    (iii) The lateral meristem (vascular cambium), a ring in the stem between xylem and phloem, adds girth
  5. Answer(i) diameter increases every year, fastest at 10–20 years (ii) rings = age, diameter rises with rings unevenly (iii) vascular cambium
8
A tree was severely debarked by an elephant (Fig. 3.22). (i) Which functions of the tree are hampered? (ii) Which tissue would be affected by further damage even after debarking? (iii) Which function is hampered if tissues beneath the bark are severely damaged? (iv) What assumptions are you making? How would the answer change if the assumptions change?
  1. Step 1
    (i) Protection: bark (cork) prevents water loss and keeps out germs, insects and injury. Phloem just inside is often torn too, so food transport to the roots suffers
  2. Step 2
    (ii) Phloem first, then the vascular cambium and outer xylem
  3. Step 3
    (iii) Food cannot reach the roots (phloem); growth in girth stops (cambium); deeper damage cuts upward water flow (xylem)
  4. Step 4
    (iv) Assumption: bark was removed from only part of the trunk, not a full ring. If a complete ring is removed (girdling), roots starve and the tree dies; a small patch can heal with new cork
  5. AnswerProtection and food transport are hampered; phloem and cambium are at risk
9
A young mango sapling’s stem bends flexibly in monsoon winds and does not break. Which tissue gives this flexibility? What would happen if it were replaced by sclerenchyma?
  1. Step 1
    Collenchyma: living cells thickened only at the corners with pectin, giving strength with flexibility
  2. Step 2
    Sclerenchyma has dead cells with thick lignified walls: rigid and stiff
  3. Step 3
    A rigid young stem could not bend in strong wind and would snap; it also could not stretch as the stem grows
  4. AnswerCollenchyma; with sclerenchyma the stem would be stiff and break
10
Sohan grew sugarcane from two types of cuttings, A and B (Fig. 3.23). Only type B sprouted. (i) Why? (ii) What difference was present in B? (iii) What observation was made? (iv) Which parameters should be kept the same?
  1. Step 1
    (i) Type B had a node with a bud and meristematic (intercalary) tissue that can divide to form shoots and roots; type A was only internode (permanent tissue)
  2. Step 2
    (ii) Type B contained a node (bud); type A did not
  3. Step 3
    (iii) Whether shoots and roots appeared, how many cuttings sprouted, shoot length after some weeks
  4. Step 4
    (iv) Same variety and age, same length and thickness, same soil, water, light, temperature, planting depth and time
  5. AnswerB had a node with meristem; a fair test keeps all other conditions the same
11
Rohan says, “A tissue is a group of similar cells performing similar functions.” Rajiv says, “This is true for simple tissues but a little different for complex tissues.” Explain.
  1. Step 1
    Simple tissues (like parenchyma) have one type of cell, so Rohan’s definition fits
  2. Step 2
    Complex tissues like xylem have different cells (tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma, fibres), some living and some dead, all working together for one function
  3. Step 3
    Better definition: a tissue is a group of cells, similar or different, working together for a specific function
  4. AnswerRajiv is right
12
Coconut husk fibres are used for tough mats. Which tissue gives this strength? Why couldn’t living parenchyma do the same?
  1. Step 1
    Sclerenchyma: long, dead, tightly packed cells with very thick lignified walls, hard and slow to rot
  2. Step 2
    Parenchyma: thin walls, loosely packed cells with air spaces and living contents; soft, would squash and tear, and would decay once dried
  3. AnswerSclerenchyma; parenchyma is too soft and perishable
13
Vibha says, “Meristematic cells are located only at the root and shoot apices.” Is this right? What question can Neha ask her?
  1. Step 1
    Only apical meristems are at the tips
  2. Step 2
    Lateral meristem forms a ring in the stem (girth); intercalary meristem is at the base of internodes / nodes (regrowth)
  3. Step 3
    Neha could ask: “Then how does a tree trunk get thicker every year, and how does grass grow back after mowing?”
  4. AnswerThe statement is incorrect
14
A plant cell and an animal cell are the same size. (i) Which has a larger vacuole? Give reasons. (ii) What assumptions are you making?
  1. Step 1
    (i) A mature plant cell has a large central vacuole that stores water and sap and keeps the cell turgid; animal cells have only small, temporary vacuoles
  2. Step 2
    (ii) Assumption: both are mature, ordinary cells (a meristematic plant cell has almost no vacuole)
  3. Answer(i) The plant cell (ii) both are mature cells
15
A textbook says, “Each plant tissue performs only one specific function.” What questions would you ask to examine this, and which examples would you use?
  1. Step 1
    Questions: Does parenchyma only store food? Does epidermis only protect? Does xylem only conduct water?
  2. Step 2
    Parenchyma stores food, photosynthesises (chlorenchyma) and gives buoyancy (aerenchyma)
  3. Step 3
    Epidermis protects, but also has stomata for gas exchange and root hairs for absorption
  4. Step 4
    Xylem conducts water and also gives mechanical strength
  5. AnswerThe statement is not correct: a tissue has a main function but may do several
