📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Distance formula: PQ = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]. From the origin: OP = √(x² + y²).
  • Section formula (internal, m : n): P = ((mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n)).
  • Mid-point: ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2). Ratio questions: take the ratio as k : 1.
  • On the x-axis y = 0; on the y-axis x = 0. Distance of (x, y) from the x-axis is |y|, from the y-axis |x|.
  • Removed from syllabus: area of a triangle formula (and collinearity by area).
  • Most-asked in CBSE: distance / mid-point MCQs, "ratio in which the x-axis (or y-axis) divides" (3 marks, asked in 2023, 2025, 2026), centre-radius problems giving two values (2 marks), and proving a figure type. Unit Coordinate Geometry carries 6 marks.
7.1

The coordinate plane: a quick recap

Two perpendicular number lines, the x-axis and y-axis, meet at the origin O(0, 0). A point P(x, y) is x units along (the abscissa) and y units up (the ordinate).

🤔 Real life: coordinates everywhere

A cinema seat "Row F, Seat 12", a chessboard square "e4", a city map grid "C-7" and GPS latitude–longitude all use two numbers to fix a position. Coordinate geometry turns geometry questions into simple arithmetic.

🧪 Try it nowClassroom coordinates

  1. Make the front-left corner of your classroom the origin. Rows going back are the y-axis, desk columns going right are the x-axis.
  2. Write your own coordinates, say (3, 4), and your best friend's, say (6, 8).
  3. Measure the straight distance between you in "desk units". The distance formula gives √(9 + 16) = 5. Check with a tape!
7.2

Distance formula

Join P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂). Draw a horizontal line through P and a vertical line through Q, meeting at T. Then ΔPTQ is right-angled at T with PT = x₂ − x₁ and QT = y₂ − y₁. By Pythagoras:

PQ = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
🎯

From the origin

OP = √(x² + y²). Distance of (3, 4) from O is 5.

↔️

Same y

(3, −5) and (x, −5): distance = |x − 3|. CBSE 2024: = 15 ⇒ x = 18 or −12.

🔁

Order doesn't matter

(x₂ − x₁)² = (x₁ − x₂)², so subtract either way.

NCERT Ex 7.1 Q1: find the distances

(i) (2, 3), (4, 1): √(4 + 4) = 2√2. (ii) (−5, 7), (−1, 3): √(16 + 16) = 4√2. (iii) (a, b), (−a, −b): √(4a² + 4b²) = 2√(a² + b²).

CBSE 2023: distance between (0, 2√5) and (−2√5, 0)

√[(2√5)² + (2√5)²] = √(20 + 20) = √40 = 2√10.

⚠️ Sign slips

  • x₂ − x₁ with negatives: 4 − (−2) = 6, not 2.
  • Square the whole difference: (−4)² = 16, not −16.
  • Do not take the square root of each term separately: √(9 + 16) = 5, not 3 + 4.

🧪 Try itMap distance

  1. On a city map with a 1 km grid, mark your home at (1, 2) and school at (4, 6).
  2. "As the crow flies" distance = √(9 + 16) = 5 km. Walking along the grid roads it is 3 + 4 = 7 km.
  3. The straight line is always shorter: that is why helicopters and drones save time.

7.2.1

Using distance: collinear points, shapes and equidistant points

To show…Check with distances
A, B, C are collinearthe largest distance = sum of the other two (AB + BC = AC)
Isosceles / equilateral triangletwo sides equal / all three equal
Right triangle(largest side)² = sum of squares of the other two
Square4 equal sides and equal diagonals
Rhombus4 equal sides, diagonals unequal
Rectangleopposite sides equal and diagonals equal
Parallelogramopposite sides equal (or diagonals bisect each other)

NCERT Ex 7.1 Q5: Champa or Chameli?

Students sit at A(3, 4), B(6, 7), C(9, 4), D(6, 1). AB = BC = CD = DA = √18 = 3√2 and diagonals AC = BD = 6. Four equal sides and equal diagonals: ABCD is a square, so Champa is right.

Equidistant point on an axis (NCERT Ex 7.1 Q7)

Point on the x-axis equidistant from (2, −5) and (−2, 9): take (x, 0). (x − 2)² + 25 = (x + 2)² + 81 ⇒ −8x = 56 ⇒ (−7, 0).

Centre and radius give two answers (CBSE 2025)

Centre (2a, a − 7), circle through (11, −9), diameter 10√2 (radius 5√2): (11 − 2a)² + (−2 − a)² = 50 ⇒ 5a² − 40a + 75 = 0 ⇒ a² − 8a + 15 = 0 ⇒ a = 3 or 5.

Relation between x and y (NCERT Ex 7.1 Q10)

(x, y) equidistant from (3, 6) and (−3, 4): (x − 3)² + (y − 6)² = (x + 3)² + (y − 4)² ⇒ −12x − 4y + 20 = 0 ⇒ 3x + y − 5 = 0.

⚡ Shortcuts

  • Compare squares of distances (AB², BC²) to avoid roots until the end.
  • For "equidistant", square both sides at once: the x² and y² terms cancel, leaving a linear equation.
  • Collinear check: if √5 + √20 = √45, write it as √5 + 2√5 = 3√5 ✓.

🧪 Try itFind the treasure

  2. Take (x, 0): (x − 1)² + 9 = (x − 5)² + 1 ⇒ 8x = 16 ⇒ x = 2.
  3. Dig at (2, 0)! Check: distance to each tree is √10.
7.3

Section formula and mid-point formula

If P lies on AB and divides it internally in the ratio m : n (AP : PB = m : n), then P is found by "cross-multiplying" the ratio with the end points.

P = ( (m x₂ + n x₁)/(m + n) , (m y₂ + n y₁)/(m + n) )
Mid-point (m = n = 1): ( (x₁ + x₂)/2 , (y₁ + y₂)/2 )

⚡ Memory trick: "m goes with the far end"

m is next to A in the ratio, but it multiplies the coordinates of B (the far end). Think of a see-saw: the bigger weight pulls P towards its side.

NCERT Ex 7.2 Q1: divide (−1, 7) and (4, −3) in 2 : 3

x = (2 × 4 + 3 × (−1))/5 = 5/5 = 1, y = (2 × (−3) + 3 × 7)/5 = 15/5 = 3. P = (1, 3).

Trisection points (NCERT Ex 7.2 Q2): (4, −1) and (−2, −3)

Ratio 1 : 2: ((−2 + 8)/3, (−3 − 2)/3) = (2, −5/3). Ratio 2 : 1: ((−4 + 4)/3, (−6 − 1)/3) = (0, −7/3).

Other end of a diameter (CBSE 2024)

Centre (2, 0) is the mid-point of the diameter; one end (6, 0). Other end (x, y): (6 + x)/2 = 2, (0 + y)/2 = 0 ⇒ (−2, 0).

🤔 Bonus: centroid

The medians of a triangle meet at the centroid G, which divides each median in 2 : 1. Using the section formula on the median from A to the mid-point of BC gives G = ((x₁ + x₂ + x₃)/3, (y₁ + y₂ + y₃)/3). Handy for checking answers.

🧪 Try itRangoli dots

  1. Draw a line between dots at (0, 0) and (6, 9) on graph paper.
  2. Mark the point dividing it in 1 : 2 using the formula: (6/3, 9/3) = (2, 3). Mark 2 : 1: (4, 6).
  3. Measure: the three pieces are equal. You have trisected the line without a ruler!

7.3.1

Finding the ratio; axes; trisection; parallelograms

When the ratio is unknown, take it as k : 1. Then P = ((kx₂ + x₁)/(k + 1), (ky₂ + y₁)/(k + 1)). Use the coordinate you know to find k.

Ratio by a given point (NCERT Ex 7.2 Q4)

(−1, 6) divides (−3, 10) and (6, −8) in k : 1: (6k − 3)/(k + 1) = −1 ⇒ 7k = 2 ⇒ k = 2/7. Ratio 2 : 7. (Check y: (−16 + 70)/9 = 6 ✓)

Division by the x-axis (CBSE 2026, 3 marks)

The x-axis divides the join of (−6, 5) and (−4, −1). On the x-axis y = 0: (−k + 5)/(k + 1) = 0 ⇒ k = 5. Ratio 5 : 1. 1½

x = (5(−4) + (−6))/6 = −26/6 = −13/3. Point (−13/3, 0). 1½

Fourth vertex of a parallelogram (NCERT Ex 7.2 Q6)

(1, 2), (4, y), (x, 6), (3, 5) taken in order. Diagonals bisect each other, so the mid-points of AC and BD coincide: ((1 + x)/2, 4) = (7/2, (y + 5)/2) ⇒ x = 6, y = 3.

Divide into four equal parts (NCERT Ex 7.2 Q9)

A(−2, 2), B(2, 8). Mid-point Q = (0, 5); mid-point of AQ = (−1, 7/2); mid-point of QB = (1, 13/2). Points: (−1, 7/2), (0, 5), (1, 13/2).

CBSE 2023: equilateral triangle

Two vertices (−5, 3) and (5, 3), origin inside. The third vertex lies on the perpendicular bisector x = 0, at height √3/2 × side = 5√3 from y = 3. Since the origin is inside, it is below: (0, 3 − 5√3).

⚠️ Watch out

  • A negative k means the point divides the segment externally (not in our syllabus): recheck your work.
  • For "divides by the y-axis" use x = 0; for "by the x-axis" use y = 0.
  • Write the final answer as a ratio and the point, if both are asked.

🧪 Try itShadow of a stick on the axis

  1. Draw A(2, 6) and B(8, −3) on graph paper and join them.
  2. Where does AB cross the x-axis? (6 − 3k)/(k + 1) = 0 ⇒ k = 2: ratio 2 : 1, point ((16 + 2)/3, 0) = (6, 0).
  3. Check on your drawing: the line crosses the x-axis at 6 ✓.

Mind map, formulas and common mistakes

COORDINATE GEOMETRY

📍 Basics

  • Quadrants: (+,+) (−,+) (−,−) (+,−)
  • x-axis: y = 0; y-axis: x = 0
  • Distance from x-axis = |y|
  • Distance from y-axis = |x|

📏 Distance

  • √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]
  • From O: √(x² + y²)
  • Collinear: AB + BC = AC
  • Shapes: sides + diagonals
  • Equidistant ⇒ squares equal

➗ Section

  • ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n))
  • Mid-point: average
  • Unknown ratio: k : 1
  • Parallelogram: diagonals share mid-point
Question says…Do this
"point on the x-axis"take (x, 0)
"point on the y-axis"take (0, y)
"in what ratio"take k : 1, use the known coordinate
"centre is the mid-point of the diameter"mid-point formula backwards
"vertices of a parallelogram in order"mid-point of AC = mid-point of BD
"show that it is a square / rhombus"4 sides and 2 diagonals by distance formula

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. (−3)² written as −9.
  2. Using mx₁ + nx₂ instead of mx₂ + nx₁ in the section formula.
  3. For the x-axis taking x = 0 (it is y = 0 on the x-axis).
  4. Calling a figure a square after checking only the sides (a rhombus also has 4 equal sides).
  5. Forgetting the ± when solving (x − 3)² = 16.
  6. Rounding √ values early; keep surds like 3√2 till the end.
  7. Leaving the answer as k = 2/7 instead of the ratio 2 : 7.
  8. Using the area-of-triangle formula, which is no longer in the syllabus, to test collinearity.

CBSE

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Standard, 041) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 38 questions. Unit Coordinate Geometry carries 6 marks: typically 2 MCQs, one 2-mark and one 3-mark question.

✍️ How the CBSE marking scheme splits marks

  • Ratio by an axis (3 marks): 1 for the section formula with k : 1, 1 for k, 1 for the point.
  • Centre–radius (2 marks): 1 for the distance equation, 1 for both values.
  • Shape proof (3 marks): 1½ for the four sides, 1 for the diagonals, ½ for the conclusion.

🏆 Answer-writing tips for full marks

  1. Write "Let P(x, y) divide AB in k : 1" or "Let the point on the x-axis be (x, 0)".
  2. Write the formula in symbols, then substitute.
  3. Keep surds simplified: √72 = 6√2.
  4. State the conclusion in words: "Hence ABCD is a square."

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q23 · 2 marksThe centre of a circle is (x − 7, 2x) and it passes through (−9, 11). If its radius is 5√2, find the possible values of x.

(−9 − x + 7)² + (11 − 2x)² = 50 ⇒ (x + 2)² + (11 − 2x)² = 50 1

5x² − 40x + 75 = 0 ⇒ x² − 8x + 15 = 0 ⇒ x = 3 or 5. 1

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q27 · 3 marksFind the ratio in which the x-axis divides the line segment joining (−6, 5) and (−4, −1). Also find the point of intersection.

Let the ratio be k : 1. y-coordinate = (−k + 5)/(k + 1) = 0 1 ⇒ k = 5: ratio 5 : 1. 1

x = (5 × (−4) + 1 × (−6))/6 = −26/6 = −13/3. Point (−13/3, 0). 1

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q3 · 1 markThe mid-point of the line segment joining P(−4, 5) and Q(4, 6) lies on (a) the x-axis (b) the y-axis (c) the origin (d) neither axis

(b) the y-axis. Mid-point (0, 11/2).

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q28 · 3 marksFind the ratio in which the y-axis divides the line segment joining (5, −6) and (−1, −4). Also find the point of intersection.

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q6 · 1 markThe length of the median AD of ΔABC with vertices A(5, −6), B(6, 4) and C(0, 0) is (a) √68 (b) 2√15 (c) √50 (d) √65

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q26 (A) · 3 marksFind the ratio in which the point (8/5, y) divides the line segment joining (1, 2) and (2, 3). Also find the value of y.

y = (3 × 3 + 2 × 2)/5 = 13/5. 1

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q26 (B, OR) · 3 marksABCD is a rectangle with A(−1, −1), B(−1, 6), C(3, 6) and D(3, −1). P, Q, R, S are the mid-points of AB, BC, CD and DA. Show that the diagonals of PQRS bisect each other.

P(−1, 5/2), Q(1, 6), R(3, 5/2), S(1, −1). 1½

Mid-point of PR = (1, 5/2); mid-point of QS = (1, 5/2). 1

Same point ⇒ the diagonals bisect each other (PQRS is a parallelogram; in fact PQ = QR = RS = SP = √(65)/2, a rhombus). ½

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q4 · 1 markThe ratio in which the x-axis divides the line segment joining A(3, 6) and B(−12, −3) is (a) 2 : 1 (b) 1 : 2 (c) −2 : 1 (d) 1 : −2

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q13 · 1 markThe distance between the points (0, 2√5) and (−2√5, 0) is (a) 2√10 (b) 4√10 (c) 2√20 (d) 0

(b). Put x = 0: y = 2, so A is true. R is true too, but it does not explain A.

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q28 · 3 marks(−5, 3) and (5, 3) are two vertices of an equilateral triangle. Find the coordinates of the third vertex, given that the origin lies inside the triangle.

Let the third vertex be (x, y). Equal distances from (−5, 3) and (5, 3) ⇒ x = 0. 1

Side = 10: (0 − 5)² + (y − 3)² = 100 ⇒ (y − 3)² = 75 ⇒ y = 3 ± 5√3. 1

The origin is inside, so the vertex is below the x-axis: (0, 3 − 5√3). 1

AD = √(0² + 9²) = 9 units. 1

B4Show that (1, 7), (4, 2), (−1, −1) and (−4, 4) are the vertices of a square.

AB = √(9 + 25), BC = √(25 + 9), CD = √(9 + 25), DA = √(25 + 9): all = √34. 1

C1Prove that A(2, −2), B(−2, 1) and C(5, 2) are the vertices of a right-angled triangle.

AB² = 16 + 9 = 25, AC² = 9 + 16 = 25, BC² = 49 + 1 = 50. 1½

AB² + AC² = 50 = BC² ⇒ by the converse of Pythagoras, ∠A = 90°. 1

It is a right isosceles triangle, right-angled at A. ½

C2A(−2, 1), B(a, 0), C(4, b) and D(1, 2) are the vertices of parallelogram ABCD. Find a and b, and the lengths of its sides.

Diagonals bisect each other: (−2 + 4)/2 = (a + 1)/2 ⇒ a = 1; (1 + b)/2 = (0 + 2)/2 ⇒ b = 1. 1½

AB = √(9 + 1) = √10, BC = √(9 + 1) = √10, so all sides are √10. 1½

(Since all sides are equal and the diagonals 6 and 2 are not, it is actually a rhombus.)

C3The point A(3, y) is equidistant from P(6, 5) and Q(0, −3). Find y and the distance PA.

9 + (y − 5)² = 9 + (y + 3)² 1

C4P and Q are the points of trisection of AB, where A(2, 1) and B(5, −8), with P nearer to A. If P lies on the line 2x − y + k = 0, find k.

2(3) − (−2) + k = 0 ⇒ k = −8. 1

D1Show that A(1, −2), B(3, 6), C(5, 10) and D(3, 2), taken in order, form a parallelogram. Is it a rectangle? Also find the point which divides AC in the ratio 1 : 3.

D2The x-axis divides the segment joining A(2, −3) and B(5, 6) at P. Find the ratio, the point P, and verify the ratio using the distance formula. Does the y-axis cut the segment AB?

k : 1 with y = 0: (6k − 3)/(k + 1) = 0 ⇒ k = 1/2 ⇒ ratio 1 : 2. 1½

P = ((1 × 5 + 2 × 2)/3, 0) = (3, 0). 1

Exercise 7.1

1Find the distance between the following pairs of points: (i) (2, 3), (4, 1) (ii) (−5, 7), (−1, 3) (iii) (a, b), (−a, −b)
(i) √(4 + 4) = 2√2 (ii) √(16 + 16) = 4√2 (iii) √(4a² + 4b²) = 2√(a² + b²)
2Find the distance between the points (0, 0) and (36, 15). Can you now find the distance between the two towns A and B discussed in Section 7.2?
√(1296 + 225) = √1521 = 39. Yes: with A at (0, 0) and B at (36, 15), the towns are 39 km apart.
3Determine if the points (1, 5), (2, 3) and (−2, −11) are collinear.
AB = √(1 + 4) = √5 ≈ 2.24, BC = √(16 + 196) = √212 ≈ 14.56, AC = √(9 + 256) = √265 ≈ 16.28. AB + BC ≈ 16.80 ≠ AC, and no other sum works either. Not collinear.
4Check whether (5, −2), (6, 4) and (7, −2) are the vertices of an isosceles triangle.
AB = √(1 + 36) = √37, BC = √(1 + 36) = √37, AC = √(4 + 0) = 2. AB = BC, so yes, isosceles.
5In a classroom, 4 friends are seated at A(3, 4), B(6, 7), C(9, 4) and D(6, 1). Champa says ABCD is a square; Chameli disagrees. Who is correct?
AB = BC = CD = DA = √(9 + 9) = 3√2, and diagonals AC = 6, BD = 6. All sides equal and diagonals equal ⇒ ABCD is a square. Champa is correct. (Try it in the Shape Detective lab.)
6Name the type of quadrilateral formed, if any, by the points, and give reasons: (i) (−1, −2), (1, 0), (−1, 2), (−3, 0) (ii) (−3, 5), (3, 1), (0, 3), (−1, −4) (iii) (4, 5), (7, 6), (4, 3), (1, 2)

(i) All sides = √8 = 2√2, diagonals AC = 4, BD = 4 ⇒ square.

(ii) AB = √52 = 2√13, BC = √13, AC = √13. Since AC + CB = AB, the points A, C, B are collinear ⇒ no quadrilateral.

(iii) AB = √10, BC = √18, CD = √10, DA = √18, diagonals AC = 2, BD = √52. Opposite sides equal, diagonals unequal ⇒ parallelogram.

7Find the point on the x-axis which is equidistant from (2, −5) and (−2, 9).
(x − 2)² + 25 = (x + 2)² + 81 ⇒ −4x + 29 = 4x + 85 ⇒ x = −7. Point (−7, 0).
8Find the values of y for which the distance between P(2, −3) and Q(10, y) is 10 units.
64 + (y + 3)² = 100 ⇒ y + 3 = ±6 ⇒ y = 3 or y = −9.
9If Q(0, 1) is equidistant from P(5, −3) and R(x, 6), find the values of x. Also find the distances QR and PR.
QP² = 25 + 16 = 41 = x² + 25 ⇒ x = ±4. QR = √41. For R(4, 6): PR = √(1 + 81) = √82; for R(−4, 6): PR = √(81 + 81) = 9√2.
10Find a relation between x and y such that the point (x, y) is equidistant from the points (3, 6) and (−3, 4).
(x − 3)² + (y − 6)² = (x + 3)² + (y − 4)² ⇒ −6x − 12y + 45 = 6x − 8y + 25 ⇒ 12x + 4y = 20 ⇒ 3x + y − 5 = 0.

Exercise 7.2

1Find the coordinates of the point which divides the join of (−1, 7) and (4, −3) in the ratio 2 : 3.
((8 − 3)/5, (−6 + 21)/5) = (1, 3).
2Find the coordinates of the points of trisection of the line segment joining (4, −1) and (−2, −3).
Ratio 1 : 2: (2, −5/3). Ratio 2 : 1: (0, −7/3).
3To conduct Sports Day, lines are drawn with chalk powder 1 m apart in a rectangular school ground, with 100 flower pots 1 m apart along AD. Niharika runs 1/4 of AD on the 2nd line and posts a green flag. Preet runs 1/5 of AD on the 8th line and posts a red flag. What is the distance between the flags? If Rashmi posts a blue flag exactly halfway between them, where is it?
Green flag: (2, ¼ × 100) = (2, 25). Red flag: (8, ⅕ × 100) = (8, 20). Distance = √(36 + 25) = √61 m. Blue flag = mid-point = (5, 22.5): on the 5th line, 22.5 m along.
4Find the ratio in which the segment joining (−3, 10) and (6, −8) is divided by (−1, 6).
(6k − 3)/(k + 1) = −1 ⇒ 7k = 2 ⇒ 2 : 7.
5Find the ratio in which the segment joining A(1, −5) and B(−4, 5) is divided by the x-axis. Also find the point of division.
(5k − 5)/(k + 1) = 0 ⇒ k = 1: ratio 1 : 1. Point = ((−4 + 1)/2, 0) = (−3/2, 0).
6If (1, 2), (4, y), (x, 6) and (3, 5) are the vertices of a parallelogram taken in order, find x and y.
Mid-points of the diagonals coincide: (1 + x)/2 = 7/2 and 4 = (y + 5)/2 ⇒ x = 6, y = 3.
7Find the coordinates of A, where AB is a diameter of a circle whose centre is (2, −3) and B is (1, 4).
(x + 1)/2 = 2 and (y + 4)/2 = −3 ⇒ A(3, −10).
8If A and B are (−2, −2) and (2, −4), find P such that AP = (3/7) AB and P lies on AB.
AP : PB = 3 : 4. P = ((6 − 8)/7, (−12 − 8)/7) = (−2/7, −20/7).
9Find the coordinates of the points which divide the segment joining A(−2, 2) and B(2, 8) into four equal parts.
Mid-point of AB = (0, 5); mid-points of the two halves = (−1, 7/2) and (1, 13/2). Points: (−1, 7/2), (0, 5), (1, 13/2).
10Find the area of a rhombus whose vertices, taken in order, are (3, 0), (4, 5), (−1, 4) and (−2, −1). [Area = ½ × product of diagonals]
AC = √(16 + 16) = 4√2, BD = √(36 + 36) = 6√2. Area = ½ × 4√2 × 6√2 = 24 square units.
