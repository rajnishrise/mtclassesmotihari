📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Ecosystem = all living things (biotic) + physical surroundings (abiotic) of an area, interacting. Natural: forest, pond. Artificial: garden, crop field, aquarium.
  • Producers (green plants) make food by photosynthesis; consumers eat others; decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead matter and return nutrients to the soil.
  • Food chain = a series of organisms, each eating the one before. Each step is a trophic level. Food chains usually have only 3 or 4 steps.
  • Energy flow is unidirectional. Plants capture about 1% of the sunlight falling on leaves. Only about 10% of the energy passes to the next level (10 per cent law).
  • Biological magnification: non-biodegradable chemicals (like DDT) build up at each trophic level, highest at the top.
  • Ozone (O₃) in the upper atmosphere shields us from UV rays. CFCs deplete it. UNEP agreement (1987): freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.
  • Biodegradable wastes are broken down by microbes; non-biodegradable ones (plastic, glass, DDT) are not.
  • CBSE weightage: unit Natural Resources (this chapter) carries 5 marks: usually 1 to 2 MCQs or an A–R, plus one 2-mark or 3-mark question.
13.1

Ecosystem and its components

All the organisms in an area, such as plants, animals and microorganisms, together with the non-living things around them, form an ecosystem. The living members interact with each other, and their growth, reproduction and other activities are affected by the non-living parts.

GroupWhat they doExamples
Producers (autotrophs)make sugar and starch from inorganic substances using sunlight and chlorophyllgreen plants, some bacteria, algae
Herbivoreseat producersdeer, goat, grasshopper, cow
Carnivoreseat other animalslion, frog, snake, eagle
Omnivoreseat both plants and animalshuman, crow, bear
Parasiteslive on or in a host and feed on itcuscuta, lice, tapeworm
Decomposersbreak down dead remains and wastes into simple substances that go back to the soilbacteria, fungi

🤔 Why are decomposers called "nature's cleaners"?

Without bacteria and fungi, dead plants and animals would pile up, and nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus would stay locked inside them. Decomposers return these nutrients to the soil so plants can use them again. They recycle matter. (Energy, however, cannot be recycled: it flows one way.)

🧪 Try itMake a bottle ecosystem

  1. Put some soil, a few pebbles, a small plant and a little water in a large clear jar. Add a snail or a few earthworms.
  2. Close the lid and keep it in indirect sunlight for two weeks.
  3. List the producers, consumers, decomposers and abiotic parts in your jar. This is exactly what CBSE 2023 asked: a "self-sustaining jar".
13.1.1

Food chains, food webs and the 10% law

A food chain shows who eats whom. Each step of the chain is a trophic level. Producers are always the first trophic level (T1); herbivores (primary consumers) are T2; small carnivores (secondary consumers) are T3; large carnivores are T4.

🌾

Grassland

Grass → deer → lion

🐟

Pond

Phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → big fish

🌲

Forest

Plants → rabbit → snake → hawk

Flow of energy

  1. Green plants capture about 1% of the sunlight that falls on their leaves and convert it into food energy. (So about 99% is not converted: CBSE 2025 MCQ.)
  2. When a herbivore eats a plant, a large part of the food is used for its life processes or lost as heat. Only about 10% of the food eaten becomes its body mass and is available to the next level.
  3. The same happens at every level. This is the 10 per cent law.
  4. Energy does not come back from carnivores to herbivores or to the Sun: energy flow is unidirectional.

CBSE 2023 type: plants → rats → snakes → hawks

If rats (T2) get 20 000 J, then snakes (T3) get 10% = 2000 J, and hawks (T4) get 200 J.

Food web

In nature, an animal usually eats more than one kind of food and is eaten by more than one predator. So food chains join up into a branching network called a food web.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Arrows point from the food to the eater (grass → deer), in the direction energy flows.
  • A food chain always starts with a producer. "Tiger, grass, snake, frog" has no herbivore at T2 (CBSE 2024).
  • An omnivore like a human can sit at T2, T3 or T4 in different chains.

🧪 Try itYour plate's food chain

  1. Write down everything you ate yesterday: rice, dal, milk, egg, fish…
  2. For each item, write the chain that ends with you: grass → cow → milk → you (you are T3); rice plant → you (T2).
  3. Which meals put you closest to the Sun? Those are the most energy-efficient.

13.1.2

Biological magnification

Farmers spray pesticides and other chemicals to protect crops. Some of these chemicals are washed into the soil and water, absorbed by plants with water and minerals, and enter the food chain. Since they are not degradable, they build up at each trophic level. This increase in concentration at each higher level is called biological magnification.

🤔 Real life: pesticides in our food

Studies in India have found pesticide residues in fruits, vegetables, packaged water and even mother's milk. Washing vegetables removes only surface residue. The real solution is limited, careful use of pesticides and organic or bio-pesticides (like neem extract).

CBSE 2023 and 2025: why is excessive use of pesticides harmful?

(1) The chemicals wash into soil and water bodies and harm both biotic and abiotic components. 1

(2) They are non-biodegradable, so they enter the food chain and get concentrated at each trophic level (biological magnification), reaching the highest level in humans. 1

⚠️ Watch out

Energy decreases up the chain, but the concentration of non-biodegradable chemicals increases up the chain. Do not mix the two.

🧪 Try itMagnification with beads

  1. Give 20 classmates ("plankton") 5 red beads each in 100 white beads. Five "small fish" each collect all the beads of 4 plankton.
  2. One "big fish" collects the beads of all 5 small fish, but drops the white beads (digested food).
  3. Count the red beads: plankton 5, small fish 20, big fish 100. The poison is magnified at each step!

13.2.1

Ozone layer and how it is getting depleted

Ozone (O₃) is a molecule of three oxygen atoms. At ground level it is a deadly poison. But high up in the atmosphere (the stratosphere) it forms a layer that shields the Earth from the Sun's harmful ultraviolet (UV) radiation.

O₂ —UV→ O + O   ·   O + O₂ → O₃ (ozone)
QuestionAnswer
Why is ozone important?It absorbs harmful UV rays. UV causes skin cancer, cataracts, weak immunity, and damages plants and plankton.
What is depleting it?Synthetic chemicals such as chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers (and earlier in aerosol sprays).
When was the drop noticed?The amount of ozone began to drop sharply in the 1980s (the "ozone hole" over Antarctica).
What was done?In 1987, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) got countries to agree to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels. CFC-free refrigerators are now required worldwide.

CBSE 2024 OR (3 marks): gas X

"Gas X is poisonous at ground level but performs an essential function high up." X = ozone; function: shields the Earth from UV radiation; chemicals responsible for its decrease: CFCs; step taken: UNEP agreement (1987) to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.

CBSE 2025 Assertion–Reason

A: The amount of ozone in the atmosphere began to drop sharply in the 1980s. R: Oxygen atoms combine with molecular oxygen to form ozone.

Both are true, but R (how ozone forms) does not explain A (why it dropped: CFCs). Answer (b).

⚠️ Watch out

  • Ozone depletion is caused by CFCs, not by CO₂. CO₂ causes global warming, a different problem.
  • Ozone is O₃, not O₂. It is harmful to breathe, so "ozone is poisonous" and "ozone protects us" are both correct, at different heights.

🧪 Try itCheck your fridge and AC

  1. Look at the label on the back of your refrigerator or air conditioner. Find the refrigerant name: R-600a, R-134a, R-32 or R-22.
  2. R-22 is an HCFC (still harmful to ozone); R-600a (isobutane) and R-32 are ozone-friendly.
  3. Make a class chart of how many homes have ozone-friendly appliances.

13.2.2

Managing the garbage we produce

As we become richer, our lifestyle changes and we produce more garbage: more packaging, more disposable items. Substances that are broken down by biological processes (bacteria, fungi) are biodegradable. Substances that are not broken down this way are non-biodegradable.

BiodegradableNon-biodegradable
Broken down by microbes?YesNo (may break into smaller pieces, but persist)
Examplesvegetable peels, paper, wood, cotton, leather, cow dungplastic, polythene, glass, metal cans, DDT
Harm (if in excess)bad smell, flies and germs, extra load on decomposers, can pollute waterstay for years, choke drains and animals, enter food chains (magnification), make soil infertile
🔻

Reduce

Use less: carry a cloth bag, refuse straws and disposable cups.

🔁

Reuse

Use again: glass jars for storage, old clothes as dusters.

♻️

Recycle

Segregate: green bin for wet (biodegradable) waste, blue bin for dry (recyclable) waste.

🤔 Story: kulhads on trains

Indian Railways once replaced plastic cups with disposable clay cups (kulhads). But making millions of kulhads used a lot of fertile topsoil. Then disposable paper cups were tried. The lesson: a "natural" option can also harm the environment if used on a huge scale. Reducing use is always best.

🏠 Methods of waste disposal (answer for "two methods")

  • Composting / vermicomposting: turns kitchen waste into manure.
  • Segregation and recycling: paper, plastic, glass and metal sent for recycling.
  • Biogas plants: cow dung and plant waste give fuel and manure.
  • Landfills (for what cannot be recycled) and incineration (for hospital waste).

🧪 Try itThe burial test (NCERT activity)

  1. Bury small pieces of a fruit peel, paper, a plastic wrapper, a cotton cloth and a piece of aluminium foil in a pot of soil. Keep it moist.
  2. Dig them up after 2 weeks and again after 4 weeks.
  3. Which ones changed? The peel and paper rot; plastic and foil look the same. Record your results in a table.

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Drawing arrows from eater to food. Arrows go from food to eater.
  2. Starting a food chain with an animal. Always start with a producer.
  3. Saying energy is recycled. Matter (nutrients) is recycled; energy flows one way.
  4. Writing "10% of sunlight is trapped by plants". It is about 1%; the 10% law is between trophic levels.
  5. Saying poison concentration decreases up the chain. It increases.
  6. Blaming CO₂ for ozone depletion. It is CFCs.
  7. Calling leather or wood non-biodegradable. They are biodegradable (slowly).
  8. Forgetting the year and organisation: UNEP, 1987, freeze at 1986 levels.

CBSE

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Science (086) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 39 questions: Section A has 20 one-mark questions (16 MCQs and 4 assertion–reason), Section B 6 two-mark, Section C 7 three-mark, Section D 3 five-mark, and Section E 3 four-mark case studies. Unit Natural Resources (Our Environment) carries 5 marks.

YearWhereWhat was asked

✍️ How marks are given

  • Biomagnification (2 marks): 1 for "chemicals enter the food chain because they are non-biodegradable", 1 for "they accumulate at each trophic level" (name the term).
  • Removing a trophic level (3 marks): 1 for the level below, 1 for the level above, 1 for the food-web comparison.
  • Ozone (3 marks): ½ name, 1 function, ½ CFCs, 1 UNEP step.

🏆 Answer-writing tips

  1. Draw food chains with arrows and label T1, T2, T3.
  2. Use the keywords examiners look for: "non-biodegradable", "trophic level", "unidirectional", "10 per cent law", "UV radiation", "CFCs".
  3. For energy numbers, show the ×1/10 step each time.

CBSE board questions (2023–2026)

Our Environment questions from CBSE Class 10 Science papers (set 31/1/1) with solutions. Wording lightly reworded; 2023 questions are reconstructed from the official marking scheme.

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q7 · 1 markWhich group of substances is not biodegradable? (A) vegetable peels, dead leaves, paper (B) cow dung, leather bag, water (C) polythene bag, rubber band, ball pen (D) paper, fruits, bones

(C) polythene bag, rubber band (synthetic), ball pen (plastic).

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q13 · 3 marksA four-tier pyramid shows (i) producers at the base, then (ii) primary, (iii) secondary and (iv) tertiary consumers. (a) Place deer, grass, lion, snake and rabbit at the correct levels. (b) Why do primary consumers have more energy than secondary consumers? (c) Why is the base of the pyramid broad?

(a) (i) grass; (ii) deer and rabbit; (iii) snake; (iv) lion, the top carnivore. 1

(b) Primary consumers eat producers directly. Only about 10% of energy passes from one level to the next, so secondary consumers receive only a tenth of what primary consumers have. 1

(c) Producers trap the most energy and are the most numerous, with the largest biomass, so the base is broadest. 1

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q14(c) · 1 markDifferentiate between consumers and decomposers.

Consumers feed on producers or other consumers and pass energy along the food chain. Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead organic matter into simple substances and return nutrients to the soil.

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q15 · 1 markWhich groups do NOT form a food chain? (i) wolf, rabbit, grass, lion (ii) plankton, man, grasshopper, fish (iii) hawk, grass, snake, grasshopper, frog (iv) grass, snake, wolf, tiger. (A) (i) and (iv) (B) (i) and (iii) (C) (ii) and (iii) (D) (ii) and (iv)

(D). (i) grass → rabbit → wolf → lion and (iii) grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk are chains. In (ii) a grasshopper does not eat fish, and in (iv) there is no herbivore.

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q16 · 1 markIn a terrestrial ecosystem, what percentage of the solar energy falling on leaves is NOT converted into food energy? (A) 1% (B) 10% (C) 90% (D) 99%

(D) 99%. Plants convert only about 1%.

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q20 · Assertion–ReasonA: The amount of ozone in the atmosphere began to drop sharply in the 1980s. R: Oxygen atoms combine with molecular oxygen to form ozone.

(B). Both true; R explains how ozone forms, not why it dropped (CFCs).

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q26 · 2 marksJustify: "Excessive use of chemicals and pesticides in agriculture adversely affects the environment."

They are washed into the soil and water bodies and harm the living and non-living components there. 1

Being non-biodegradable, they enter food chains and get concentrated at each trophic level (biological magnification), harming top consumers including humans. 1

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q15 · 1 markIdentify the food chain in which the organisms of the second trophic level are missing. (a) grass, goat, lion (b) zooplankton, phytoplankton, small fish, large fish (c) tiger, grass, snake, frog (d) grasshopper, grass, snake, frog, eagle

(c). There is no herbivore to eat the grass.

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q18 · Assertion–ReasonA: Accumulation of harmful chemicals is maximum in organisms at the highest trophic level of a food chain. R: Harmful chemicals are sprayed on crops to protect them from diseases and pests.

(a). The chemicals sprayed on crops enter the food chain and are magnified (biological magnification).

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q33 (A) · 3 marksIn the food chain plants → deer → lion, what will be the impact of removing all the organisms of the second trophic level on the first and third levels? Will the impact be the same for the third level if it were part of a food web? Justify.

First level: plants will increase at first, since nothing eats them (later they may be limited by space and nutrients). 1

Third level: lions will starve and decline, because their food is gone. 1

In a food web the impact is smaller, because lions have other prey (other herbivores). 1

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q33 (B, OR) · 3 marksGas X is a deadly poison at ground level but performs an essential function at higher levels of the atmosphere. Name X, state its function, name the chemicals linked to its decrease, and state the measure taken.

X = ozone (O₃). ½ It shields the Earth's surface from UV radiation. 1

Chemicals: CFCs (refrigerants, fire extinguishers). ½ Measure: in 1987 UNEP got an agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; CFC-free refrigerators are mandatory. 1

CBSE 2023 · 31/1/1 · Q26 · 2 marksHow do pesticides sprayed on crops reach our bodies? Name the phenomenon.

Pesticides wash into the soil and water, are absorbed by plants along with water and minerals, and enter the food chain through animals. 1

Being non-degradable, they accumulate at each successive trophic level: biological magnification. 1

CBSE 2023 · 31/1/1 · Q33 (A) · 3 marks(i) How is a kitchen garden different from a forest as an ecosystem? (ii) Suggest how to set up a self-sustaining jar ecosystem and justify why it can sustain itself.

(i) A kitchen garden is a human-made (artificial) ecosystem that needs care; a forest is a natural, self-sustaining ecosystem. 1

(ii) A jar with water, an air pump, a few aquatic plants and animals (like an aquarium). 1 Oxygen is replenished continuously and the plants serve as food, so it can sustain itself. 1

CBSE 2023 · 31/1/1 · Q33 (B, OR) · 3 marks(i) Make a food chain with plants, hawks, rats and snakes. (ii) If the second trophic level receives 20 000 J of energy, how much will be transferred to the third and fourth levels?

(i) Plants → rats → snakes → hawks. 1

(ii) By the 10 per cent law: third level = 2000 J, fourth level = 200 J. 2

Biological magnification came in 2023, 2024 and 2025. A food-chain MCQ (which is or isn't a chain, or which level is missing) came in both 2024 and 2025. Ozone and CFCs appeared in 2024 and 2025, and the 10% law with numbers in 2023 and 2026. Learn these four topics thoroughly.

Section B: 2 marks each

B1Why do food chains usually have only three or four trophic levels?

Only about 10% of energy passes to the next level; 90% is used in life processes or lost as heat. 1

After 3 or 4 steps, the energy left is too little to support another level of organisms. 1

B2Differentiate between biodegradable and non-biodegradable substances, with two examples of each.

Biodegradable: broken down by microorganisms, e.g. vegetable peels, paper. 1

Non-biodegradable: not broken down by microorganisms, e.g. plastic, DDT. 1

B3State two harmful effects of UV radiation on human beings.

Skin cancer; cataract (eye damage). Also weakens the immune system. 1 + 1

B4Why does an aquarium need to be cleaned regularly, while a pond or lake does not?

An aquarium is an artificial ecosystem with very few decomposers, so wastes and dead matter pile up. 1

A pond is a natural ecosystem with plenty of decomposers that break down wastes and recycle nutrients. 1

B5Give two ways in which non-biodegradable substances affect the environment.

(1) They persist for a long time, block drains and make soil infertile. 1

(2) They enter food chains and undergo biological magnification, harming animals and humans. 1

Section C: 3 marks each

C1What is biological magnification? Will its level be different at different trophic levels? Explain with an example.

The increase in concentration of harmful non-biodegradable chemicals at each successive trophic level. 1

Yes, it is lowest at the producers and highest at the top. 1

Example: DDT in water → plankton → small fish → big fish → fish-eating bird; the bird has the highest concentration. 1

C2State the 10 per cent law with an example. Why is the flow of energy in an ecosystem unidirectional?

Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next. 1

Example: grass 10 000 J → deer 1000 J → lion 100 J. 1

Energy captured by producers does not return to the Sun, and energy passed to herbivores does not come back to producers; at each step it is lost as heat. 1

C3"A food web is more stable than a food chain." Justify with an example.

In a food web, each organism has several food sources and several predators. 1

Example: if rabbits disappear, a hawk can still eat mice and snakes. 1

In a single chain (grass → rabbit → hawk), removing rabbits would starve the hawks. So the web is more stable. 1

C4How is ozone formed in the upper atmosphere? What is depleting it, and what step has been taken internationally?

UV radiation splits O₂ into free oxygen atoms; O + O₂ → O₃. 1

CFCs (used in refrigerators and fire extinguishers) deplete it. 1

UNEP agreement of 1987: freeze CFC production at 1986 levels. 1

Section D: 5 marks

D1(a) Define ecosystem and list its biotic and abiotic components. (b) Write a four-step food chain found in a pond. If the producers trap 10 000 J, how much energy reaches the top consumer? (c) What would happen if decomposers were absent?

(a) All living organisms of an area along with the non-living surroundings, interacting with each other. Biotic: producers, consumers, decomposers. Abiotic: temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, minerals, sunlight. 2

(b) Phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → big fish. Energy at the top = 10 000 × (1/10)³ = 10 J. 2

(c) Dead remains and wastes would pile up and nutrients would not return to the soil, so producers would run short of nutrients. 1

Section E: Case study (1 + 1 + 2)

E1Scientists measured DDT near a farm: water 0.00005 ppm, zooplankton 0.04 ppm, small fish 0.5 ppm, large fish 2 ppm, fish-eating birds 25 ppm. (i) Name this phenomenon. (ii) Which organism has the highest concentration, and why? (iii) Suggest two ways to reduce this problem. OR (iii) Why is DDT not broken down in the environment?

(i) Biological magnification. 1

(ii) Fish-eating birds: they are at the highest trophic level and DDT accumulates at each step. 1

(iii) Use bio-pesticides (like neem) and organic farming; spray only the needed amount and avoid spraying near water bodies. 2

OR (iii) DDT is a synthetic chemical; decomposers do not have enzymes to break it down (enzymes are specific), so it is non-biodegradable. 2

In-text questions

1What are trophic levels? Give an example of a food chain and state the different trophic levels in it.
The various steps in a food chain at which energy is transferred are called trophic levels. Example: grass (T1, producer) → deer (T2, primary consumer/herbivore) → lion (T3, secondary consumer/carnivore).
2What is the role of decomposers in the ecosystem?
Decomposers (bacteria, fungi) break down dead plants, animals and their wastes into simple inorganic substances. These go back into the soil and are used again by plants. So decomposers recycle nutrients and keep the ecosystem clean.
3Why are some substances biodegradable and some non-biodegradable?
Microorganisms break down substances using enzymes, and enzymes are specific. Natural materials (peels, paper, cotton) can be acted on by these enzymes, so they are biodegradable. Man-made substances like plastics and DDT cannot be broken down by these enzymes, so they are non-biodegradable.
4Give any two ways in which biodegradable substances would affect the environment.
(1) In large amounts they rot and give a foul smell and attract flies, spreading disease. (2) They can pollute water bodies and use up oxygen as they decompose. (They also return nutrients to the soil, a good effect.)
5Give any two ways in which non-biodegradable substances would affect the environment.
(1) They remain for a long time, choking drains and making soil infertile. (2) They enter food chains and get concentrated by biological magnification, harming animals and humans.
6What is ozone and how does it affect any ecosystem?
Ozone (O₃) is a molecule of three oxygen atoms. In the upper atmosphere it forms a layer that absorbs harmful UV radiation. If it is depleted, more UV reaches the Earth, causing skin cancer and cataracts in humans and damaging plants and plankton, which affects the whole ecosystem.
7How can you help in reducing the problem of waste disposal? Give any two methods.
(1) Segregate waste into wet (biodegradable) and dry (recyclable) bins, and compost the kitchen waste. (2) Reduce and reuse: carry cloth bags, use steel bottles instead of disposable plastic ones.

(b) grass → goat → human.

(d) all of the above.
4What will happen if we kill all the organisms in one trophic level?
The food chain breaks. The organisms of the level above lose their food and decline; the organisms of the level below lose their predators and multiply. For example, killing all herbivores makes plants overgrow and carnivores starve. The balance of the ecosystem is disturbed. (Try it in the Food-web Breaker lab.)
5Will the impact of removing all the organisms in a trophic level be different for different trophic levels? Can the organisms of any trophic level be removed without causing any damage to the ecosystem?
Yes, the impact differs. Removing producers would destroy the entire ecosystem, because all energy enters through them. Removing herbivores would make plants overgrow and carnivores starve. Removing top carnivores would make herbivores multiply and overgraze. Removing decomposers would stop nutrient recycling. No, organisms of no trophic level can be removed without damaging the ecosystem.
6What is biological magnification? Will the levels of this magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?
Biological magnification is the progressive increase in the concentration of harmful non-biodegradable chemicals (like DDT) at each successive trophic level of a food chain. Yes, the levels are different: lowest at the producers and highest at the top consumers, such as humans and birds of prey.
7What are the problems caused by the non-biodegradable wastes that we generate?
They persist for years and pile up as garbage; plastics choke drains and cause flooding; animals swallow plastic bags and die; chemicals like DDT enter food chains and get magnified; they reduce soil fertility and pollute water; burning them releases toxic gases.
8If all the waste we generate is biodegradable, will this have no impact on the environment?
It will still have an impact. Huge amounts of biodegradable waste take time to decompose; meanwhile they smell, breed flies and germs, and can pollute water and use up its oxygen. Producing them (like kulhads) can also use up resources. But the impact is less long-lasting than that of non-biodegradable waste.
9Why is damage to the ozone layer a cause for concern? What steps are being taken to limit this damage?
The ozone layer absorbs harmful UV radiation. Its damage lets more UV reach the Earth, causing skin cancer, cataracts and weak immunity in humans, and harming plants and aquatic life. Steps: in 1987 UNEP got countries to agree to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels; CFC-free refrigerators and ozone-friendly refrigerants are now used, and CFCs are being phased out.
