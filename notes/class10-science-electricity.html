📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Current I = Q/t (ampere, A). Electrons flow − to +; conventional current flows + to −. 1 C = charge of 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons.
  • Potential difference V = W/Q (volt, V). Ammeter goes in series, voltmeter in parallel.
  • Ohm's law: V = IR at constant temperature; the V–I graph is a straight line through the origin. 1 Ω = 1 V/A.
  • Resistance R = ρl/A: R ∝ length, R ∝ 1/area. Resistivity ρ (Ω m) depends only on the material and temperature.
  • Series: Rs = R₁ + R₂ + R₃, same current. Parallel: 1/Rp = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃, same voltage. Homes use parallel wiring.
  • Joule's law of heating: H = I²Rt. Used in heaters, irons, bulbs and fuses.
  • Power P = VI = I²R = V²/R (watt). Energy = Pt; 1 kWh (1 unit) = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
  • CBSE weightage: unit Effects of Current (Ch 11 and 12) carries 13 marks. Expect one numerical of 3 to 5 marks every year.
11.1

Electric current and circuit

A continuous, closed path for electric charge is an electric circuit. The rate of flow of charge through any cross-section of a conductor is the electric current. In metal wires the charges that move are electrons.

I = Q / t · SI unit: ampere (A) = coulomb per second
🔋

Charge of an electron

e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C. So 1 C = 1 / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons. Q = ne.

📏

Small units

1 mA = 10⁻³ A; 1 μA = 10⁻⁶ A. Current is measured with an ammeter, always connected in series.

🐢

Slow drift, fast signal

Electrons drift only about 1 mm/s, but the bulb lights at once because the electric field is set up through the whole wire almost instantly.

Ex 11.1A current of 0.5 A is drawn by a bulb for 10 minutes. Find the charge that flows through the circuit.

I = 0.5 A, t = 10 × 60 = 600 s. Q = It = 0.5 × 600 = 300 C.

  2. Touch the pin to a drawing pin: the circuit closes and the bulb glows. Lift it: the circuit opens.
  3. Draw your circuit using the symbols in 11.3. Which way does the conventional current flow?
11.2

Electric potential and potential difference

Water flows in a pipe only when there is a pressure difference; charge flows in a wire only when there is a difference of electric potential. A cell keeps this difference using chemical reactions. The potential difference between two points is the work done to move a unit charge from one point to the other.

V = W / Q · 1 volt = 1 joule / 1 coulomb

One volt is the potential difference when 1 J of work is done to move 1 C of charge. Potential difference is measured with a voltmeter, connected in parallel across the points. A voltmeter has very high resistance, an ammeter very low resistance.

Ex 11.2How much work is done in moving a charge of 2 C across two points having a potential difference of 12 V?

W = VQ = 12 × 2 = 24 J.

🤔 Water analogy

Battery = pump, potential difference = pressure difference, current = rate of water flow, resistance = a narrow pipe. A bigger "push" (voltage) or a wider pipe (less resistance) gives more flow (current).

🧪 Try itCount the volts in a torch

  1. Open a torch and see how the cells are placed: + of one touches − of the next. This is a battery (cells in series).
  2. In the school lab, measure one cell with a voltmeter (about 1.5 V), then two cells in series (about 3 V).
  3. Why does the torch glow brighter with two cells?
11.3

Circuit diagram symbols

A circuit diagram shows how the components of a circuit are connected, using standard symbols. Learn them: CBSE asks you to draw circuits in almost every paper.

⚠️ Drawing rules that save marks

  • Draw wires with a ruler as straight lines and right-angled corners.
  • Ammeter in series with the resistor; voltmeter across (parallel to) the resistor.
  • Mark + and − on the ammeter and voltmeter: + goes towards the + terminal of the battery.
  • Show the direction of conventional current with an arrow.
11.4

Ohm's law

In 1827 Georg Simon Ohm found that the potential difference V across a metallic wire is directly proportional to the current I through it, provided its temperature remains the same. This is Ohm's law.

V ∝ I → V = I R · R = resistance, SI unit ohm (Ω) · 1 Ω = 1 V / 1 A
  • Resistance is the property of a conductor to oppose the flow of charge through it. I = V/R: for the same V, more R means less current.
  • A rheostat is a variable resistance used to change the current without changing the voltage source.
  • Good conductors have low resistance; insulators have very high resistance.
Ex 11.3(a) How much current will a bulb draw from a 220 V source if its filament has a resistance of 1200 Ω? (b) How much current will a heater coil of 100 Ω draw from the same source?

(a) I = V/R = 220/1200 = 0.18 A. (b) I = 220/100 = 2.2 A. The heater, with less resistance, draws more current.

Ex 11.4The potential difference across an electric heater is 60 V when it draws 4 A. What current will it draw if the potential difference is raised to 120 V?

R = V/I = 60/4 = 15 Ω. At 120 V: I = 120/15 = 8 A. (Double the voltage, double the current, when R is constant.)

🧪 Try itVerify Ohm's law (school lab)

  1. Set up the circuit above with a 0.5 m nichrome wire, an ammeter and a voltmeter.
  2. Use one cell, then two, three and four. Record V and I in a table and work out V/I each time.
  3. Plot V (y-axis) against I (x-axis). Is the graph a straight line through the origin?

11.5

Factors on which the resistance of a conductor depends

Activity 11.3 shows that the resistance of a conductor depends on (i) its length, (ii) its area of cross-section and (iii) the nature of its material. It also changes with temperature.

R = ρ l / A → ρ = R A / l · SI unit of resistivity: ohm metre (Ω m)
  • Resistivity is a property of the material. It does not change when you cut, stretch or bend the wire; resistance does.
  • Metals and alloys have very low resistivity (10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁶ Ω m); insulators like rubber and glass have 10¹² to 10¹⁷ Ω m.
  • Resistivity of an alloy is generally higher than that of its constituent metals, and alloys do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures. So nichrome is used in heaters, toasters and irons.
  • Tungsten is used for bulb filaments; copper and aluminium for transmission wires (lowest resistivity, cheap).

Ex 11.5(a) A 1 m long metal wire has a resistance of 26 Ω at 20 °C. Its diameter is 0.3 mm. Find the resistivity. (b) What will be the resistance of the wire if its length is doubled?

(a) A = πd²/4 = 3.14 × (3 × 10⁻⁴)² / 4 = 7.07 × 10⁻⁸ m². ρ = RA/l = 26 × 7.07 × 10⁻⁸ / 1 = 1.84 × 10⁻⁶ Ω m (manganese).

(b) R ∝ l, so R becomes 52 Ω. ρ stays the same.

Ex 11.6A wire of length l and area A has a resistance of 4 Ω. What is the resistance of a wire of the same material with length l/2 and area 2A?

R₂ = ρ(l/2)/(2A) = ¼ × ρl/A = ¼ × 4 = 1 Ω.

🏆 Stretching trick (CBSE favourite)

When a wire is stretched to n times its length, its volume stays the same, so the area becomes 1/n. Then R becomes n² R. Stretched to double length: R → 4R. Resistivity is unchanged (CBSE 2024).

🧪 Try itPencil-lead resistor

  1. Shade a thick strip about 10 cm long with a soft (4B or 6B) pencil on paper. Graphite conducts.
  2. With a multimeter in the lab, measure the resistance between points 2 cm, 5 cm and 10 cm apart.
  3. Then shade the strip twice as wide and measure again. Do your readings follow R ∝ l and R ∝ 1/A?

11.6

Resistance of a system of resistors

11.6.1 Resistors in series

Resistors joined end to end are in series. The same current flows through each, and the total potential difference is the sum of the potential differences across them.

Ex 11.7An electric lamp of 20 Ω and a conductor of 4 Ω are connected in series to a 6 V battery. Find (a) the total resistance (b) the current (c) the potential difference across the lamp and the conductor.

(a) Rs = 20 + 4 = 24 Ω. (b) I = V/R = 6/24 = 0.25 A.

(c) V lamp = 0.25 × 20 = 5 V; V conductor = 0.25 × 4 = 1 V. Check: 5 + 1 = 6 V ✓

11.6.2 Resistors in parallel

Resistors connected between the same two points are in parallel. The potential difference is the same across each, and the total current is the sum of the separate currents.

Ex 11.8Resistors of 5 Ω, 10 Ω and 30 Ω are connected in parallel to a 12 V battery. Find the current through each resistor, the total current and the total resistance.

I₁ = 12/5 = 2.4 A; I₂ = 12/10 = 1.2 A; I₃ = 12/30 = 0.4 A. Total I = 4 A.

1/Rp = 1/5 + 1/10 + 1/30 = (6 + 3 + 1)/30 = 10/30, so Rp = 3 Ω. Check: 12/3 = 4 A ✓

Ex 11.9In a mixed circuit, R₁ = 10 Ω and R₂ = 40 Ω are in parallel; this pair is in series with a group of R₃ = 30 Ω, R₄ = 20 Ω and R₅ = 60 Ω in parallel. A 12 V battery is connected. Find the total resistance and the current.

Pair: 1/R′ = 1/10 + 1/40 = 5/40 → R′ = 8 Ω. Group: 1/R″ = 1/30 + 1/20 + 1/60 = 6/60 → R″ = 10 Ω.

Total R = 8 + 10 = 18 Ω. I = 12/18 = 0.67 A.

🏠

Why homes use parallel

Each appliance gets the full 220 V, can be switched on or off on its own, and draws the current it needs. If one fails, the others keep working.

🎄

Problem with series

If one bulb in a series string fuses, the whole circuit breaks. The same current through a heater and a bulb is not suitable for both.

🧮

Two-resistor shortcut

For two resistors in parallel, Rp = R₁R₂ / (R₁ + R₂). For n equal resistors R: series nR, parallel R/n.

🧪 Try itSeries vs parallel torch bulbs

  2. Now connect the two bulbs in parallel with the same cells. Compare the brightness, and unscrew one bulb again.
  3. Explain both results using "same current" and "same voltage".

11.7

Heating effect of electric current

A battery spends energy to keep current flowing. In a purely resistive circuit (only resistors), all this energy turns into heat. This is the heating effect of electric current.

Work done to move charge Q through potential difference V in time t: W = VQ = VIt. Using V = IR:

H = V I t = I² R t = V² t / R (Joule's law of heating)

Joule's law: heat produced in a resistor is (i) directly proportional to the square of the current, (ii) directly proportional to the resistance, and (iii) directly proportional to the time for which current flows.

  • Bulb: the tungsten filament (m.p. 3380 °C) glows white-hot. The bulb is filled with inert nitrogen and argon to make the filament last longer. Most of the energy is lost as heat, so LEDs are better.
  • Unwanted heating wastes energy in wires, computers and transformers and can damage components.
  • Fuse rating: an iron of 1 kW at 220 V draws 1000/220 = 4.54 A, so a 5 A fuse is used.
Ex 11.11100 J of heat is produced each second in a 4 Ω resistor. Find the potential difference across it.

H = I²Rt → 100 = I² × 4 × 1 → I = 5 A. V = IR = 5 × 4 = 20 V.

🤔 Why does the heater coil glow but its cord stays cool?

The same current flows through both (series), but the coil (nichrome) has a much higher resistance than the copper cord. Since H = I²Rt, nearly all the heat is produced in the coil.

🧪 Try itHeat detective at home

  1. With an adult, touch (carefully) a charger, a TV back and an LED bulb after they have run for 15 minutes. Which is warmest?
  2. Look at the label of an electric iron or kettle. Note its power and voltage.
  3. Calculate the current it draws (I = P/V) and pick a suitable fuse.
11.8

Electric power and energy

The rate at which electric energy is consumed (or dissipated) in a circuit is electric power.

P = V I = I² R = V² / R · SI unit: watt (W) = 1 V × 1 A

1 kW = 1000 W. The commercial unit of energy is the kilowatt hour (kWh), called 1 unit on your electricity bill. Note: "kilowatt hour" is a unit of energy, not power.

Ex 11.10An electric iron uses 840 W at its highest setting and 360 W at its lowest setting at 220 V. Find the current and the resistance in each case.

Highest: I = P/V = 840/220 = 3.82 A; R = V²/P = 220²/840 = 57.6 Ω.

Lowest: I = 360/220 = 1.64 A; R = 220²/360 = 134.4 Ω.

Ex 11.12(a) A bulb on a 220 V supply draws 0.50 A. What is its power? (b) A 400 W refrigerator runs 8 hours a day. What is the cost of running it for 30 days at ₹3.00 per kWh?

(a) P = VI = 220 × 0.50 = 110 W.

(b) Energy = 0.4 kW × 8 h × 30 = 96 kWh. Cost = 96 × 3 = ₹288.

🏆 Which formula to use?

  • Same voltage (parallel, house wiring): use P = V²/R. A higher power bulb has lower resistance.
  • Same current (series): use P = I²R. The larger resistance gets more power, so a 40 W bulb in series with a 100 W bulb glows brighter.

  • V = IR (Ohm's law)

  • H = I²Rt
  • Bulb, heater, fuse

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Forgetting to convert minutes to seconds, or mm² to m². Always use SI units: 1 mm = 10⁻³ m, so 1 mm² = 10⁻⁶ m².
  2. Using radius instead of diameter (or the other way) in A = πr².
  3. Saying resistivity changes when a wire is cut or stretched. Only resistance changes.
  4. Writing 1/Rp and forgetting to take the reciprocal at the end.
  5. Connecting the voltmeter in series or the ammeter in parallel in circuit diagrams.
  6. Calling kWh a unit of power. It is a unit of energy; kW is power.
  7. Using P = I²R for appliances on the same mains voltage. Use P = V²/R when V is fixed.
  8. Writing "Ohm's law holds always". It holds only if temperature (and other physical conditions) stay the same; a bulb filament does not obey it as it heats.

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Science (086) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 39 questions: Section A has 20 one-mark questions (16 MCQs and 4 assertion–reason), Section B 6 two-mark, Section C 7 three-mark, Section D 3 five-mark, and Section E 3 four-mark case studies. Unit Effects of Current (Chapters 11 and 12) carries 13 marks, and Electricity almost always brings a long numerical.

✍️ How marks are given in numericals

  • Formula written first: ½ mark.
  • Substitution with correct SI units: ½ to 1 mark.
  • Answer with unit: ½ mark. A correct number without a unit loses this.
  • Circuit diagrams: correct symbols, ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel, current arrow.

🏆 Answer-writing tips

  1. Write "Given:", list the values in SI units, then "To find:", then the formula. Examiners love this layout.
  2. Check parallel answers: Rp must be smaller than the smallest resistor; series Rs larger than the largest.
  3. Round only at the last step and keep two decimal places.

(b) Alloys have high resistivity, so they produce more heat, and they do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperature. 2

(c) 1 ampere is the current when 1 coulomb of charge flows through a cross-section of a conductor in 1 second. 2

(b) In series with the circuit (its + terminal towards the + of the battery). 1

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q25 · 2 marksA wire of resistance R is cut into three equal parts, which are then connected in parallel. Find the new resistance. OR Define electric power and the SI unit watt.

Each part has resistance R/3. ½ Three equal resistances in parallel: 1/Rp = 3/R + 3/R + 3/R = 9/R. 1 Rp = R/9. ½

OR Electric power is the rate at which electric energy is consumed or dissipated in a circuit, P = VI. 1 1 watt is the power consumed by a device that carries 1 A when operated at a potential difference of 1 V (1 W = 1 V × 1 A). 1

CBSE 2024 · 31/1/1 · Q25 · 2 marksHow will you connect three resistors of 6 Ω each to get an equivalent resistance of 9 Ω? Show with a diagram.

Connect two 6 Ω resistors in parallel: Rp = 6 × 6 / (6 + 6) = 3 Ω. 1

Connect this pair in series with the third 6 Ω: R = 3 + 6 = 9 Ω (draw the pair side by side, followed by the third). 1

(b). R = V²/P = 48400/60 ≈ 807 Ω.

Section B: 2 marks each

B1State Ohm's law. Draw the V–I graph for a metallic conductor and state what its slope gives.

At constant temperature, the potential difference across a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the current through it: V = IR. 1

The graph is a straight line through the origin; its slope (V/I) gives the resistance. 1

n = Q/e = 150 / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 9.4 × 10²⁰ electrons. 1

= 6 × 3.6 × 10⁶ = 2.16 × 10⁷ J. 1

B4Give two reasons why series arrangement is not used in domestic circuits.

If one appliance fails or is switched off, the circuit breaks and all others stop. 1

All appliances get the same current and share the voltage, but each needs the full 220 V and a different current; the total resistance also becomes large. 1

C4State Joule's law of heating. Derive H = I²Rt. Give two applications.

Heat produced in a resistor ∝ I², ∝ R and ∝ t. 1

Work done to move charge Q through p.d. V: W = VQ = VIt. Since V = IR, H = I²Rt (all work turns into heat in a resistor). 1

Applications: electric heater, iron, toaster, bulb, fuse (any two). 1

NCERT

All textbook questions answered

In-text questions

1What does an electric circuit mean?
A continuous and closed path of an electric current, made of a source (cell or battery), connecting wires, a key and devices such as a bulb or resistor.
2Define the unit of current.
The SI unit of current is the ampere. 1 A is the current when 1 coulomb of charge flows through any cross-section of a conductor in 1 second (1 A = 1 C/s).
3Calculate the number of electrons constituting one coulomb of charge.
n = Q/e = 1 / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons.
4Name a device that helps to maintain a potential difference across a conductor.
A cell or a battery (also a generator).
5What is meant by saying that the potential difference between two points is 1 V?
1 joule of work is done to move 1 coulomb of charge from one point to the other.
6How much energy is given to each coulomb of charge passing through a 6 V battery?
W = VQ = 6 × 1 = 6 J.
7On what factors does the resistance of a conductor depend?
(i) Length (R ∝ l), (ii) area of cross-section (R ∝ 1/A), (iii) nature of the material (resistivity), and (iv) temperature.
8Will current flow more easily through a thick wire or a thin wire of the same material, connected to the same source? Why?
Through the thick wire, because R ∝ 1/A: a thicker wire has less resistance, so more current flows.
9The resistance of a component stays constant while the potential difference across it is halved. What happens to the current?
By Ohm's law I = V/R, the current also becomes half.
10Why are coils of electric toasters and electric irons made of an alloy rather than a pure metal?
Alloys have higher resistivity (so they produce more heat) and do not oxidise or burn readily at high temperatures.
11Use Table 11.2 to answer: (a) Which among iron and mercury is a better conductor? (b) Which material is the best conductor?
(a) Iron (ρ = 10.0 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m) is better than mercury (94.0 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m). (b) Silver (1.60 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m), the lowest resistivity.
12Draw a schematic diagram of a circuit with a battery of three 2 V cells, resistors of 5 Ω, 8 Ω and 12 Ω and a plug key, all in series.
Draw three cells joined + to − (battery of 6 V), then a plug key, then the 5 Ω, 8 Ω and 12 Ω resistors end to end, and close the loop back to the battery. (See the series diagram in 11.6.1; use three resistors and three cells.)
13Redraw the circuit with an ammeter to measure the current through the resistors and a voltmeter across the 12 Ω resistor. What are the readings?
Ammeter in series anywhere in the loop; voltmeter in parallel across the 12 Ω resistor. R = 5 + 8 + 12 = 25 Ω, V = 6 V. Ammeter: I = 6/25 = 0.24 A. Voltmeter: V = IR = 0.24 × 12 = 2.88 V.
14Judge the equivalent resistance when these are connected in parallel: (a) 1 Ω and 10⁶ Ω (b) 1 Ω, 10³ Ω and 10⁶ Ω.
In parallel, Rp is less than the smallest resistance. (a) Rp = 10⁶/(10⁶ + 1) ≈ 0.999999 Ω ≈ 1 Ω (just under 1 Ω). (b) 1/Rp = 1 + 0.001 + 0.000001 = 1.001001 → Rp ≈ 0.999 Ω.
15A lamp of 100 Ω, a toaster of 50 Ω and a water filter of 500 Ω are connected in parallel to 220 V. What is the resistance of an iron that takes as much current as all three together, and what is the current through it?
1/Rp = 1/100 + 1/50 + 1/500 = (5 + 10 + 1)/500 = 16/500 → Rp = 31.25 Ω. The iron has this resistance. I = 220/31.25 = 7.04 A.
16What are the advantages of connecting electrical devices in parallel with the battery instead of in series?
(i) Each device gets the full voltage. (ii) Each can be switched on and off independently. (iii) If one fails, the others keep working. (iv) Total resistance is less, and each device draws the current it needs.
17How can resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω be connected to give (a) 4 Ω (b) 1 Ω?
(a) 3 Ω and 6 Ω in parallel (= 2 Ω), in series with 2 Ω: 2 + 2 = 4 Ω. (b) All three in parallel: 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 1 → 1 Ω.
18What is (a) the highest (b) the lowest total resistance that can be obtained with coils of 4 Ω, 8 Ω, 12 Ω and 24 Ω?
(a) All in series: 4 + 8 + 12 + 24 = 48 Ω. (b) All in parallel: 1/R = 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/12 + 1/24 = (6 + 3 + 2 + 1)/24 = 1/2 → 2 Ω.
19Why does the cord of an electric heater not glow while the heating element does?
The same current flows through both, but the element (nichrome) has a much higher resistance than the copper cord. Heat H = I²Rt is far greater in the element, so it glows; the cord stays almost cool.
20Compute the heat generated while transferring 96 000 C of charge in one hour through a potential difference of 50 V.
H = VQ = 50 × 96 000 = 4.8 × 10⁶ J.
21An electric iron of resistance 20 Ω takes a current of 5 A. Calculate the heat developed in 30 s.
H = I²Rt = 5² × 20 × 30 = 1.5 × 10⁴ J.
22What determines the rate at which energy is delivered by a current?
Electric power, P = VI (the product of potential difference and current).
23An electric motor takes 5 A from a 220 V line. Determine the power of the motor and the energy consumed in 2 h.
P = VI = 220 × 5 = 1100 W. E = Pt = 1100 × 7200 = 7.92 × 10⁶ J (= 2.2 kWh).

Exercise questions

1A wire of resistance R is cut into five equal parts, which are connected in parallel. If the equivalent resistance is R′, the ratio R/R′ is (a) 1/25 (b) 1/5 (c) 5 (d) 25
(d) 25. Each part R/5; five in parallel: R′ = (R/5)/5 = R/25, so R/R′ = 25.
2Which term does not represent electrical power? (a) I²R (b) IR² (c) VI (d) V²/R
(b) IR².
3A bulb is rated 220 V, 100 W. When operated on 110 V, the power consumed is (a) 100 W (b) 75 W (c) 50 W (d) 25 W
(d) 25 W. R = 220²/100 = 484 Ω; P = 110²/484 = 25 W (half voltage → quarter power).
4Two identical wires are connected first in series and then in parallel across the same potential difference. The ratio of heat produced (series : parallel) is (a) 1:2 (b) 2:1 (c) 1:4 (d) 4:1
(c) 1:4. Rs = 2R, Rp = R/2. H = V²t/R, so Hs : Hp = (1/2R) : (2/R) = 1 : 4.
5How is a voltmeter connected in the circuit to measure the potential difference between two points?
In parallel across the two points, with its + terminal towards the + terminal of the battery.
6A copper wire has diameter 0.5 mm and resistivity 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ Ω m. What length gives a resistance of 10 Ω? How does the resistance change if the diameter is doubled?
A = πd²/4 = 3.14 × (0.5 × 10⁻³)² / 4 = 1.96 × 10⁻⁷ m². l = RA/ρ = 10 × 1.96 × 10⁻⁷ / (1.6 × 10⁻⁸) ≈ 122.7 m.
Doubling the diameter makes the area four times, so the resistance becomes one-fourth: 2.5 Ω.
7For a resistor, I = 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0, 4.0 A and V = 1.6, 3.4, 6.7, 10.2, 13.2 V. Plot V against I and calculate the resistance.
The points lie close to a straight line through the origin. Slope = (13.2 − 1.6)/(4.0 − 0.5) = 11.6/3.5 ≈ 3.3 Ω. (V/I for each pair is about 3.2 to 3.4 Ω.)
8A 12 V battery connected across an unknown resistor gives a current of 2.5 mA. Find the resistance.
R = V/I = 12 / (2.5 × 10⁻³) = 4800 Ω = 4.8 kΩ.
9A 9 V battery is connected in series with resistors of 0.2 Ω, 0.3 Ω, 0.4 Ω, 0.5 Ω and 12 Ω. How much current flows through the 12 Ω resistor?
Rs = 0.2 + 0.3 + 0.4 + 0.5 + 12 = 13.4 Ω. In series the same current flows through all: I = 9/13.4 = 0.67 A.
10How many 176 Ω resistors in parallel are required to carry 5 A on a 220 V line?
Required Rp = 220/5 = 44 Ω. For n equal resistors, Rp = 176/n → n = 176/44 = 4.
11How would you connect three resistors of 6 Ω each to get (i) 9 Ω (ii) 4 Ω?
(i) Two in parallel (3 Ω) in series with the third: 3 + 6 = 9 Ω.
(ii) Two in series (12 Ω) in parallel with the third: 12 × 6 / 18 = 4 Ω.
12Several bulbs rated 10 W for 220 V are connected in parallel across a 220 V line. How many can be connected if the maximum allowable current is 5 A?
Current per bulb = P/V = 10/220 = 1/22 A. Number = 5 ÷ (1/22) = 110 bulbs. (Or: R of each = 4840 Ω; required Rp = 44 Ω; n = 4840/44 = 110.)
13A hot plate on a 220 V line has two coils A and B of 24 Ω each, which can be used separately, in series or in parallel. What are the currents in the three cases?
Separately: 220/24 = 9.17 A. Series (48 Ω): 220/48 = 4.58 A. Parallel (12 Ω): 220/12 = 18.33 A.
14Compare the power used in the 2 Ω resistor in: (i) a 6 V battery in series with 1 Ω and 2 Ω resistors (ii) a 4 V battery in parallel with 12 Ω and 2 Ω resistors.
(i) I = 6/(1 + 2) = 2 A; P = I²R = 4 × 2 = 8 W.
(ii) Full 4 V across the 2 Ω: P = V²/R = 16/2 = 8 W.
The power is the same in both.
15Two lamps rated 100 W and 60 W at 220 V are connected in parallel to 220 V mains. What current is drawn from the line?
Total power = 160 W. I = P/V = 160/220 = 0.73 A.
16Which uses more energy: a 250 W TV in 1 hour or a 1200 W toaster in 10 minutes?
TV: 250 × 3600 = 9 × 10⁵ J. Toaster: 1200 × 600 = 7.2 × 10⁵ J. The TV uses more energy.
17An electric heater of resistance 8 Ω draws 15 A from the mains for 2 hours. Calculate the rate at which heat is developed.
Rate of heat = power = I²R = 15² × 8 = 1800 W (1800 J every second). (Total heat in 2 h = 1800 × 7200 = 1.3 × 10⁷ J.)
18Explain: (a) Why is tungsten used almost exclusively for bulb filaments? (b) Why are conductors of heating devices made of an alloy? (c) Why is series arrangement not used in domestic circuits? (d) How does resistance vary with area of cross-section? (e) Why are copper and aluminium used for transmission wires?
(a) Tungsten has a very high melting point (3380 °C) and high resistivity; it glows white-hot without melting.
(b) Alloys have high resistivity and do not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperatures.
(c) In series, all devices get the same current, the voltage is shared, the total resistance is high, and if one device fails the whole circuit stops.
(d) Resistance is inversely proportional to the area of cross-section (R ∝ 1/A): thicker wire, lower resistance.
(e) They have very low resistivity (good conductors), so less energy is lost as heat; aluminium is also light and cheap.
