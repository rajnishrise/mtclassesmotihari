📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Similar figures: same shape, not necessarily the same size. Congruent figures are similar with ratio 1.
  • Similar polygons: corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides in the same ratio (both needed). For triangles, either one guarantees the other.
  • BPT (Thales): a line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides in the same ratio. Converse: a line dividing two sides in the same ratio is parallel to the third side.
  • Criteria: AAA (or AA), SSS (sides proportional), SAS (one angle equal, including sides proportional).
  • Removed from syllabus: areas of similar triangles theorem and the Pythagoras theorem section of this chapter. Proofs of AA, SSS, SAS are not asked.
  • Most-asked in CBSE: BPT statement and proof (3–5 marks), "find x" using BPT or similar triangles, "prove that" using AA or SAS, and ratio questions. Unit Geometry carries 15 marks; this chapter usually brings 6 to 9.
6.1

Similar figures

Two figures are similar if they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size. A photo and its enlargement, a map and the land, a model aeroplane and the real one: all similar.

⭕

Always similar

All circles, all squares, all equilateral triangles, all line segments.

🟰

Congruent ⇒ similar

Congruent figures are similar with scale factor 1. But similar figures need not be congruent.

🚫

Not always similar

Two rectangles, two rhombuses, two isosceles triangles: they may have different shapes.

🤔 Real life: scale factor

A map with scale 1 : 50000 is similar to the ground: every distance is multiplied by 50000, every angle stays the same. A 4 × 6 photo enlarged to 8 × 12 keeps its shape (scale factor 2); enlarged to 8 × 8 it gets stretched and is no longer similar.

🧪 Try it nowPhone zoom test

  1. Open a photo of a triangle-shaped roof on a phone. Pinch to zoom: the picture gets bigger but the angles do not change.
  2. Now use the edit "stretch" or "crop to square" option. The roof looks flatter: the angles changed, so the new picture is not similar.
  3. Conclusion: zooming makes similar figures; stretching does not.
6.2

Similar polygons and similar triangles

Two polygons with the same number of sides are similar if (i) their corresponding angles are equal and (ii) their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportional).

ΔABC ~ ΔDEF ⇔ ∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, ∠C = ∠F and AB/DE = BC/EF = CA/FD
ShapesAngles equal?Sides proportional?Similar?
Square and rhombus (side 5 each)❌ (90° vs other)✅ (all 1 : 1)❌
Square and rectangle 5 × 8✅ (all 90°)❌❌
Triangles with angles 50°, 60°, 70°✅✅ automatically✅

⚠️ Order of letters matters

"ΔABC ~ ΔPQR" means A ↔ P, B ↔ Q, C ↔ R. So AB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RP. If ∠A = ∠Q and ∠B = ∠R, you must write ΔABC ~ ΔQRP, not ΔPQR. CBSE cuts marks for a wrong correspondence.

Quick example (CBSE 2026)

ΔABC ~ ΔDEF with 2AB = DE and BC = 8 cm. Then AB/DE = BC/EF = 1/2 ⇒ EF = 16 cm.

🧪 Try itTwo rectangles

  1. Cut a rectangle 4 cm × 6 cm and another 6 cm × 9 cm from paper.
  2. Check: angles all 90° ✓; ratios 4/6 = 6/9 = 2/3 ✓. They are similar. Put the small one on a corner of the big one: the diagonals line up!
  3. Now cut 4 × 6 and 5 × 7. Ratios 4/5 ≠ 6/7: not similar, and the diagonals do not line up.
6.3

Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales theorem)

Theorem 6.1 (BPT): If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

ProofProve the BPT (the most-asked proof of Class 10: write it exactly like this)

Given: In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC, with D on AB and E on AC.

To prove: AD/DB = AE/EC.

Construction: Join BE and CD. Draw EN ⊥ AB and DM ⊥ AC. 1

Proof: ar(ADE) = ½ × AD × EN and ar(BDE) = ½ × DB × EN.

So ar(ADE)/ar(BDE) = AD/DB …(1) 1

Similarly ar(ADE) = ½ × AE × DM and ar(DEC) = ½ × EC × DM, so ar(ADE)/ar(DEC) = AE/EC …(2) 1

ΔBDE and ΔDEC are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC, so ar(BDE) = ar(DEC) …(3) 1

From (1), (2) and (3): AD/DB = AE/EC. Hence proved. 1

📏 Useful forms of BPT (all follow from AD/DB = AE/EC)

  • AD/AB = AE/AC   ·   DB/AB = EC/AC   ·   AB/DB = AC/EC
  • Also ΔADE ~ ΔABC, so DE/BC = AD/AB.

Theorem 6.2 (Converse of BPT): If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.

NCERT Ex 6.2 Q1: find EC and AD (DE ∥ BC)

(i) AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm: 1.5/3 = 1/EC ⇒ EC = 2 cm.

(ii) DB = 7.2 cm, AE = 1.8 cm, EC = 5.4 cm: AD/7.2 = 1.8/5.4 ⇒ AD = 2.4 cm.

CBSE 2026: algebra with BPT

DE ∥ BC, AD = x, DB = x − 2, AE = x + 2, EC = x − 1. Then x/(x − 2) = (x + 2)/(x − 1) ⇒ x² − x = x² − 4 ⇒ x = 4.

Is EF ∥ QR? (converse, NCERT Ex 6.2 Q2)

PE = 4, EQ = 4.5, PF = 8, FR = 9: PE/EQ = 8/9 and PF/FR = 8/9 ⇒ equal ⇒ EF ∥ QR.

PE = 3.9, EQ = 3, PF = 3.6, FR = 2.4: 1.3 ≠ 1.5 ⇒ not parallel.

⚡ Mid-point theorem as a special case

If D is the mid-point of AB and DE ∥ BC, then AD/DB = 1 ⇒ AE/EC = 1 ⇒ E is the mid-point of AC (NCERT Ex 6.2 Q7). Conversely, the line joining mid-points of two sides is parallel to the third side (Q8).

🧪 Try itRuled-paper BPT

  1. Draw any triangle on ruled notebook paper with its base on a printed line.
  2. The printed lines are parallel to the base. Pick one crossing the triangle; measure AD, DB, AE, EC with a ruler.
  3. Work out AD/DB and AE/EC on a calculator: they match (within measuring error). Try another line: still equal!

6.4

Criteria for similarity of triangles

You do not need to check all six conditions. Any one of these criteria is enough to say two triangles are similar.

CriterionWhat you must showTypical clue in the question
AAtwo pairs of equal anglesparallel lines (alternate / corresponding angles), common angle, right angles, vertically opposite angles
SSSAB/PQ = BC/QR = CA/RPall six side lengths given
SAS∠A = ∠P and AB/PQ = AC/PRa common angle and a ratio condition like AD/AB = AE/AC

⚠️ Similar vs congruent criteria

  • There is no ASA, RHS or SSA for similarity. AA already covers ASA.
  • SAS needs the angle between the two proportional sides. An angle elsewhere does not work (NCERT Ex 6.3 Q1 (v) is not similar for this reason).
  • Two triangles with ∠B = ∠E, ∠C = ∠F and AB = 3DE are similar but not congruent (CBSE 2025).

Example (NCERT Ex 6.3 Q2): ΔODC ~ ΔOBA

∠BOC = 125°, ∠CDO = 70°. ∠DOC = 180° − 125° = 55° (linear pair). ∠DCO = 180° − 70° − 55° = 55°. Since ΔODC ~ ΔOBA, ∠OAB = ∠OCD = 55°.

CBSE 2025: find PQ + QR

ΔABC ~ ΔPQR with AB = 6, BC = 4, AC = 8 and PR = 6. Ratio = PR/AC = 6/8 = 3/4. PQ = ¾ × 6 = 4.5, QR = ¾ × 4 = 3. PQ + QR = 7.5 cm.

🧪 Try itSet-square similarity

  1. Take a 30°–60°–90° set square and draw round it. Draw a much bigger triangle with the same angles using a protractor.
  2. Measure the shortest side and the hypotenuse of each. Shortest ÷ hypotenuse is ½ in both.
  3. AA guaranteed it: equal angles ⇒ proportional sides.

6.5

Using similarity: "prove that" questions and heights

Most "prove that" questions follow one recipe: spot two triangles that contain the segments in the result, prove them similar (usually AA), then write the ratio of sides and cross-multiply.

🧭 The 4-step recipe

  1. Look at the result, e.g. CA² = CB·CD. Write it as a ratio: CA/CB = CD/CA.
  2. The letters C, A, B form ΔCAB; C, D, A form ΔCDA. These are the triangles to compare.
  3. Find two equal angles: ∠C is common, and ∠ADC = ∠BAC (given) ⇒ ΔADC ~ ΔBAC (AA).
  4. Write the side ratios in the correct order: CA/CB = CD/CA ⇒ CA² = CB·CD.
Result to rememberWhy
Trapezium: OA/OC = OB/ODΔAOB ~ ΔCOD (alternate angles)
In right ΔABC (∠A = 90°) with AD ⊥ BC: AD² = BD·DC, AB² = BD·BC, AC² = CD·CBΔDBA ~ ΔDAC ~ ΔABC (AA)
Similar triangles: ratio of corresponding medians, altitudes, angle bisectors and perimeters = ratio of sidesNCERT Ex 6.3 Q10, Q16
Two altitudes AD and CE meeting at P give many similar pairs (ΔAEP ~ ΔCDP, ΔABD ~ ΔCBE, …)right angles + common or vertically opposite angles
Shadows at the same time: height/shadow is the same for all objectssun's rays are parallel ⇒ equal angles

CBSE 2025 (OR): AD² = BD·DC ⇒ ∠BAC = 90°

AD ⊥ BC and AD² = BD·DC ⇒ AD/BD = DC/AD, and ∠ADB = ∠CDA = 90° ⇒ ΔADB ~ ΔCDA (SAS).

So ∠BAD = ∠ACD and ∠ABD = ∠CAD. In ΔABC: ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180° ⇒ (∠BAD + ∠CAD) + ∠CAD + ∠BAD = 180° ⇒ 2∠BAC = 180° ⇒ ∠BAC = 90°.

NCERT Ex 6.3 Q8: parallelogram

E is on AD produced; BE meets CD at F. In ΔABE and ΔCFB: ∠A = ∠C (opposite angles of a parallelogram) and ∠AEB = ∠CBF (alternate angles, AE ∥ BC) ⇒ ΔABE ~ ΔCFB (AA).

⚡ Writing tips for proofs

  • Write the reason next to every angle: "(common)", "(alternate angles, AB ∥ DC)", "(each 90°)", "(vertically opposite)".
  • End with "∴ ΔABC ~ ΔPQR (AA similarity criterion)".
  • Mark the figure with the given data; draw it neatly even if it is printed in the paper.

🧪 Try itMeasure your school flagpole

  1. On a sunny day, stand a 1 m ruler upright and measure its shadow (say 0.8 m).
  2. At the same time, measure the flagpole's shadow (say 6.4 m) by pacing or with a tape.
  3. Height = 6.4 × 1/0.8 = 8 m. Try it in the Shadow Lab below with your own numbers.

Mind map, key results and common mistakes

TRIANGLES (SIMILARITY)

🔍 Similar figures

  • Same shape, any size
  • Polygons: angles equal AND sides proportional
  • All circles, squares, equilateral Δs similar
  • Congruent ⇒ similar (ratio 1)

📐 BPT

  • DE ∥ BC ⇒ AD/DB = AE/EC
  • Proof: areas of ΔADE, ΔBDE, ΔDEC
  • Converse: equal ratios ⇒ parallel
  • Mid-point theorem is a special case

✅ Criteria

  • AA (AAA)
  • SSS: all sides in one ratio
  • SAS: included angle + 2 sides
  • Ratio of medians, altitudes, perimeters = ratio of sides
Need to prove…Usually use
a ratio like AD/DB = AE/ECBPT (look for a parallel line)
two lines are parallelconverse of BPT
a product like PA·PB = PC·PD or a square like CA² = CB·CDsimilar triangles (AA), then cross-multiply
a missing lengthsimilar triangles: write the ratio with the correct correspondence

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Writing the correspondence in the wrong order (ΔABC ~ ΔPQR when it should be ΔQRP).
  2. Using AD/AB = AE/EC (mixing part with whole). Keep the same pattern on both sides.
  3. Using SAS with an angle that is not between the two sides.
  4. Writing "SSA" or "RHS" as a similarity criterion.
  5. In the BPT proof, forgetting the construction or the reason "same base, same parallels".
  6. Not writing reasons for equal angles.
  7. Assuming two rectangles or two rhombuses are always similar.
  8. Using area results (areas of similar triangles), which are no longer in the syllabus, instead of side ratios.

CBSE

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Standard, 041) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 38 questions: Section A 20 × 1 (18 MCQs + 2 assertion–reason), B 5 × 2, C 6 × 3, D 4 × 5, E 3 case studies × 4. Unit Geometry (Triangles and Circles) carries 15 marks; the BPT proof appears in almost every paper.

This chapter in recent CBSE papers (set 30/1/1)

YearWhereWhat was asked
2026Q7 (MCQ), Q22 (2, choice), Q34 (5, choice)EF from ΔABC ~ ΔDEF; x from BPT OR sides from similar triangles; prove BPT OR medians of similar triangles
2025Q8 (MCQ), Q24 (2, choice), Q33 (5, choice)similar but not congruent; PQ + QR OR ΔPQS ~ ΔTQR; DF·EF = FB·FA in a parallelogram OR AD² = BD·DC ⇒ ∠A = 90°
2024Q24 (2), Q34 (5, choice)BD bisects ∠B and ∠D ⇒ AB = BC; prove BPT OR 1/x + 1/z = 1/y with three perpendiculars
2023Q22 (2), Q32 (5)XY from XZ ∥ BC; prove BPT (with an application)

✍️ How the CBSE marking scheme splits marks

  • BPT proof (3 marks inside a 5-mark question): ½ given/to prove, ½ figure and construction, 2 for the proof with reasons. The other 2 marks go to an application of BPT.
  • "Prove that" with similarity (3–5 marks): 1 for each correct pair of equal angles with reasons, 1 for stating the similarity correctly, the rest for the ratio and conclusion.
  • Find x (2 marks): 1 for the correct proportion, 1 for the answer.

🏆 Answer-writing tips for full marks

  1. Always draw the figure and write Given, To prove, Construction, Proof for theorems.
  2. Write the correspondence carefully: the equal angles must be in the same position.
  3. Give a reason in brackets for every step.
  4. Use "∴" and end with the exact result asked.

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q34 (B, OR) · 5 marksCM and RN are medians of ΔABC and ΔPQR respectively. If ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, prove that (i) ΔAMC ~ ΔPNR (ii) ΔCMB ~ ΔRNQ.

(ii) BM = AB/2, QN = PQ/2 ⇒ BM/QN = AB/PQ = BC/QR, and ∠B = ∠Q ⇒ ΔCMB ~ ΔRNQ (SAS). 1½

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q24 (B, OR) · 2 marksIn the figure (NCERT Ex 6.3 Q4), QR/QS = QT/PR and ∠1 = ∠2 (∠PQR = ∠PRQ). Show that ΔPQS ~ ΔTQR.

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q33 (A) · 5 marksABCD is a parallelogram. E is a point on BC, and diagonal BD meets AE at F. Prove that DF × EF = FB × FA.

In ΔAFD and ΔEFB: ∠AFD = ∠EFB (vertically opposite angles). 1

AD ∥ BC (opposite sides of a parallelogram), so ∠DAF = ∠BEF (alternate angles, with transversal AE). 1½

∴ ΔAFD ~ ΔEFB (AA). 1

So AF/EF = DF/BF ⇒ DF × EF = FB × FA. 1½

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q33 (B, OR) · 5 marksIn ΔABC, AD ⊥ BC with D on BC, and AD² = BD × DC. Prove that ∠BAC = 90°.

AD² = BD × DC ⇒ AD/BD = DC/AD. 1

In ΔADB and ΔCDA: ∠ADB = ∠CDA = 90° and AD/BD = DC/AD (sides including the right angles) ⇒ ΔADB ~ ΔCDA (SAS). 1½

∴ ∠BAD = ∠ACD and ∠ABD = ∠CAD. 1

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q24 · 2 marksIn quadrilateral ABCD, diagonal BD bisects both ∠B and ∠D. Prove that (i) ΔABD ~ ΔCBD (ii) AB = BC.

(ii) AB/CB = BD/BD = 1 ⇒ AB = BC. 1

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q34 (B, OR) · 5 marksPA, QB and RC are each perpendicular to AC, with PA = x, QB = y and RC = z, where Q is the point where PC and AR meet and B lies on AC. Prove that 1/x + 1/z = 1/y.

AD/AB = 3/8 = AE/AC. 1

AE = 3/8 × 5.6 = 2.1 cm. 1

B2D lies on CA and E on CB with CD = 8 cm, DA = 7 cm, CE = 10 cm, EB = 2 cm, AB = 9 cm and ∠A = ∠CED. Prove ΔCAB ~ ΔCED and find DE.

∠C common, ∠CAB = ∠CED ⇒ ΔCAB ~ ΔCED (AA). 1

CA/CE = AB/ED ⇒ 15/10 = 9/DE ⇒ DE = 6 cm. (Check CB/CD = 12/8 = 1.5 ✓) 1

B3The diagonals of trapezium ABCD (AB ∥ DC) meet at O. If AB = 2CD and AO = 6 cm, find OC.

ΔAOB ~ ΔCOD (AA) ⇒ AO/OC = AB/CD = 2. 1

OC = 3 cm. 1

B4In ΔPQR, ST ∥ QR with S on PQ and T on PR. If PS = 4x − 3, SQ = 3x − 1, PT = 8x − 7 and TR = 5x − 3, find x.

(4x − 3)/(3x − 1) = (8x − 7)/(5x − 3) ⇒ 20x² − 27x + 9 = 24x² − 29x + 7 ⇒ 2x² − x − 1 = 0. 1

(2x + 1)(x − 1) = 0 ⇒ x = 1 (x = −½ makes lengths negative). 1

B5A vertical stick 20 m long casts a 10 m shadow. At the same time a tower casts a 50 m shadow. Find the height of the tower.

Similar triangles (AA): 20/10 = h/50. 1

h = 100 m. 1

Section C: 3 marks each

C1In ΔABC, D and E lie on AB and AC with AD/DB = AE/EC and ∠ADE = ∠ACB. Prove that ΔABC is isosceles.

AD/DB = AE/EC ⇒ DE ∥ BC (converse of BPT). 1

∴ ∠ADE = ∠ABC (corresponding angles). Given ∠ADE = ∠ACB ⇒ ∠ABC = ∠ACB. 1

⇒ AB = AC: ΔABC is isosceles. 1

C2ABCD is a trapezium with AB ∥ DC. A line EF ∥ AB meets AD at E and BC at F. Prove that AE/ED = BF/FC.

Join AC, meeting EF at G. In ΔADC, EG ∥ DC ⇒ AE/ED = AG/GC …(1) 1

In ΔCAB, GF ∥ AB ⇒ CG/GA = CF/FB ⇒ AG/GC = BF/FC …(2) 1

From (1) and (2): AE/ED = BF/FC. 1

C3ΔABC is right-angled at B and BD ⊥ AC. Prove that BD² = AD × DC.

In ΔADB and ΔBDC: ∠ADB = ∠BDC = 90°. ½

∠ABD = 90° − ∠DBC = ∠BCD (angle sum in ΔBDC) ⇒ ΔADB ~ ΔBDC (AA). 1½

AD/BD = BD/DC ⇒ BD² = AD × DC. 1

C4A girl 90 cm tall walks away from the base of a lamp-post at 1.2 m/s. The lamp is 3.6 m above the ground. Find the length of her shadow after 4 seconds.

She is 4.8 m from the post. Let the shadow be s m. The girl and the post make similar triangles with the tip of the shadow (AA). 1

3.6/0.9 = (4.8 + s)/s ⇒ 4s = 4.8 + s 1

s = 1.6 m. 1

D1State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem. Using it, find AB if in ΔABC, DE ∥ BC, AD = 2.4 cm, AE = 3.2 cm and EC = 4.8 cm.

Statement, figure, given, to prove, construction and proof as in section 6.3. 3

D2Through the mid-point M of side CD of parallelogram ABCD, the line BM is drawn, meeting AC at L and AD produced at E. Prove that EL = 2BL.

In ΔBMC and ΔEMD: MC = MD, ∠BMC = ∠EMD (vertically opposite), ∠MBC = ∠MED (alternate, BC ∥ AE) ⇒ ΔBMC ≅ ΔEMD (AAS) ⇒ BC = DE. 2

So AE = AD + DE = 2BC (AD = BC). 1

In ΔAEL and ΔCBL: ∠ALE = ∠CLB (vertically opposite), ∠LAE = ∠LCB (alternate) ⇒ ΔAEL ~ ΔCBL (AA). 1

EL/BL = AE/CB = 2 ⇒ EL = 2BL. 1

E1A street lamp is fixed 6 m above the ground. Aman, 1.5 m tall, stands 9 m from the foot of the pole. (i) Which similarity criterion shows that the two triangles formed (lamp–pole–shadow tip and Aman's head–feet–shadow tip) are similar? (ii) Find the length of Aman's shadow. (iii) If Aman walks 3 m further away, by how much does his shadow grow? OR (iii) How far from the pole is Aman when his shadow is 5 m long?

(i) AA: both have a right angle and share the angle at the shadow tip. 1

(ii) 6/1.5 = (9 + s)/s ⇒ 4s = 9 + s ⇒ s = 3 m. 1

(iii) At 12 m: 4s = 12 + s ⇒ s = 4 m. 1 The shadow grows by 1 m. 1

OR (iii) 4 × 5 = d + 5 1 ⇒ d = 15 m. 1

E2A model of a house is made at a scale of 1 : 50. The triangular front of the model's roof has sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. (i) Is the model's roof similar to the real roof? Name the criterion. (ii) Find the sides of the real roof. (iii) Find the perimeter of the real roof and the ratio of the perimeters. OR (iii) What is the angle of the real roof opposite its longest side? Give a reason.

(i) Yes, every side is multiplied by 50: SSS. 1

(ii) 3 m, 4 m, 5 m. 1

(iii) Perimeter = 12 m 1; ratio of perimeters = 1 : 50 (same as the sides). 1

OR (iii) 6² + 8² = 10², so the model's angle opposite 10 cm is 90°. 1 Similar triangles have equal angles, so the real angle is also 90°. 1

Exercise 6.1

1Fill in the blanks: (i) All circles are ___. (ii) All squares are ___. (iii) All ___ triangles are similar. (iv) Two polygons of the same number of sides are similar if (a) their corresponding angles are ___ and (b) their corresponding sides are ___.
(i) similar (ii) similar (iii) equilateral (iv) (a) equal (b) proportional (in the same ratio).
2Give two different examples of a pair of (i) similar figures (ii) non-similar figures.
(i) Two circles of radii 2 cm and 5 cm; two equilateral triangles of sides 3 cm and 6 cm. (ii) A square and a rectangle 2 × 5; a square and a rhombus with angle 60° (many answers possible).
3State whether the given square and rhombus (equal sides) are similar.
Not similar. The sides are in the same ratio, but the angles of the rhombus are not 90°, so the corresponding angles are not equal.

Exercise 6.2

1In the figures, DE ∥ BC. Find EC in (i) [AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm, AE = 1 cm] and AD in (ii) [DB = 7.2 cm, AE = 1.8 cm, EC = 5.4 cm].
(i) 1.5/3 = 1/EC ⇒ EC = 2 cm. (ii) AD/7.2 = 1.8/5.4 ⇒ AD = 2.4 cm.
2E and F are points on sides PQ and PR of ΔPQR. State whether EF ∥ QR: (i) PE = 3.9, EQ = 3, PF = 3.6, FR = 2.4 (ii) PE = 4, QE = 4.5, PF = 8, RF = 9 (iii) PQ = 1.28, PR = 2.56, PE = 0.18, PF = 0.36 (all in cm)

(i) 3.9/3 = 1.3, 3.6/2.4 = 1.5 ⇒ not parallel.

(ii) 4/4.5 = 8/9 = 8/9 ⇒ EF ∥ QR.

(iii) PE/PQ = 0.18/1.28 = 9/64 and PF/PR = 0.36/2.56 = 9/64 ⇒ EF ∥ QR.

3In the figure, LM ∥ CB and LN ∥ CD (L on AC, M on AB, N on AD). Prove that AM/AB = AN/AD.
In ΔABC, LM ∥ CB ⇒ AM/AB = AL/AC. In ΔADC, LN ∥ CD ⇒ AN/AD = AL/AC. Hence AM/AB = AN/AD.
4In the figure, DE ∥ AC and DF ∥ AE (D on AB, E and F on BC). Prove that BF/FE = BE/EC.
In ΔABC, DE ∥ AC ⇒ BD/DA = BE/EC. In ΔABE, DF ∥ AE ⇒ BD/DA = BF/FE. Hence BF/FE = BE/EC.
5In the figure, DE ∥ OQ and DF ∥ OR (D on PO, E on PQ, F on PR). Show that EF ∥ QR.
In ΔPOQ: PE/EQ = PD/DO. In ΔPOR: PF/FR = PD/DO. So PE/EQ = PF/FR ⇒ EF ∥ QR (converse of BPT in ΔPQR).
6A, B and C are points on OP, OQ and OR such that AB ∥ PQ and AC ∥ PR. Show that BC ∥ QR.
In ΔOPQ: OA/AP = OB/BQ. In ΔOPR: OA/AP = OC/CR. So OB/BQ = OC/CR ⇒ BC ∥ QR (converse of BPT in ΔOQR).
7Using Theorem 6.1, prove that a line drawn through the mid-point of one side of a triangle parallel to another side bisects the third side.
In ΔABC, D is the mid-point of AB and DE ∥ BC. By BPT, AD/DB = AE/EC. AD = DB ⇒ AE/EC = 1 ⇒ AE = EC: E is the mid-point of AC.
8Using Theorem 6.2, prove that the line joining the mid-points of any two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.
D, E mid-points of AB, AC ⇒ AD/DB = 1 = AE/EC. By the converse of BPT, DE ∥ BC.
9ABCD is a trapezium with AB ∥ DC; its diagonals meet at O. Show that AO/BO = CO/DO.
Draw EO ∥ AB (∥ DC) meeting AD at E. In ΔADC, EO ∥ DC ⇒ AE/ED = AO/OC. In ΔDAB, EO ∥ AB ⇒ AE/ED = BO/OD. So AO/OC = BO/OD ⇒ AO/BO = CO/DO.
10The diagonals of quadrilateral ABCD meet at O such that AO/BO = CO/DO. Show that ABCD is a trapezium.
Draw OE ∥ AB meeting AD at E. In ΔABD, AE/ED = BO/OD. Given AO/BO = CO/DO ⇒ AO/OC = BO/OD = AE/ED. In ΔADC this gives EO ∥ DC (converse of BPT). So AB ∥ EO ∥ DC: ABCD is a trapezium.

Exercise 6.3

1State which pairs of triangles in the figure (six pairs, i to vi) are similar. Write the criterion used and the similarity in symbolic form.

(i) Angles 60°, 80°, 40° in both: ΔABC ~ ΔPQR (AAA).

(ii) Sides 2, 2.5, 3 and 4, 5, 6 in ratio 1 : 2: ΔABC ~ ΔQRP (SSS).

(iii) Sides not in one ratio: not similar.

(iv) ∠M = ∠Q = 70° with including sides in ratio 1 : 2: ΔMNL ~ ΔQPR (SAS).

(v) The equal angle is not between the given sides: not similar (cannot use SAS).

(vi) ∠F = 180° − 70° − 80° = 30° and ∠P = 180° − 80° − 30° = 70°: ΔDEF ~ ΔPQR (AAA).

2In the figure, ΔODC ~ ΔOBA, ∠BOC = 125° and ∠CDO = 70°. Find ∠DOC, ∠DCO and ∠OAB.
∠DOC = 180° − 125° = 55°; ∠DCO = 180° − 70° − 55° = 55°; ∠OAB = ∠OCD = 55°.
3Diagonals AC and BD of trapezium ABCD with AB ∥ DC meet at O. Using a similarity criterion, show that OA/OC = OB/OD.
In ΔAOB and ΔCOD: ∠OAB = ∠OCD and ∠OBA = ∠ODC (alternate angles), so ΔAOB ~ ΔCOD (AA) ⇒ OA/OC = OB/OD.
4In the figure, QR/QS = QT/PR and ∠1 = ∠2. Show that ΔPQS ~ ΔTQR.
∠1 = ∠2 ⇒ PQ = PR. So QR/QS = QT/PQ, i.e. QS/QR = PQ/QT. ∠Q is common ⇒ ΔPQS ~ ΔTQR (SAS).
5S and T are points on sides PR and QR of ΔPQR such that ∠P = ∠RTS. Show that ΔRPQ ~ ΔRTS.
∠R is common and ∠RPQ = ∠RTS ⇒ ΔRPQ ~ ΔRTS (AA).
6In the figure, if ΔABE ≅ ΔACD, show that ΔADE ~ ΔABC.
By CPCT, AB = AC and AE = AD ⇒ AD/AB = AE/AC. ∠A is common ⇒ ΔADE ~ ΔABC (SAS).
7Altitudes AD and CE of ΔABC meet at P. Show that (i) ΔAEP ~ ΔCDP (ii) ΔABD ~ ΔCBE (iii) ΔAEP ~ ΔADB (iv) ΔPDC ~ ΔBEC.

(i) ∠AEP = ∠CDP = 90°, ∠APE = ∠CPD (vertically opposite) ⇒ AA.

(ii) ∠ADB = ∠CEB = 90°, ∠B common ⇒ AA.

(iii) ∠AEP = ∠ADB = 90°, ∠A common ⇒ AA.

(iv) ∠PDC = ∠BEC = 90°, ∠C common ⇒ AA.

8E is a point on side AD produced of parallelogram ABCD, and BE meets CD at F. Show that ΔABE ~ ΔCFB.
∠A = ∠C (opposite angles of a parallelogram) and ∠AEB = ∠CBF (alternate angles, AE ∥ BC) ⇒ ΔABE ~ ΔCFB (AA).
9ABC and AMP are two right triangles, right-angled at B and M. Prove that (i) ΔABC ~ ΔAMP (ii) CA/PA = BC/MP.
(i) ∠ABC = ∠AMP = 90° and ∠A common ⇒ ΔABC ~ ΔAMP (AA). (ii) Hence CA/PA = BC/MP.
10CD and GH are bisectors of ∠ACB and ∠EGF, with D on AB and H on FE of ΔABC and ΔEFG. If ΔABC ~ ΔFEG, show that (i) CD/GH = AC/FG (ii) ΔDCB ~ ΔHGE (iii) ΔDCA ~ ΔHGF.
∠C = ∠G ⇒ ∠ACD = ∠FGH and ∠DCB = ∠HGE (halves). Also ∠A = ∠F and ∠B = ∠E. (iii) ΔDCA ~ ΔHGF (AA). (ii) ΔDCB ~ ΔHGE (AA). (i) From (iii), CD/GH = AC/FG.
11E is a point on side CB produced of isosceles ΔABC with AB = AC. If AD ⊥ BC and EF ⊥ AC, prove that ΔABD ~ ΔECF.
AB = AC ⇒ ∠ABD = ∠ECF. ∠ADB = ∠EFC = 90° ⇒ ΔABD ~ ΔECF (AA).
12Sides AB and BC and median AD of ΔABC are respectively proportional to sides PQ and QR and median PM of ΔPQR. Show that ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.
AB/PQ = BC/QR = AD/PM, and BD = BC/2, QM = QR/2 ⇒ AB/PQ = BD/QM = AD/PM ⇒ ΔABD ~ ΔPQM (SSS) ⇒ ∠B = ∠Q. With AB/PQ = BC/QR ⇒ ΔABC ~ ΔPQR (SAS).
13D is a point on side BC of ΔABC such that ∠ADC = ∠BAC. Show that CA² = CB·CD.
In ΔADC and ΔBAC: ∠ADC = ∠BAC and ∠C is common ⇒ ΔADC ~ ΔBAC (AA) ⇒ CA/CB = CD/CA ⇒ CA² = CB·CD.
14Sides AB and AC and median AD of ΔABC are respectively proportional to sides PQ and PR and median PM of ΔPQR. Show that ΔABC ~ ΔPQR.
Produce AD to E with DE = AD and PM to N with MN = PM. ABEC and PQNR are parallelograms, so BE = AC and QN = PR. Then AB/PQ = BE/QN = AE/PN ⇒ ΔABE ~ ΔPQN (SSS) ⇒ ∠BAE = ∠QPN. Similarly ΔACE ~ ΔPRN ⇒ ∠CAE = ∠RPN. Adding, ∠A = ∠P. With AB/PQ = AC/PR ⇒ ΔABC ~ ΔPQR (SAS).
15A vertical pole of length 6 m casts a shadow 4 m long on the ground, and at the same time a tower casts a shadow 28 m long. Find the height of the tower.
The triangles are similar (AA: right angles and equal sun angles). 6/4 = h/28 ⇒ h = 42 m.
16If AD and PM are medians of triangles ABC and PQR, where ΔABC ~ ΔPQR, prove that AB/PQ = AD/PM.
∠B = ∠Q and AB/PQ = BC/QR = (BC/2)/(QR/2) = BD/QM ⇒ ΔABD ~ ΔPQM (SAS) ⇒ AB/PQ = AD/PM.
