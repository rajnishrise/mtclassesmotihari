📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Angle of elevation: between the horizontal and the line of sight when you look up. Angle of depression: when you look down.
  • The angle of depression from P to Q equals the angle of elevation from Q to P (alternate angles).
  • Most-used ratio: tan (height and horizontal distance). Use sin or cos only when a slant length (rope, ladder, string, slide) is involved.
  • Values: tan 30° = 1/√3, tan 45° = 1, tan 60° = √3; sin 30° = ½, sin 60° = √3/2. Take √3 ≈ 1.732.
  • Syllabus: problems use at most two right triangles, and angles of 30°, 45° and 60° only.
  • Most-asked in CBSE: a 5-mark long question (2023, 2024) or a 4-mark case study (2025), plus a 1-mark MCQ (kite, tower). Unit Trigonometry (Ch 8 + Ch 9) carries 12 marks.
9.1

Line of sight, elevation and depression

The line of sight is the line from the observer's eye to the object. The horizontal is the level line through the eye. The angle between them is the angle of elevation (looking up) or the angle of depression (looking down).

⬆️

Elevation

Object above the eye: the top of a tower, a kite, a bird, a balloon.

⬇️

Depression

Object below the eye: a car seen from a building, a boat seen from a lighthouse.

🔁

They are equal

Depression from P to Q = elevation from Q to P, because the two horizontals are parallel.

⚠️ The most common diagram mistake

The angle of depression is between the horizontal line at the top and the line of sight. It is not the angle between the tower and the line of sight. Always draw a dotted horizontal line at the observer's eye first.

🧪 Try itMake a clinometer

  1. Tape a straw along the straight edge of a protractor. Hang a thread with a small stone from the centre hole.
  2. Look at the top of your school flagpole through the straw. The thread hangs straight down.
  3. The angle between the thread and the 90° mark is the angle of elevation. You will use it in the next section to find the height!
9.2

One right triangle: the basic case

The tower, the ground and the line of sight make a right triangle. Choose the ratio that links the known side with the side you want.

You knowYou wantUse
horizontal distanceheighttan θ = height/distance
heighthorizontal distancetan θ (or cot θ)
slant length (rope, string, ladder, slide)heightsin θ = height/slant
slant lengthhorizontal distancecos θ = distance/slant

NCERT Ex 9.1 Q4: tower from 30 m away at 30°

tan 30° = h/30 ⇒ h = 30/√3 = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m.

NCERT Ex 9.1 Q5: kite at 60 m, string at 60°

The string is the slant side: sin 60° = 60/L ⇒ L = 60 × 2/√3 = 40√3 m ≈ 69.28 m.

CBSE 2025 MCQ: kite 150 m high, string at 30°

sin 30° = 150/L ⇒ L = 300 m.

CBSE 2026 MCQ: 30 m tower, car 10√3 m from its foot

tan θ = 30/(10√3) = √3 ⇒ θ = 60°.

NCERT Ex 9.1 Q2: broken tree

The top touches the ground 8 m from the foot at 30°. Standing part = 8 tan 30° = 8/√3; broken part = 8/cos 30° = 16/√3.

Height of the tree = 24/√3 = 8√3 m.

🤔 Real life: why tan rules this chapter

Surveyors measure the horizontal distance on the ground (easy) and the angle with a theodolite (easy). The height is the hard part, and tan connects exactly these two: height = distance × tan θ. The Great Trigonometrical Survey of India (started 1802) used this to measure Mount Everest from over 150 km away.

🧪 Try itMeasure your school building

  1. Stand 20 m from the building (count 25 normal steps of 80 cm). Use your clinometer to read the angle to the roof, say 35°.
  2. tan 35° ≈ 0.70, so height above your eye ≈ 20 × 0.70 = 14 m.
  3. Add your eye height (about 1.4 m): the building is about 15.4 m tall. Check with the Height Finder lab below.

9.3

Two right triangles: the 5-mark patterns

Long questions give two angles, so there are two right triangles sharing a side (usually the height h or a distance x). Write one tan equation for each triangle and solve the pair. Almost every board question is one of these six patterns.

#PatternShared sideExample
1Observer walks towards the tower (same side)hNCERT Q6, Q11
2Two poles on opposite sides of a roadhNCERT Q10
3From the top of a building: elevation of a tower's top and depression of its foothorizontal distanceNCERT Q12
4Object on top of object (pole on tower, statue on pedestal)horizontal distanceNCERT Q7, Q8; CBSE 2024
5Two ships or cars seen from one top (depressions)heightNCERT Q13, Q15
6Moving object at a fixed height (balloon, aeroplane)heightNCERT Q14

Pattern 1: walking towards the tower

NCERT Ex 9.1 Q11: TV tower across a canal

h = x tan 60° = x√3 and h = (x + 20) tan 30° = (x + 20)/√3.

3x = x + 20 ⇒ x = 10. Height h = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m, width of the canal 10 m.

⚡ Shortcut for 30° and 60° (same side)

When the angle changes from 30° to 60°, the distance walked equals the slant line from the second point, and h = (√3/2) × distance walked. Check: 20 × √3/2 = 10√3 ✓. Use this to check answers, but show full working in the exam.

Pattern 4: object on top of object

CBSE 2024 (5 marks): 6 m pole on a tower

From ΔPBC: tan 45° = h/x ⇒ x = h. 1 From ΔPBD: tan 60° = (h + 6)/x ⇒ h + 6 = h√3. 2

h = 6/(√3 − 1) = 3(√3 + 1) ≈ 8.19 m. Distance of P from the foot = 8.19 m. 2

Pattern 5: ships from a lighthouse

NCERT Ex 9.1 Q15: how much longer for the car?

Depression 30°: car at h√3. Six seconds later, 60°: car at h/√3. It covered h√3 − h/√3 = 2h/√3 in 6 s.

Remaining distance h/√3 is half of that ⇒ 3 seconds.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Subtract the observer's eye height from the object's height first (NCERT Q6, Q14: 30 − 1.5 = 28.5 m).
  • In pattern 2 the two distances add to the road width; in pattern 1 they differ by the distance walked.
  • Rationalise: 6/(√3 − 1) × (√3 + 1)/(√3 + 1) = 3(√3 + 1).

NCERT Ex 9.1 Q12: from the top of a 7 m building

Depression of the tower's foot 45° ⇒ horizontal distance = 7 m. Elevation of its top 60° ⇒ extra height = 7 tan 60° = 7√3.

Tower = 7(√3 + 1) m ≈ 19.12 m.

CBSE 2023 OR (5 marks): balloon of radius r

A spherical balloon of radius r subtends 60° at the eye; its centre is at elevation 45°. The tangent line, the radius and the eye-to-centre line make a right triangle with angle 30° at the eye.

Eye-to-centre = r/sin 30° = 2r. Height of the centre = 2r sin 45° = √2 r.

🏆 Answer-writing tips

  1. One clear figure is worth a mark: use a ruler, mark the right angle.
  2. Write "In right ΔABC, tan 60° = AB/BC".
  3. Box the final answer with units: "Height of the tower = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m".
  4. If the question says "use √3 = 1.73", use exactly that value.

🧪 Try itShadow method for a tree

  1. On a sunny day, measure a 1 m stick's shadow, say 1.73 m. Then tan(sun's angle) = 1/1.73 ⇒ the sun is at 30°.
  2. Measure a tree's shadow, say 12 m. Tree height = 12 × tan 30° = 12/1.73 ≈ 6.9 m.
  3. This is Chapter 6 (similar triangles) and Chapter 9 together!

Mind map, formulas and common mistakes

HEIGHTS AND DISTANCES

👀 Angles

  • Elevation: look up from horizontal
  • Depression: look down from horizontal
  • Depression = elevation (alternate)

🗼 One triangle

  • h = d tan θ
  • d = h / tan θ
  • slant: h = L sin θ, d = L cos θ
  • subtract eye height

📐 Two triangles

  • same side: distances differ
  • opposite sides: distances add
  • on top of: heights add
  • one tan equation per triangle
Question says…Do this
"angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point …"tan θ = height/distance
"string / rope / ladder / slide of length …"sin θ = height/length (cos for the base)
"angle of depression of a car / ship from the top"copy the angle to the ground (alternate angles)
"walks towards the tower, angle changes from 30° to 60°"pattern 1: h = x√3 and h = (x + d)/√3
"on opposite sides of a road of width w"pattern 2: x + y = w
"a man of height 1.5 m"use (height − 1.5) in the triangle

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Measuring the angle of depression from the vertical tower instead of the horizontal.
  2. Forgetting to subtract the observer's height.
  3. Using sin when only height and horizontal distance are involved (use tan).
  4. tan 30° and tan 60° swapped: tan 60° = √3 is the big one.
  5. Not rationalising 1/(√3 − 1).
  6. Adding distances in a same-side problem (they should be subtracted).
  7. No figure, or a figure without the right angle marked: loses a mark.
  8. Rounding √3 too early; keep surds till the last line.

CBSE

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Standard, 041) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 38 questions. Unit Trigonometry carries 12 marks, and this chapter usually supplies the 5-mark long answer (Section D) or the 4-mark case study (Section E), plus an MCQ.

This chapter in recent CBSE papers (set 30/1/1)

YearWhereWhat was asked
2026Q11 (MCQ), Q37 (case study)angle of elevation of a tower from a car; a radio-tower case study
2025Q17 (MCQ), Q38 (case study, 4)length of a kite string; lighthouse seen from two heights
2024Q35 (5)pole on a tower: elevation 60°, depression 45°
2023Q33 (5, choice)two towers and the wire between their tops OR a balloon of radius r

✍️ How the CBSE marking scheme splits marks

  • 5-mark question: 1 for the correct figure, 1 to 2 for the two equations, 1 to 2 for solving, ½ to 1 for each final answer.
  • Case study (1 + 1 + 2): the first two parts are one-line answers from one triangle; the 2-mark part combines both triangles.
  • Answers left in surd form (10√3 m) get full marks unless a decimal is asked.

🏆 How to score full marks

  1. Draw the figure first, even if the question has one.
  2. Write "In right ΔABC" before each ratio.
  3. Rationalise and simplify; then give the decimal value.
  4. Answer every part asked (height and distance).

CBSE board questions (2023–2026)

Heights and distances questions from CBSE Class 10 Maths Standard papers (set 30/1/1) with full solutions and step marks. Wording lightly shortened; MCQ options are not repeated.

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q11 · 1 markThe angle of elevation of the top of a 30 m high tower from a car at a distance of 10√3 m from its foot is

tan θ = 30/(10√3) = √3 ⇒ θ = 60°.

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q17 · 1 markA kite is flying at a height of 150 m. The string makes an angle of 30° with the ground. Assuming no slack, the length of the string is

sin 30° = 150/L ⇒ L = 150 × 2 = 300 m.

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q38 · Case study (1 + 1 + 2)A lighthouse CE stands on level ground. From a point A on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top C is 60°. From a point B, 40 m vertically above A, the angle of elevation of C is 45°. D is the point on CE at the level of B, and CD = h. (i) Find BD in terms of h. (ii) Find BC in terms of h. (iii) Find the height of the lighthouse. OR (iii) If AC = 100 m, find the distance AE.

(i) In ΔBDC, tan 45° = h/BD ⇒ BD = h. 1

(ii) BC = h/sin 45° = √2 h. 1

(iii) AE = BD = h and CE = h + 40. In ΔAEC: tan 60° = (h + 40)/h ⇒ h√3 = h + 40 ⇒ h = 40/(√3 − 1) = 20(√3 + 1). 1 CE = 20(√3 + 1) + 40 ≈ 94.64 m. 1

OR (iii) cos 60° = AE/AC ⇒ AE = 100 × ½ = 50 m. 2

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q35 · 5 marksA 6 m long pole stands on the top of a tower. From a point P on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the pole is 60°, and the angle of depression of P from the top of the tower is 45°. Find the height of the tower and the distance of P from the foot of the tower. (Use √3 = 1.73)

Figure (see section 9.3): tower BC = h, pole CD = 6, PB = x. 1

Depression 45° ⇒ elevation of C from P is 45°: tan 45° = h/x ⇒ x = h. 1

tan 60° = (h + 6)/x ⇒ √3 h = h + 6 1 ⇒ h = 6/(√3 − 1) = 3(√3 + 1) = 3 × 2.73 = 8.19 m. 1

Distance of P from the foot = x = 8.19 m. 1

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q33 (A) · 5 marksThe angle of elevation of the top of a 24 m high tower AB from the foot D of another tower CD is 60°. The angle of elevation of the top C of CD from the foot B of AB is 30°. Find the distance between the towers and the height of the other tower. Also find the length of the wire joining their tops.

Let BD = x. In ΔABD: tan 60° = 24/x ⇒ x = 24/√3 = 8√3 m. 2

In ΔCDB: tan 30° = CD/x ⇒ CD = 8√3/√3 = 8 m. 1½

Wire: horizontal gap 8√3, vertical gap 24 − 8 = 16 ⇒ length = √(192 + 256) = √448 = 8√7 m. 1½

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q33 (B, OR) · 5 marksA spherical balloon of radius r subtends an angle of 60° at the eye of an observer. If the angle of elevation of its centre is 45°, find the height of the centre of the balloon.

Let O be the eye and C the centre. A tangent from O touches the balloon at T, with CT ⊥ OT and ∠COT = 30° (half of 60°). 1½

sin 30° = CT/OC = r/OC ⇒ OC = 2r. 1½

Height of the centre = OC sin 45° = 2r/√2 = √2 r. 2

📈 Pattern spotted

Two-triangle problems with 45° and 60° came in 2024 and 2025, and both needed h = k/(√3 − 1), then rationalising. The kite and tower MCQs are one-line tan or sin questions. Master patterns 1, 3 and 4 from section 9.3.

(a) 5√3 m. 10 sin 60°.

Section A: Assertion–Reason (1 mark each)

(a) Both A and R are true and R explains A. (b) Both true, R does not explain A. (c) A true, R false. (d) A false, R true.

AR1A: If a tower of height h casts a shadow of length h√3, the sun's elevation is 30°. R: tan θ = height/length of shadow.
(a). tan θ = 1/√3.
AR2A: The angle of elevation of the top of a tower increases as the observer walks away from it. R: tan θ = h/d decreases as d increases.
(d). The angle decreases as you walk away.

Section B: 2 marks each

B1The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point 15 m from its foot is 60°. Find the height of the tower.

tan 60° = h/15 1 ⇒ h = 15√3 m ≈ 25.98 m. 1

B2An observer 1.5 m tall is 28.5 m away from a 30 m tall tower. Find the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from his eyes.

Height above the eye = 30 − 1.5 = 28.5 m. tan θ = 28.5/28.5 = 1 1 ⇒ θ = 45°. 1

B3A ladder leaning against a wall makes 60° with the ground. Its foot is 2.5 m from the wall. Find the length of the ladder.

cos 60° = 2.5/L 1 ⇒ L = 5 m. 1

B4A 12 m tall tree casts a shadow 4√3 m long. Find the sun's angle of elevation.

tan θ = 12/(4√3) = √3 1 ⇒ θ = 60°. 1

B5From a point on a bridge 3 m above a river, the angles of depression of the two banks on opposite sides are 30° and 45°. Find the width of the river.

Distances: 3/tan 30° = 3√3 and 3/tan 45° = 3. 1

Width = 3(√3 + 1) m ≈ 8.2 m. 1

Section C: 3 marks each

C1The shadow of a tower is 40 m longer when the sun's altitude is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower.

Shadows: h/tan 30° = h√3 and h/tan 60° = h/√3. 1

h√3 − h/√3 = 40 ⇒ 2h/√3 = 40 1

h = 20√3 m ≈ 34.64 m. 1

C2From the top of a 60 m building, the angles of depression of the top and the bottom of a tower are 30° and 60°. Find the height of the tower.

Bottom at 60°: distance = 60/√3 = 20√3 m. 1

Top at 30°: drop from the building's top to the tower's top = 20√3 × tan 30° = 20 m. 1

Tower = 60 − 20 = 40 m. 1

C3From an aeroplane 300 m above a river, the angles of depression of two points on opposite banks directly in line are 30° and 45°. Find the width of the river.

Distances from the point below the plane: 300√3 and 300. 2

Width = 300(√3 + 1) ≈ 819.6 m. 1

C4The angle of elevation of a jet from a point on the ground is 60°. After 15 seconds of flight it is 30°. The jet flies at a constant height of 1500√3 m. Find its speed.

Horizontal distances: 1500√3/√3 = 1500 m and 1500√3 × √3 = 4500 m. 1½

Distance flown = 3000 m in 15 s ⇒ speed = 200 m/s = 720 km/h. 1½

Section D: 5 marks

D1From a point P on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and top of a flagstaff fixed on top of a 10 m building are 30° and 45°. Find the length of the flagstaff and the distance of P from the building. (√3 = 1.732)

Figure with building AB = 10 m, flagstaff BC = f, PA = x. 1

tan 30° = 10/x ⇒ x = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m. 1½

tan 45° = (10 + f)/x ⇒ 10 + f = 10√3 1½ ⇒ f = 10(√3 − 1) ≈ 7.32 m. 1

D2A man on the deck of a ship 10 m above the water sees the top of a cliff at an elevation of 45° and the base of the cliff at a depression of 30°. Find the distance of the cliff from the ship and the height of the cliff.

Figure: deck point P, 10 m above water; cliff CB. 1

Depression 30° of the base: tan 30° = 10/d ⇒ d = 10√3 ≈ 17.32 m. 1½

Elevation 45° of the top: height above deck = d tan 45° = 10√3. 1½

Cliff = 10 + 10√3 = 10(√3 + 1) ≈ 27.32 m. 1

Section E: Case study (1 + 1 + 2)

E1Riya looks out of her window, 15 m above the ground. She sees the top of a mobile tower at an elevation of 30° and its foot at a depression of 45°. (i) How far is the tower from her building? (ii) How high is the tower's top above her window? (iii) Find the height of the tower. OR (iii) Find the distance from her window to the top of the tower.

(ii) 15 tan 30° = 5√3 m ≈ 8.66 m. 1

(iii) Height = 15 + 5√3 1 ≈ 23.66 m. 1

(ii) Find its height above the ground. (iii) The wind changes and the string makes 30° with the horizontal. How much more string is needed to keep the kite at the same height? OR (iii) Find the horizontal distance of the kite from Rohan when the string is at 60°.

(i) 120 sin 60° = 60√3 m ≈ 103.92 m. 1

(ii) ≈ 104.92 m. 1

1A circus artist is climbing a 20 m long rope, tightly stretched and tied from the top of a vertical pole to the ground. Find the height of the pole if the rope makes an angle of 30° with the ground.
sin 30° = h/20 ⇒ h = 10 m.
2A tree breaks due to a storm and the broken part bends so that the top touches the ground, making an angle of 30° with it. The distance between the foot of the tree and the point where the top touches the ground is 8 m. Find the height of the tree.
Standing part = 8 tan 30° = 8/√3; broken part = 8/cos 30° = 16/√3. Height = 24/√3 = 8√3 m ≈ 13.86 m.
3A contractor plans two slides: for children below 5 years, the top at 1.5 m and inclined at 30° to the ground; for elder children, the top at 3 m and inclined at 60°. What should be the length of each slide?
Small slide: 1.5/sin 30° = 3 m. Big slide: 3/sin 60° = 6/√3 = 2√3 m ≈ 3.46 m.
4The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground, 30 m from its foot, is 30°. Find the height of the tower.
h = 30 tan 30° = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m.
5A kite is flying at a height of 60 m. Its string is tied to a point on the ground and makes 60° with the ground. Find the length of the string, assuming no slack.
L = 60/sin 60° = 120/√3 = 40√3 m ≈ 69.28 m.
6A 1.5 m tall boy stands some distance from a 30 m tall building. The angle of elevation from his eyes to the top increases from 30° to 60° as he walks towards the building. Find the distance he walked.
Height above the eye = 28.5 m. Distances: 28.5√3 and 28.5/√3. Walked = 28.5(√3 − 1/√3) = 28.5 × 2/√3 = 19√3 m ≈ 32.91 m.
7From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and the top of a transmission tower fixed at the top of a 20 m high building are 45° and 60°. Find the height of the tower.
45° ⇒ distance = 20 m. Total height = 20 tan 60° = 20√3. Tower = 20(√3 − 1) m ≈ 14.64 m.
8A statue 1.6 m tall stands on a pedestal. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the statue is 60° and that of the top of the pedestal is 45°. Find the height of the pedestal.
45° ⇒ distance = h. tan 60° = (h + 1.6)/h ⇒ h√3 = h + 1.6 ⇒ h = 1.6/(√3 − 1) = 0.8(√3 + 1) m ≈ 2.19 m.
9The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of a tower is 30° and that of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. If the tower is 50 m high, find the height of the building.
Distance = 50/tan 60° = 50/√3. Building = (50/√3) tan 30° = 50/3 = 16⅔ m.
10Two poles of equal heights stand opposite each other on either side of an 80 m wide road. From a point between them on the road, the angles of elevation of their tops are 60° and 30°. Find the height of the poles and the distances of the point from the poles.
h = x√3 = (80 − x)/√3 ⇒ 3x = 80 − x ⇒ x = 20. Height = 20√3 m ≈ 34.64 m; distances 20 m and 60 m.
11A TV tower stands on one bank of a canal. From a point on the other bank directly opposite, the angle of elevation of its top is 60°. From another point 20 m away from this point on the same line, it is 30°. Find the height of the tower and the width of the canal.
h = x√3 = (x + 20)/√3 ⇒ x = 10. Height = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m, width = 10 m.
12From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Determine the height of the tower.
Distance = 7 m (45°). Extra height = 7 tan 60° = 7√3. Tower = 7(√3 + 1) m ≈ 19.12 m.
13From the top of a 75 m high lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships are 30° and 45°. If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side of the lighthouse, find the distance between the two ships.
Distances 75/tan 45° = 75 and 75/tan 30° = 75√3. Gap = 75(√3 − 1) m ≈ 54.9 m.
14A 1.2 m tall girl spots a balloon moving with the wind in a horizontal line at a height of 88.2 m from the ground. The angle of elevation from her eyes is 60°; after some time it becomes 30°. Find the distance travelled by the balloon.
Height above the eye = 87 m. Distances 87/√3 = 29√3 and 87√3. Travelled = 58√3 m ≈ 100.46 m.
15A straight highway leads to the foot of a tower. A man at the top of the tower sees a car at an angle of depression of 30°, approaching the foot with uniform speed. Six seconds later, the angle of depression is 60°. Find the time taken by the car to reach the foot of the tower from this point.
Distances h√3 and h/√3. Covered 2h/√3 in 6 s; the remaining h/√3 is half of that ⇒ 3 seconds.
