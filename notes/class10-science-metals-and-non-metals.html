📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Metals are lustrous, hard, malleable, ductile, sonorous and good conductors. Non-metals are dull, brittle, poor conductors (graphite is an exception) and can be solid, liquid (bromine) or gas.
  • Metal + oxygen → metal oxide (basic; Al₂O₃ and ZnO are amphoteric). Non-metal + oxygen → acidic or neutral oxide.
  • Metal + water → oxide/hydroxide + H₂ (K, Na violently; Ca, Mg less; Al, Fe, Zn only with steam). Metal + dilute acid → salt + H₂ (not with HNO₃).
  • Reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au. A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from its salt solution.
  • Ionic compounds form by transfer of electrons (Na⁺Cl⁻). They are hard solids with high melting points, soluble in water, and conduct when molten or dissolved.
  • Extraction: concentrate the ore → (top metals) electrolysis; (middle) roasting/calcination then reduction with carbon; (low) heating alone → electrolytic refining.
  • Corrosion: rusting needs air + water. Prevent by painting, oiling, galvanising, chrome plating, anodising, alloying.
  • CBSE weightage: unit Chemical Substances (Ch 1–4) carries 25 marks. This chapter is a favourite for case studies (refining, extraction) and A–R questions.
3.1

Physical properties of metals and non-metals

About 118 elements are known; more than 90 of them are metals. Metals and non-metals can be told apart by their physical properties (Activities 3.1 to 3.6), but there are many exceptions, so chemical properties are more reliable.

🤔 Why are electric wires coated with PVC or rubber?

Copper and aluminium conduct electricity very well, so a bare wire can give a shock. PVC and rubber are insulators. Copper and aluminium are also used to make cooking pans because they conduct heat well.

  3. Make a table. Which non-metal surprised you? (Pencil "lead" is graphite: it conducts electricity!)
3.2

Chemical properties of metals and the reactivity series

3.2.1 Metals burnt in air

Metal + Oxygen → Metal oxide · 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO (black) · 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃
  • Metal oxides are basic. But Al₂O₃ and ZnO react with both acids and bases: they are amphoteric oxides.
    Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O · Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ (sodium aluminate) + H₂O
  • Soluble metal oxides dissolve in water to form alkalis: Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH; K₂O + H₂O → 2KOH.
  • Different metals react at different rates: K and Na catch fire in open air, so they are kept under kerosene. Mg, Al, Zn, Pb are covered with a thin protective oxide layer. Iron does not burn but iron filings burn in a flame. Copper forms black CuO on heating. Silver and gold do not react with oxygen even at high temperature.
  • Anodising: aluminium is made the anode in dilute H₂SO₄; oxygen at the anode thickens its protective oxide layer, which can also be dyed.

3.2.2 Metals with water

K, Naviolent with cold water; exothermic, H₂ catches fire2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ + heat

3.2.3 Metals with acids

Metal + dilute acid → Salt + Hydrogen · Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

Rate of bubbling with dilute HCl (Activity 3.11): Mg > Al > Zn > Fe. Copper does not react. Hydrogen is not evolved when a metal reacts with nitric acid, because HNO₃ is a strong oxidising agent that oxidises H₂ to water and is itself reduced to nitrogen oxides. Only Mg and Mn give H₂ with very dilute HNO₃.

🔑 Aqua regia ("royal water")

A freshly prepared mixture of concentrated HCl and concentrated HNO₃ in the ratio 3 : 1. It can dissolve gold and platinum, although neither acid can do so alone. It is highly corrosive and fuming.

3.2.4 Metals with solutions of other metal salts

Metal A + Salt solution of B → Salt solution of A + Metal B (if A is more reactive)

Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu (blue → green). Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag (solution turns blue). But Cu + FeSO₄ → no reaction.

3.2.5 The reactivity series

⚠️ Watch out

  • Lead is above hydrogen but reacts very slowly with dilute HCl (PbCl₂ coats it). In the board exam, list Pb as above H.
  • Zinc + nitric acid gives no H₂ (CBSE 2024 A–R): the reason is that HNO₃ oxidises H₂ to water.
  • Lead is a poor conductor of electricity compared with other metals (CBSE 2026 MCQ).

🧪 Try itCoin and vinegar race

  1. Put a small iron nail, a piece of aluminium foil (scratched) and a copper wire into three cups of vinegar (a weak acid).
  2. Watch closely with a magnifying glass for tiny bubbles over 30 minutes.
  3. Rank them by bubbling and compare with the reactivity series. (Al and Fe bubble slowly; Cu does not.)

3.3

How do metals and non-metals react? Ionic compounds

Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar) have a completely filled outer shell and hardly react. Other atoms react to get this stable octet (or duplet for He). Metals have 1 to 3 outer electrons and lose them to form positive ions (cations). Non-metals have 5 to 7 outer electrons and gain electrons to form negative ions (anions).

ElementAtomic numberElectrons in K, L, M, NIon formed
Na112, 8, 1Na⁺ (2, 8)
Mg122, 8, 2Mg²⁺ (2, 8)
Al132, 8, 3Al³⁺ (2, 8)
K192, 8, 8, 1K⁺ (2, 8, 8)
O82, 6O²⁻ (2, 8)
Cl172, 8, 7Cl⁻ (2, 8, 8)

3.3.1 Properties of ionic compounds

🧱

Hard, brittle solids

Strong attraction between ions. They break into pieces under pressure.

🌡️

High melting and boiling points

A lot of energy is needed to overcome the strong inter-ionic attraction (NaCl melts at 1074 K). "Weak electrostatic forces" is wrong (CBSE 2025).

💧

Solubility

Generally soluble in water; insoluble in kerosene and petrol.

⚡

Conduction

Do not conduct as solids (ions are fixed). Conduct when molten or dissolved in water, because the ions can move.

🧪 Try itSalt vs sugar conductivity (with a teacher)

  1. In the school lab, make a circuit with a battery, a bulb and two carbon rods (from old cells).
  2. Dip the rods into dry salt, then salt solution, then sugar solution.
  3. Only salt solution lights the bulb. Why does dry salt not conduct? (Its ions cannot move.)

3.4

Occurrence and extraction of metals

The Earth's crust is the main source of metals; sea water also has soluble salts like NaCl and MgCl₂. Elements or compounds occurring naturally in the crust are minerals. Minerals from which a metal can be extracted profitably are ores. Ores contain earthy impurities like soil and sand, called gangue.

3.4.1 Extraction depends on reactivity

3.4.2 Enrichment of ores

Removing gangue from the ore (by physical or chemical methods based on differences in physical or chemical properties) is called concentration or enrichment.

3.4.3 Metals low in the series: heating alone

Cinnabar (HgS): 2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂, then 2HgO → 2Hg + O₂ (heat).
Copper glance (Cu₂S): 2Cu₂S + 3O₂ → 2Cu₂O + 2SO₂, then 2Cu₂O + Cu₂S → 6Cu + SO₂ (CBSE 2026 case).

3.4.4 Metals in the middle: roast or calcine, then reduce

Reduction with carbon (coke): ZnO + C → Zn + CO. Highly reactive metals like Na, Ca, Al can also act as reducing agents. The thermit reaction is very exothermic, so the iron comes out molten:

3.4.5 Metals at the top: electrolysis

Na, K, Ca, Mg and Al have more affinity for oxygen than carbon has, so carbon cannot reduce their oxides. They are obtained by electrolytic reduction of their molten chlorides (Na, Ca, Mg: CBSE 2025) or oxide (Al from molten Al₂O₃). The metal is deposited at the cathode and chlorine is set free at the anode.
At cathode: Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na · At anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻

3.5

Corrosion and its prevention; alloys

Silver turns black (silver sulphide) with sulphur in air. Copper gets a green coat of basic copper carbonate with moist CO₂. Iron gets a brown flaky coat called rust. This slow eating away of metals is corrosion.

3.5.1 Prevention of corrosion

  • Painting, oiling, greasing: keeps out air and moisture.
  • Galvanisation: coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc. Even if the zinc coat breaks, the iron is protected because zinc is more reactive and corrodes first.
  • Chrome plating, anodising (for Al).
  • Making alloys: stainless steel (iron + nickel + chromium) does not rust.

Alloys

An alloy is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal. It is made by melting the main metal and dissolving the others in it. Pure gold (24 carat) is very soft, so it is alloyed with silver or copper (22 carat) for jewellery. If one metal is mercury, the alloy is an amalgam.

🤔 The iron pillar of Delhi

Built about 1600 years ago near the Qutub Minar, this 8 m tall iron pillar weighing about 6 tonnes has not rusted. It shows the advanced iron technology of ancient Indian craftsmen.

Map

Mind map, key terms and common mistakes

METALS AND NON-METALS

🔩 Metals

  • Lustrous, malleable, ductile, sonorous
  • Good conductors (Ag, Cu best)
  • Basic or amphoteric oxides
  • Lose electrons → cations
  • Reactivity: K Na Ca Mg Al Zn Fe Pb [H] Cu Hg Ag Au

🧪 Non-metals

  • Brittle, dull, poor conductors
  • Exceptions: graphite conducts, iodine shines, diamond hardest
  • Acidic or neutral oxides
  • Gain electrons → anions
  • Do not give H₂ with acids

⛏️ Metallurgy

  • Ore → concentration (remove gangue)
  • Low: heat alone (Hg, Cu)
  • Middle: roast / calcine, then reduce (Zn, Fe)
  • Top: electrolysis (Na, Mg, Al)
  • Refining, corrosion, alloys

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Saying all metals are solid. Mercury is a liquid metal; bromine is a liquid non-metal.
  2. Writing that magnesium floats in cold water. Calcium and magnesium (in hot water) float because H₂ bubbles stick to them; sodium and potassium catch fire.
  3. Writing H₂ for metal + nitric acid. HNO₃ is an oxidising agent: it oxidises H₂ to water (only Mg and Mn give H₂ with very dilute HNO₃).
  4. Confusing roasting (air, sulphides) with calcination (limited air, carbonates).
  5. Saying carbon can reduce Al₂O₃. Na, K, Ca, Mg, Al have more affinity for oxygen than carbon, so electrolysis is used.
  6. In refining, putting impure metal at the cathode. Impure = anode, pure = cathode.
  7. Saying ionic compounds conduct as solids. They conduct only when molten or in solution.
  8. Thinking galvanised iron rusts once scratched. Zinc is more reactive, so it corrodes first and still protects the iron.

CBSE

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Science (086) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 39 questions: Section A has 20 one-mark questions (16 MCQs and 4 assertion–reason), Section B 6 two-mark, Section C 7 three-mark, Section D 3 five-mark, and Section E 3 four-mark case studies. Unit Chemical Substances: Nature and Behaviour (Chapters 1–4) carries 25 marks, and Metals and Non-metals is asked every year, often as a 4-mark case study.

✍️ How marks are given

  • Equations: ½ for the correct products, ½ for balancing. Unbalanced equations lose marks.
  • Electron-dot structures: ½ for each atom's dots, ½ for the arrow showing transfer, ½ for the ions with charges.
  • Refining diagram: label anode, cathode, electrolyte and anode mud; each label carries ½.
  • Reasons: one clear chemistry reason (reactivity, affinity for oxygen, oxide layer) earns the mark; a vague answer does not.

🏆 Answer-writing tips

  1. Learn the reactivity series with "Please Stop Calling Me A Zebra, I Like Her Call Me Smart Goat": K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe (iron), Pb (lead), H, Cu, Hg (mercury), Ag (silver), Au (gold).
  2. For amphoteric oxides, always write two equations: one with HCl and one with NaOH.
  3. In extraction answers, name the method (roasting, calcination, reduction, electrolysis) before the equation.

CBSE board questions (2023–2026)

Metals and Non-metals questions from CBSE Class 10 Science papers (set 31/1/1) with solutions. Wording and options lightly reworded; 2023 questions are reconstructed from the official marking scheme.

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q21 · 1 markWhich of these metals is a poor conductor of heat? (A) silver (B) copper (C) lead (D) aluminium

(C) lead. Lead and mercury are comparatively poor conductors of heat; silver and copper are the best.

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q23 · 1 markA metal reacts with water less violently than sodium; it starts floating because bubbles of hydrogen stick to its surface. The metal is (A) potassium (B) calcium (C) iron (D) zinc

(B) calcium. Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂. The heat is not enough to ignite the hydrogen, and its bubbles make calcium float.

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q25 · 2 marksWhat are amphoteric oxides? Classify ZnO, Na₂O and CO₂ as acidic, basic or amphoteric.

Metal oxides that react with both acids and bases to give salt and water are amphoteric. ½

ZnO: amphoteric ½; Na₂O: basic ½; CO₂: acidic ½

CBSE 2026 · 31/1/1 · Q28 · 4 marks (case)Case on extraction of metals. (i) Why are carbonate and sulphide ores converted into oxides before reduction? (i) It is easier to obtain a metal from its oxide than from its sulphide or carbonate. 1

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q2 · 1 markAluminium and magnesium burn in air to form (A) AlO and MgO (B) Al₂O₃ and MgO (C) Al₂O₃ and Mg₂O (D) AlO₂ and MgO₂

(B). 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃ and 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (Al³⁺ with O²⁻; Mg²⁺ with O²⁻).

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q5 · 1 markWhich statement about ionic compounds is NOT true? (A) They are solids and hard. (B) They have high melting points. (C) They have weak electrostatic forces between ions. (D) They conduct electricity in the molten state.

(C). The forces between oppositely charged ions are strong, which is why melting points are high.

CBSE 2025 · 31/1/1 · Q6 · 1 markWhich pair of metals is obtained by electrolytic reduction of their molten chlorides? (A) Fe and Zn (B) Ca and Mg (C) Cu and Ag (D) Zn and Pb

(B) Ca and Mg. Metals high in the series (Na, Ca, Mg) are obtained by electrolysis of molten chlorides.

Section B: 2 marks each

B1Give reasons: (a) Sodium is kept in kerosene. (b) Aluminium does not react with water even though it is a reactive metal.

(a) Sodium reacts vigorously with oxygen and moisture and catches fire; kerosene keeps air and water away. 1

(b) A thin, tough layer of aluminium oxide on its surface stops further reaction (it reacts only with steam, slowly). 1

B2Show the formation of MgCl₂ by transfer of electrons. Name the ions present.

Mg (2, 8, 2) loses 2 electrons → Mg²⁺; each of two Cl atoms (2, 8, 7) gains 1 electron → 2Cl⁻. Mg: → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻; 2Cl + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻. 1

Ions: magnesium ion Mg²⁺ (cation) and chloride ion Cl⁻ (anion); formula MgCl₂. 1

B3What is roasting? Write the equation for roasting of zinc blende.

Heating a sulphide ore strongly in excess air to convert it into an oxide. 1

2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂ 1

B4What is an alloy? Why is 24-carat gold mixed with copper or silver for making jewellery?

An alloy is a homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or of a metal and a non-metal. 1

Pure (24 carat) gold is very soft; adding Cu or Ag makes 22-carat gold, which is harder and keeps its shape. 1

(b) Calcination: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂ 1

C4What is galvanisation? Why does galvanised iron not rust even if the zinc layer is scratched? Name one other method of preventing rusting.

Galvanisation is coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc. 1

Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it reacts (corrodes) first and protects the iron below. 1

Others: painting, oiling or greasing, chrome plating, alloying (stainless steel). 1

D1(a) Why are highly reactive metals extracted by electrolysis? (b) Write the electrode reactions for electrolysis of molten NaCl. (c) Draw and label the electrolytic refining of copper. (d) What is anode mud?

(a) Na, K, Ca, Mg, Al have more affinity for oxygen than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce their oxides; electrolysis of molten chlorides or oxide is used. 1

(b) Cathode: Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na; Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻ 1

(c) Tank of acidified CuSO₄; thick impure Cu block as anode (+); thin pure Cu strip as cathode (−); battery; anode mud below the anode (see the refining diagram in 3.4.6). 2

(d) The insoluble impurities (such as silver and gold) that collect below the anode. 1

D2Compare metals and non-metals on (a) reaction with oxygen (b) reaction with dilute acids (c) type of ions formed (d) conduction (e) nature of oxides. Give an example for each.

(a) Both form oxides: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; C + O₂ → CO₂. 1

(b) Reactive metals give H₂ (Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂); non-metals do not react with dilute acids. 1

(c) Metals lose electrons → cations (Na⁺); non-metals gain electrons → anions (Cl⁻). 1

(d) Metals conduct heat and electricity; non-metals do not (except graphite). 1

(e) Metal oxides are basic or amphoteric (Na₂O, Al₂O₃); non-metal oxides are acidic or neutral (SO₂, CO). 1

All textbook questions answered

In-text questions

1Give an example of a metal which (i) is a liquid at room temperature (ii) can be easily cut with a knife (iii) is the best conductor of heat (iv) is a poor conductor of heat.
(i) Mercury (ii) Sodium (or lithium, potassium) (iii) Silver (then copper) (iv) Lead (or mercury).
2Explain the meanings of malleable and ductile.
Malleable: can be beaten into thin sheets (gold, silver, aluminium foil). Ductile: can be drawn into thin wires (copper, aluminium wires; 1 g of gold can make a 2 km wire).
3Why is sodium kept immersed in kerosene oil?
Sodium reacts so vigorously with oxygen and moisture in air that it catches fire. Kerosene does not react with it and keeps air and water away.
4Write equations for the reactions of (i) iron with steam (ii) calcium and potassium with water.
(i) 3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) → Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)
(ii) Ca(s) + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + H₂(g)
2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g) + heat
5Samples of four metals A, B, C and D were added to solutions of iron(II) sulphate, copper(II) sulphate, zinc sulphate and silver nitrate. A displaced copper only; B displaced iron (and showed no reaction with zinc sulphate); C displaced silver only; D displaced nothing. (i) Which is the most reactive metal? (ii) What happens if B is added to copper(II) sulphate? (iii) Arrange A, B, C, D in decreasing order of reactivity.
(i) B is the most reactive: it displaces iron, so it is above iron (and therefore above copper and silver). (ii) B displaces copper: the blue colour fades and a reddish-brown copper layer forms on B. (iii) B > A > C > D.
6Which gas is produced when dilute HCl is added to a reactive metal? Write the reaction of iron with dilute H₂SO₄.
Hydrogen gas. Fe(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)
7What would you observe when zinc is added to a solution of iron(II) sulphate? Write the reaction.
The pale green colour of FeSO₄ fades and a dark grey deposit of iron forms, because zinc is more reactive than iron: Zn(s) + FeSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Fe(s)
8(i) Write the electron-dot structures for sodium, oxygen and magnesium. (ii) Show the formation of Na₂O and MgO by transfer of electrons. (iii) What are the ions present?
(i) Na has 1 dot (2, 8, 1), O has 6 dots (2, 6), Mg has 2 dots (2, 8, 2). Try the Ionic Bond Builder above to see the dots move.
(ii) 2Na· + ·Ö· → 2Na⁺ + [:Ö:]²⁻ → Na₂O (each Na gives one electron to O).
Mg: + ·Ö· → Mg²⁺ + [:Ö:]²⁻ → MgO (Mg gives two electrons).
(iii) Na₂O: Na⁺ and O²⁻; MgO: Mg²⁺ and O²⁻.
9Why do ionic compounds have high melting points?
The oppositely charged ions are held by strong electrostatic forces of attraction, so a large amount of energy is needed to break them apart.
10Define (i) mineral (ii) ore (iii) gangue.
(i) Mineral: an element or compound occurring naturally in the earth's crust. (ii) Ore: a mineral from which a metal can be extracted profitably. (iii) Gangue: soil, sand and other impurities present in an ore.
11Name two metals which are found in nature in the free state.
Gold and platinum (silver and copper are also sometimes found free).
12What chemical process is used for obtaining a metal from its oxide?
Reduction, e.g. with carbon: ZnO + C → Zn + CO, or with a more reactive metal: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃.
13Metallic oxides of zinc, magnesium and copper were heated with the metals: which mixtures react? (Zn with MgO, CuO; Mg with ZnO, CuO; Cu with ZnO, MgO)
A metal displaces another only if it is more reactive. Mg reacts with ZnO and CuO. Zn reacts with CuO only. Cu reacts with neither. (Mg > Zn > Cu.)
14Which metals do not corrode easily?
Gold and platinum (also silver to a large extent); they are least reactive.
15What are alloys?
Homogeneous mixtures of two or more metals, or a metal and a non-metal, e.g. brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), solder (Pb + Sn), steel (Fe + C).

Exercise questions

1Which pair will give a displacement reaction? (a) NaCl solution and copper (b) MgCl₂ solution and aluminium (c) FeSO₄ solution and silver (d) AgNO₃ solution and copper
(d). Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag; copper is more reactive than silver.
2Which method is suitable for preventing an iron frying pan from rusting? (a) applying grease (b) applying paint (c) applying a coating of zinc (d) all of the above
(c) a coating of zinc. Grease and paint would burn off or contaminate food when the pan is heated.
3An element reacts with oxygen to give a compound with a high melting point. This compound is also soluble in water. The element is likely to be (a) calcium (b) carbon (c) silicon (d) iron
(a) calcium. CaO is ionic (high m.p.) and dissolves in water to form Ca(OH)₂.
4Food cans are coated with tin and not with zinc because (a) zinc is costlier than tin (b) zinc has a higher melting point than tin (c) zinc is more reactive than tin (d) zinc is less reactive than tin
(c). Zinc could react with acids in food and form harmful compounds; tin is less reactive.
5You are given a hammer, a battery, a bulb, wires and a switch. (a) How could you use them to distinguish between samples of metals and non-metals? (b) Assess the usefulness of these tests.
(a) Hammer: beat the sample. A metal flattens into a sheet (malleable); a non-metal breaks into pieces (brittle). Circuit: put the sample between the two wires; if the bulb glows, it conducts and is a metal.
(b) The tests are quick and work for most samples, but there are exceptions: graphite (non-metal) conducts, and sodium is soft. So use more than one test.
6What are amphoteric oxides? Give two examples.
Oxides that react with both acids and bases to give salt and water. Examples: aluminium oxide (Al₂O₃) and zinc oxide (ZnO).
ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O; ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O
7Name two metals that will displace hydrogen from dilute acids, and two that will not.
Will: zinc, magnesium (also iron, aluminium). Will not: copper, silver (also gold). Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series displace it.
8In the electrolytic refining of a metal M, what would you take as the anode, the cathode and the electrolyte?
Anode: a thick block of impure metal M. Cathode: a thin strip of pure metal M. Electrolyte: a solution of a soluble salt of M (for copper, acidified CuSO₄).
9Pratyush took sulphur powder on a spatula, heated it and collected the gas evolved. (a) What will be the action of the gas on (i) dry litmus paper (ii) moist litmus paper? (b) Write the balanced equation.
(a) (i) No effect on dry litmus. (ii) Moist blue litmus turns red, because SO₂ dissolves in water to form sulphurous acid (SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃).
(b) S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g)
10State two ways to prevent the rusting of iron.
(1) Painting, oiling or greasing, to keep out air and moisture. (2) Galvanising (coating with zinc). Others: chrome plating, making alloys like stainless steel.
11What type of oxides are formed when non-metals combine with oxygen?
Acidic oxides (SO₂, CO₂, P₂O₅) or neutral oxides (CO, N₂O, H₂O).
12Give reasons: (a) Platinum, gold and silver are used to make jewellery. (b) Sodium, potassium and lithium are stored under oil. (c) Aluminium is a highly reactive metal, yet it is used for cooking utensils. (d) Carbonate and sulphide ores are usually converted into oxides during extraction.
(a) They are lustrous, malleable and least reactive, so they do not tarnish or corrode.
(b) They react vigorously with oxygen and moisture and catch fire; oil keeps air away.
(c) A protective layer of Al₂O₃ stops further reaction; Al is also light and a good conductor of heat.
(d) It is easier to reduce a metal oxide than its sulphide or carbonate.
13You must have seen tarnished copper vessels being cleaned with lemon or tamarind juice. Explain why these sour substances are effective.
Tarnished copper has a green coat of basic copper carbonate, CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂, which is basic. The acids in lemon (citric) and tamarind (tartaric) neutralise and dissolve it, so the shiny copper shows again.
14Differentiate between metals and non-metals on the basis of their chemical properties.
MetalsNon-metals
Form basic or amphoteric oxides (Na₂O, ZnO)Form acidic or neutral oxides (SO₂, CO)
Reactive ones give H₂ with dilute acidsDo not give H₂ with dilute acids
Lose electrons, form cations (electropositive)Gain electrons, form anions (electronegative)
Form ionic chlorides (NaCl)Form covalent chlorides (CCl₄)
Act as reducing agentsMostly act as oxidising agents
15A man went door to door posing as a goldsmith. He dipped old gold ornaments in a solution; they shone like new but lost a lot of weight. Which solution did he use?
Aqua regia, a freshly prepared mixture of concentrated HCl and concentrated HNO₃ in the ratio 3 : 1. It dissolves gold, so the outer layer dissolved, the ornaments looked shiny, and their weight dropped.
16Give reasons why copper is used to make hot water tanks and not steel (an alloy of iron).
Copper does not react with cold water, hot water or steam, so the tank does not corrode. Iron in steel reacts with steam (3Fe + 4H₂O → Fe₃O₄ + 4H₂) and rusts with hot water and air. Copper is also an excellent conductor of heat.
