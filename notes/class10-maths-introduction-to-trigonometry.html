📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Ratios (right angle at B, angle A): sin A = opp/hyp, cos A = adj/hyp, tan A = opp/adj; cosec, sec, cot are their reciprocals.
  • tan A = sin A / cos A and cot A = cos A / sin A.
  • Value table: sin 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° = √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2 = 0, ½, 1/√2, √3/2, 1. Cos is the same list backwards.
  • Identities: sin²A + cos²A = 1, 1 + tan²A = sec²A, 1 + cot²A = cosec²A.
  • Removed from syllabus: trigonometric ratios of complementary angles (sin(90° − A) = cos A type questions).
  • Most-asked in CBSE: a "prove that" identity (3 marks, every year), "given tan θ (or cot θ), find…" (2 marks), table-value evaluation (2 marks) and quick MCQs. Unit Trigonometry (Ch 8 + Ch 9) carries 12 marks.
8.1

What is trigonometry?

Trigonometry comes from Greek: tri (three) + gon (sides) + metron (measure). It studies the relation between the angles and sides of a right triangle. In Class 10 we use only acute angles (0° to 90°).

🗼

Heights

Find the height of the Qutub Minar from the ground, without climbing it, using one angle and one distance.

🪜

Ladders and ramps

A wheelchair ramp must not be steeper than about 5°. Engineers check this with tan.

🛰️

Navigation

Ships, aeroplanes and GPS use trigonometry to fix positions and distances.

🤔 History corner

Indian mathematicians Aryabhata (476 CE) and later Bhaskara used jya (half-chord) tables. Jya became jiba in Arabic, then sinus in Latin, and finally sine. So "sin" has Indian roots!

🧪 Try itYour shadow clock

  1. Stand in the sun at 9 am, 12 noon and 4 pm. Ask a friend to measure your shadow.
  2. Divide your height by the shadow length each time. This number is tan of the sun's angle.
  3. At noon the number is biggest: the sun is highest. You have just used trigonometry!
8.2

The six trigonometric ratios

In ΔABC, right-angled at B, look at the acute angle A. The side facing A is the opposite side (BC), the side next to A is the adjacent side (AB), and the longest side, facing the right angle, is the hypotenuse (AC).

RatioDefinitionIn ΔABCReciprocal of
sin Aopposite / hypotenuseBC/ACcosec A
cos Aadjacent / hypotenuseAB/ACsec A
tan Aopposite / adjacentBC/ABcot A
cosec Ahypotenuse / oppositeAC/BCsin A
sec Ahypotenuse / adjacentAC/ABcos A
cot Aadjacent / oppositeAB/BCtan A
tan A = sin A / cos A  ·  cot A = cos A / sin A

⚡ Memory trick: "Pandit Badri Prasad Har Har Bole"

P/H = sin, B/H = cos, P/B = tan (P = perpendicular, B = base, H = hypotenuse). Read the first letters: P B P on top, H H B below. Then flip each one for cosec, sec, cot.

⚠️ Watch out

  • "sin A" is one symbol. It is not sin × A, and "sin" alone means nothing.
  • (sin A)² is written sin²A, not sin A².
  • sin A and cos A are always ≤ 1 (hypotenuse is the longest side). sec A, cosec A are always ≥ 1. tan A can be any positive number.

🧪 Try itSame angle, any size

  1. Draw an angle of 35° with a protractor. Drop perpendiculars from three points on one arm at different distances.
  2. Measure the opposite side and the hypotenuse of each triangle.
  3. Divide opposite by hypotenuse. All three give about 0.57: that is sin 35°!

8.2.1

Given one ratio, find all the others

The most common 2-mark question. Follow three steps: (1) draw a right triangle and write the given ratio as sides, using k; (2) find the third side by Pythagoras; (3) read off any ratio.

NCERT Ex 8.1 Q3: sin A = 3/4. Find cos A and tan A.

Opposite = 3k, hypotenuse = 4k ⇒ adjacent = √(16k² − 9k²) = √7 k.

cos A = √7/4, tan A = 3/√7.

NCERT Ex 8.1 Q4: 15 cot A = 8. Find sin A and sec A.

cot A = 8/15: adjacent 8k, opposite 15k ⇒ hypotenuse = √(64 + 225) k = 17k.

sin A = 15/17, sec A = 17/8.

CBSE 2026: tan θ = 24/7. Find sin θ + cos θ.

Opposite 24k, adjacent 7k ⇒ hypotenuse √(576 + 49) k = 25k. 1

sin θ + cos θ = 24/25 + 7/25 = 31/25. 1

NCERT Ex 8.1 Q10: PR + QR = 25 cm, PQ = 5 cm (right angle at Q). Find sin P, cos P, tan P.

PR² − QR² = PQ² = 25 ⇒ (PR − QR)(PR + QR) = 25 ⇒ PR − QR = 1. So PR = 13, QR = 12.

sin P = QR/PR = 12/13, cos P = 5/13, tan P = 12/5.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Always write the sides as 3k and 4k (or say "let the sides be 3 and 4 units"); examiners give the first mark for this step.
  • sin A = 4/3 is impossible: opposite can never be longer than the hypotenuse (NCERT Ex 8.1 Q11(v)).

🧪 Try itRamp check

  1. A ramp rises 1 m over a horizontal distance of 12 m. Its slope is tan θ = 1/12.
  2. Hypotenuse = √(1 + 144) = √145 ≈ 12.04 m, so sin θ ≈ 0.083.
  3. Look at a ramp at your school or a station and estimate its tan θ from steps or tiles.

8.3

Ratios of 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°

These five angles appear in almost every board paper. Their values come from two famous triangles: half a square (45°) and half an equilateral triangle (30° and 60°).

∠A0°30°45°60°90°
sin A01/21/√2√3/21
cos A1√3/21/√21/20
tan A01/√31√3not defined
cosec Anot defined2√22/√31
sec A12/√3√22not defined
cot Anot defined√311/√30

⚡ Make the table in 30 seconds

  1. Write 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 under 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°.
  2. Divide each by 4 and take the square root: √0, √(1/4), √(2/4), √(3/4), √1 = 0, ½, 1/√2, √3/2, 1. That is the sin row.
  3. Write the sin row backwards: that is the cos row.
  4. tan = sin ÷ cos. Then flip sin, cos, tan for cosec, sec, cot.

🤔 Why is tan 90° not defined?

As A gets close to 90°, the adjacent side AB shrinks towards 0 while the opposite side stays big. tan A = BC/AB becomes huge. At exactly 90° we would divide by 0, which is not allowed. Similarly cosec 0° and cot 0° are not defined.

🧪 Try itFold a 45° triangle

  1. Take a square sheet of paper of side 10 cm and fold it along a diagonal.
  2. Measure the diagonal: about 14.1 cm = 10√2 cm.
  3. So sin 45° = 10/14.1 ≈ 0.707 = 1/√2 ✓. Now fold an equilateral triangle in half and check sin 30° = ½.
8.3.1

Evaluating expressions and finding angles

Substitute the values from the table, simplify fractions, and rationalise the denominator if needed. To find an angle, make the ratio equal to a table value.

NCERT Ex 8.2 Q1(i): sin 60° cos 30° + sin 30° cos 60°

= (√3/2)(√3/2) + (½)(½) = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1.

CBSE 2024: 2√2 cos 45° sin 30° + 2√3 cos 30°

= 2√2 × (1/√2) × ½ + 2√3 × (√3/2) 1 = 1 + 3 = 4. 1

NCERT Ex 8.2 Q1(v): (5cos²60° + 4sec²30° − tan²45°)/(sin²30° + cos²30°)

Numerator = 5(¼) + 4(4/3) − 1 = 5/4 + 16/3 − 1 = (15 + 64 − 12)/12 = 67/12. Denominator = 1.

Answer 67/12.

Finding angles: NCERT Ex 8.2 Q3

tan(A + B) = √3 and tan(A − B) = 1/√3, with 0° < A + B ≤ 90° and A > B.

A + B = 60°, A − B = 30° ⇒ A = 45°, B = 15°.

CBSE 2025: 7 + 4 sin θ = 9. Find θ.

4 sin θ = 2 ⇒ sin θ = ½ ⇒ θ = 30°.

CBSE 2024: If cos(α + β) = 0, find cos((α + β)/2).

cos(α + β) = 0 = cos 90° ⇒ α + β = 90° ⇒ cos 45° = 1/√2.

⚠️ Watch out

  • sin(A + B) ≠ sin A + sin B. Check: sin(30° + 60°) = 1, but sin 30° + sin 60° = (1 + √3)/2.
  • sin 2A ≠ 2 sin A (true only for A = 0°).
  • cos²30° means (cos 30°)² = 3/4, not cos(900°).

🧪 Try itCalculator detective

  1. Put your calculator in DEG mode. Find sin 30°, cos 60°, tan 45°. Do they match the table?
  2. Find sin 1°, sin 10°, sin 50°, sin 89°. Notice sin keeps increasing.
  3. Now find cos for the same angles: cos keeps decreasing. (NCERT Ex 8.2 Q4(ii), (iii))
8.4

Trigonometric identities

An identity is an equation that is true for every value of the angle (where both sides are defined). In ΔABC right-angled at B, Pythagoras says AB² + BC² = AC². Divide by AC², AB² or BC²:

Divide AB² + BC² = AC² byIdentityValid forUseful forms
AC²cos²A + sin²A = 10° ≤ A ≤ 90°sin²A = 1 − cos²A, cos²A = 1 − sin²A
AB²1 + tan²A = sec²A0° ≤ A < 90°sec²A − tan²A = 1 ⇒ (sec A − tan A)(sec A + tan A) = 1
BC²cot²A + 1 = cosec²A0° < A ≤ 90°cosec²A − cot²A = 1 ⇒ (cosec A − cot A)(cosec A + cot A) = 1

CBSE 2024 MCQ: sec θ − tan θ = m. Find sec θ + tan θ.

(sec θ − tan θ)(sec θ + tan θ) = sec²θ − tan²θ = 1 ⇒ sec θ + tan θ = 1/m.

NCERT Ex 8.3 Q1: write sin A, sec A, tan A in terms of cot A

cosec²A = 1 + cot²A ⇒ sin A = 1/√(1 + cot²A). tan A = 1/cot A. sec²A = 1 + tan²A = (cot²A + 1)/cot²A ⇒ sec A = √(1 + cot²A)/cot A.

CBSE 2026: x = h + a cos θ, y = k + b sin θ. Prove ((x − h)/a)² + ((y − k)/b)² = 1.

(x − h)/a = cos θ and (y − k)/b = sin θ. 1

LHS = cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 = RHS. 2

🧪 Try itCheck the identity with numbers

  1. Pick θ = 30°: sin²30° + cos²30° = ¼ + ¾ = 1 ✓.
  2. Pick θ = 60°: 1 + tan²60° = 1 + 3 = 4, sec²60° = 2² = 4 ✓.
  3. Pick any angle on your calculator, like 23°, and test all three. They always work!
8.4.1

How to prove identities: 8 tricks

A 3-mark "prove that" comes in every CBSE paper. Start from the more complicated side and work towards the other side. Never move terms across the "=" sign.

#TrickWhen to use
1Convert everything to sin and cosWhen you are stuck: it always works
2Take the LCM of fractionsTwo fractions added or subtracted
3Use (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²(1 + sin)(1 − sin) = cos², (sec − tan)(sec + tan) = 1
4Multiply by the conjugateSquare roots like √((1 + sin)/(1 − sin))
5Replace 1 by sec² − tan² (or cosec² − cot²)Expressions like (tan + sec − 1)/(tan − sec + 1)
6Use a³ − b³ = (a − b)(a² + ab + b²)Cubes of tan, cot, sin, cos
7Expand squares(sin A + cosec A)² + (cos A + sec A)²
8Work on both sides separatelyWhen both sides look complicated: reduce each to the same expression

CBSE 2024 and 2025 (3 marks): prove tan θ/(1 − cot θ) + cot θ/(1 − tan θ) = 1 + sec θ cosec θ

Write t = tan θ: LHS = t/(1 − 1/t) + (1/t)/(1 − t) = t²/(t − 1) − 1/(t(t − 1)) 1

= (t³ − 1)/(t(t − 1)) = (t − 1)(t² + t + 1)/(t(t − 1)) = (t² + t + 1)/t = t + 1 + 1/t 1

= 1 + (sin²θ + cos²θ)/(sin θ cos θ) = 1 + 1/(sin θ cos θ) = 1 + sec θ cosec θ. 1

CBSE 2023 (3 marks): prove (tan θ + sec θ − 1)/(tan θ − sec θ + 1) = (1 + sin θ)/cos θ

Numerator: replace 1 by sec²θ − tan²θ: tan θ + sec θ − (sec θ + tan θ)(sec θ − tan θ) = (sec θ + tan θ)(1 − sec θ + tan θ). 1½

The bracket (1 − sec θ + tan θ) is the denominator, so LHS = sec θ + tan θ 1 = 1/cos θ + sin θ/cos θ = (1 + sin θ)/cos θ. ½

NCERT Ex 8.3 Q4(vi): prove √((1 + sin A)/(1 − sin A)) = sec A + tan A

Multiply inside by (1 + sin A)/(1 + sin A): √((1 + sin A)²/(1 − sin²A)) = (1 + sin A)/cos A = sec A + tan A.

⚠️ Common proof mistakes

  • Cross-multiplying both sides at the start: this assumes what you have to prove. Work on one side.
  • Writing √(a² + b²) = a + b. It is wrong.
  • Cancelling terms in a sum: (sin A + cos A)/sin A is not cos A.
  • Not writing "= RHS. Hence proved." at the end. Examiners like a clear finish.

  2. Compare the number of lines. Which way was faster?

INTRODUCTION TO TRIGONOMETRY

🔺 Ratios

  • sin = P/H, cos = B/H, tan = P/B
  • cosec = 1/sin, sec = 1/cos, cot = 1/tan
  • tan = sin/cos
  • Depend only on the angle

⭐ Table

  • sin: 0, ½, 1/√2, √3/2, 1
  • cos: the same backwards
  • tan: 0, 1/√3, 1, √3, —
  • tan 90°, cot 0° not defined

🔑 Identities

  • sin² + cos² = 1
  • 1 + tan² = sec²
  • 1 + cot² = cosec²
  • Prove: one side → other side
Question says…Do this
"If tan θ = p/q, find…"draw the triangle, sides pk and qk, Pythagoras for the third side
"Evaluate" with 30°, 45°, 60°substitute table values, simplify, rationalise
"Find θ" or "find A and B"make each ratio equal to a table value
"sec θ − tan θ = m"use (sec − tan)(sec + tan) = 1
"Prove that"start from the harder side; convert to sin, cos if stuck
"Express in terms of …"use the identity linking the two ratios

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Mixing up opposite and adjacent: always look from the given angle.
  2. Writing sin(A + B) = sin A + sin B.
  3. Thinking sin²A means sin(A²).
  4. Using cos 60° = √3/2 (it is ½). Make the table every time.
  5. Writing tan 90° = ∞ or 0 instead of "not defined".
  6. Cross-multiplying in a "prove that" question.
  7. Not rationalising answers like 1/(2 + √3).
  8. Using sin(90° − A) = cos A: complementary angles are no longer in the syllabus, so such questions are not asked.

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Standard, 041) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 38 questions. Unit Trigonometry (Introduction to Trigonometry + Some Applications) carries 12 marks. From this chapter expect about 2 MCQs, one 2-mark question and one 3-mark proof.

✍️ How the CBSE marking scheme splits marks

  • "Given one ratio" (2 marks): 1 for the third side (Pythagoras), 1 for the final value.
  • Evaluate (2 marks): 1 for correct substitution of table values, 1 for the answer.
  • Prove that (3 marks): 1 for the first correct conversion (to sin, cos or LCM), 1 for the main step (identity or factorisation), 1 for reaching the RHS.

🏆 Answer-writing tips for full marks

  1. Write "LHS =" and work only on that side; finish with "= RHS. Hence proved."
  2. Name the identity you use, for example "(using sec²θ − tan²θ = 1)".
  3. In "given one ratio" questions, draw the small triangle with the sides marked.
  4. Write values as fractions, not decimals: √3/2, not 0.866.

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q27 (A) · 3 marksProve that tan θ/(1 − cot θ) + cot θ/(1 − tan θ) = 1 + sec θ cosec θ.

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q30 · 3 marksProve that (tan θ + sec θ − 1)/(tan θ − sec θ + 1) = (1 + sin θ)/cos θ.

Section C: 3 marks each

C1Prove that (sin A − cos A + 1)/(sin A + cos A − 1) = 1/(sec A − tan A).

Divide numerator and denominator by cos A: LHS = (tan A + sec A − 1)/(tan A − sec A + 1). 1

Replace 1 in the numerator by sec²A − tan²A: numerator = (sec A + tan A)(1 − sec A + tan A), so LHS = sec A + tan A. 1

sec A + tan A = (sec²A − tan²A)/(sec A − tan A) = 1/(sec A − tan A). 1

C2Prove that sec A (1 − sin A)(sec A + tan A) = 1.

sec A + tan A = (1 + sin A)/cos A. 1

LHS = (1/cos A)(1 − sin A)(1 + sin A)/cos A = (1 − sin²A)/cos²A 1

= cos²A/cos²A = 1. 1

C3If tan θ + sin θ = m and tan θ − sin θ = n, show that m² − n² = 4√(mn).

m² − n² = (m + n)(m − n) = (2 tan θ)(2 sin θ) = 4 tan θ sin θ. 1

mn = tan²θ − sin²θ = sin²θ(1/cos²θ − 1) = sin²θ × tan²θ. 1

4√(mn) = 4 tan θ sin θ = m² − n². Hence proved. 1

C4Prove that (cot A − cos A)/(cot A + cos A) = (cosec A − 1)/(cosec A + 1).

cot A = cos A/sin A. Take cos A common: LHS = cos A(1/sin A − 1)/(cos A(1/sin A + 1)) 2

= (cosec A − 1)/(cosec A + 1). 1

D1If sec θ + tan θ = p, prove that sin θ = (p² − 1)/(p² + 1). Hence find sin θ when p = 2.

(sec θ + tan θ)(sec θ − tan θ) = 1 ⇒ sec θ − tan θ = 1/p. 1

Adding and subtracting: sec θ = (p² + 1)/(2p), tan θ = (p² − 1)/(2p). 2

p = 2: sin θ = 3/5. 1

D2Prove that (1 + 1/tan²A)(1 + 1/cot²A) = 1/(sin²A − sin⁴A). Verify the result for A = 30°.

Exercise 8.1

1In ΔABC, right-angled at B, AB = 24 cm, BC = 7 cm. Determine: (i) sin A, cos A (ii) sin C, cos C
AC = √(576 + 49) = 25 cm. (i) sin A = 7/25, cos A = 24/25. (ii) sin C = 24/25, cos C = 7/25.
2In the given figure (ΔPQR right-angled at Q, PQ = 12 cm, PR = 13 cm), find tan P − cot R.
QR = √(169 − 144) = 5 cm. tan P = QR/PQ = 5/12, cot R = QR/PQ = 5/12. tan P − cot R = 0.
3If sin A = 3/4, calculate cos A and tan A.
Adjacent = √(16 − 9) = √7. cos A = √7/4, tan A = 3/√7.
4Given 15 cot A = 8, find sin A and sec A.
cot A = 8/15 ⇒ hypotenuse = 17. sin A = 15/17, sec A = 17/8.
5Given sec θ = 13/12, calculate all other trigonometric ratios.
Adjacent 12, hypotenuse 13 ⇒ opposite 5. sin θ = 5/13, cos θ = 12/13, tan θ = 5/12, cot θ = 12/5, cosec θ = 13/5.
6If ∠A and ∠B are acute angles such that cos A = cos B, show that ∠A = ∠B.
Take ΔABC right-angled at C. cos A = AC/AB and cos B = BC/AB. cos A = cos B ⇒ AC = BC ⇒ angles opposite equal sides are equal ⇒ ∠A = ∠B.
7If cot θ = 7/8, evaluate: (i) (1 + sin θ)(1 − sin θ)/((1 + cos θ)(1 − cos θ)) (ii) cot²θ
(i) = (1 − sin²θ)/(1 − cos²θ) = cos²θ/sin²θ = cot²θ = 49/64. (ii) 49/64.
8If 3 cot A = 4, check whether (1 − tan²A)/(1 + tan²A) = cos²A − sin²A or not.
tan A = 3/4, sides 3, 4, 5. LHS = (1 − 9/16)/(1 + 9/16) = 7/25. RHS = 16/25 − 9/25 = 7/25. Yes, equal.
9In ΔABC, right-angled at B, if tan A = 1/√3, find the value of: (i) sin A cos C + cos A sin C (ii) cos A cos C − sin A sin C
A = 30°, C = 60°. (i) (½)(½) + (√3/2)(√3/2) = 1. (ii) (√3/2)(½) − (½)(√3/2) = 0.
10In ΔPQR, right-angled at Q, PR + QR = 25 cm and PQ = 5 cm. Determine sin P, cos P and tan P.
PR² − QR² = 25 ⇒ (PR − QR) × 25 = 25 ⇒ PR − QR = 1 ⇒ PR = 13, QR = 12. sin P = 12/13, cos P = 5/13, tan P = 12/5.
11State true or false and justify: (i) tan A is always less than 1. (ii) sec A = 12/5 for some value of A. (iii) cos A is the abbreviation used for the cosecant of A. (iv) cot A is the product of cot and A. (v) sin θ = 4/3 for some angle θ.
(i) False: tan 60° = √3 > 1. (ii) True: hypotenuse 12, adjacent 5 is possible (sec A ≥ 1). (iii) False: cos is cosine; cosecant is cosec. (iv) False: cot A is one symbol. (v) False: sin θ ≤ 1 because the hypotenuse is the longest side.

Exercise 8.3

1Express the trigonometric ratios sin A, sec A and tan A in terms of cot A.
sin A = 1/√(1 + cot²A); tan A = 1/cot A; sec A = √(1 + cot²A)/cot A.
2Write all the other trigonometric ratios of ∠A in terms of sec A.
cos A = 1/sec A; tan A = √(sec²A − 1); sin A = tan A × cos A = √(sec²A − 1)/sec A; cot A = 1/√(sec²A − 1); cosec A = sec A/√(sec²A − 1).
3Choose the correct option: (i) 9sec²A − 9tan²A = (A) 1 (B) 9 (C) 8 (D) 0. (ii) (1 + tan θ + sec θ)(1 + cot θ − cosec θ) = (A) 0 (B) 1 (C) 2 (D) −1. (iii) (sec A + tan A)(1 − sin A) = (A) sec A (B) sin A (C) cosec A (D) cos A. (iv) (1 + tan²A)/(1 + cot²A) = (A) sec²A (B) −1 (C) cot²A (D) tan²A.

(i) 9(sec²A − tan²A) = 9: (B).

(ii) In sin, cos: ((cos θ + sin θ + 1)/cos θ)((sin θ + cos θ − 1)/sin θ) = ((sin θ + cos θ)² − 1)/(sin θ cos θ) = 2 sin θ cos θ/(sin θ cos θ) = 2: (C).

(iii) ((1 + sin A)/cos A)(1 − sin A) = cos²A/cos A = cos A: (D).

(iv) sec²A/cosec²A = sin²A/cos²A = tan²A: (D).

4Prove the following identities (i) to (x), where the angles involved are acute angles for which the expressions are defined.

(i) (cosec θ − cot θ)² = (1 − cos θ)/(1 + cos θ). LHS = ((1 − cos θ)/sin θ)² = (1 − cos θ)²/(1 − cos²θ) = (1 − cos θ)²/((1 − cos θ)(1 + cos θ)) = RHS.

(ii) cos A/(1 + sin A) + (1 + sin A)/cos A = 2 sec A. LHS = (cos²A + 1 + 2 sin A + sin²A)/(cos A(1 + sin A)) = 2(1 + sin A)/(cos A(1 + sin A)) = 2/cos A = RHS.

(iii) tan θ/(1 − cot θ) + cot θ/(1 − tan θ) = 1 + sec θ cosec θ. See section 8.4.1: LHS = (t³ − 1)/(t(t − 1)) = t + 1 + 1/t = 1 + 1/(sin θ cos θ) = RHS.

(iv) (1 + sec A)/sec A = sin²A/(1 − cos A). LHS = cos A + 1. RHS = (1 − cos²A)/(1 − cos A) = 1 + cos A. Equal.

(v) (cos A − sin A + 1)/(cos A + sin A − 1) = cosec A + cot A (using cosec²A = 1 + cot²A). Divide by sin A: (cot A − 1 + cosec A)/(cot A + 1 − cosec A). Replace 1 in the numerator by cosec²A − cot²A: numerator = (cosec A + cot A)(1 − cosec A + cot A). The bracket equals the denominator, so LHS = cosec A + cot A.

(vi) √((1 + sin A)/(1 − sin A)) = sec A + tan A. Multiply inside by (1 + sin A)/(1 + sin A): √((1 + sin A)²/cos²A) = (1 + sin A)/cos A = sec A + tan A.

(vii) (sin θ − 2sin³θ)/(2cos³θ − cos θ) = tan θ. LHS = sin θ(1 − 2sin²θ)/(cos θ(2cos²θ − 1)). Since 1 − 2sin²θ = 1 − 2(1 − cos²θ) = 2cos²θ − 1, LHS = sin θ/cos θ = tan θ.

(viii) (sin A + cosec A)² + (cos A + sec A)² = 7 + tan²A + cot²A. LHS = sin²A + cosec²A + 2 + cos²A + sec²A + 2 = 5 + (1 + cot²A) + (1 + tan²A) = 7 + tan²A + cot²A.

(ix) (cosec A − sin A)(sec A − cos A) = 1/(tan A + cot A). LHS = ((1 − sin²A)/sin A)((1 − cos²A)/cos A) = (cos²A/sin A)(sin²A/cos A) = sin A cos A. RHS = 1/((sin²A + cos²A)/(sin A cos A)) = sin A cos A. Equal.

(x) (1 + tan²A)/(1 + cot²A) = ((1 − tan A)/(1 − cot A))² = tan²A. First: sec²A/cosec²A = tan²A. Second: (1 − tan A)/(1 − 1/tan A) = tan A(1 − tan A)/(tan A − 1) = −tan A, whose square is tan²A.
