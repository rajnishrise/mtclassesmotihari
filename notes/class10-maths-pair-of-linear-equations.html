📌 Chapter snapshot

  • General form: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0 (a² + b² ≠ 0). Each equation is a straight line.
  • Three possibilities: lines intersect (unique solution, consistent) · parallel (no solution, inconsistent) · coincident (infinitely many solutions, dependent and consistent).
  • Ratio test: a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂ ⇒ unique · a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ ⇒ none · a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂ ⇒ infinitely many.
  • Algebraic methods in syllabus: substitution and elimination. Removed: cross-multiplication method and equations reducible to linear form.
  • Most-asked in CBSE: nature of solutions / value of k (MCQ), solve a pair (2 marks), word problem on ages, money, fractions or speed (3–5 marks), graph two lines and find the triangle they make with an axis (5 marks).
  • Marks: part of unit Algebra (20 marks). This chapter usually brings 5 to 8 marks.
3.1

Linear equations in two variables

An equation like 2x + 3y = 12 is a linear equation in two variables. It has infinitely many solutions: (0, 4), (3, 2), (6, 0), (1.5, 3)… Every solution is a point, and all the points lie on one straight line. Every point on that line is a solution.

📏

One equation

One straight line, infinitely many solutions. Not enough to find x and y.

✌️

Two equations

Two lines. The point common to both is the answer that satisfies both clues.

🤔 Real life: two clues

"Akhila spent ₹20 on rides and Hoopla" is one clue: many answers. Add "rides cost ₹3, Hoopla ₹4, and she had half as many Hoopla turns as rides" and there is only one answer. Shopkeepers, bankers and engineers solve such pairs all the time: two items with two bills, two investments with two interest totals, two speeds with two journeys.

🧪 Try it nowPlot one equation

  1. On graph paper draw x + y = 10. Make a table: (0, 10), (4, 6), (10, 0).
  2. Join them with a ruler. Pick any other point on the line, like (7, 3): check that 7 + 3 = 10.
  3. Now draw y − x = 4 on the same axes: (0, 4), (3, 7), (−4, 0). Where do the lines meet? At (3, 7): 3 boys and 7 girls in NCERT Ex 3.1 Q1.
3.2

Graphical method of solution

Draw both lines on the same axes. Three things can happen.

⚠️ Graph drawing rules (CBSE gives marks for these)

  • Make a table of at least 2 (better 3) points for each line. Show the table.
  • Use the same scale on both axes and mark it ("1 cm = 1 unit").
  • Label each line with its equation and mark the intersection point with its coordinates.
  • Use integer points: for 5x + 7y = 50, pick x = 3 (y = 5) and x = 10 (y = 0), not x = 1.

🧪 Try itRails and roads

  1. NCERT's example: rails on a track are parallel (never meet: no solution). Two roads crossing at a chowk intersect (one meeting point). Two bus routes using the same road are coincident.
  2. Look out of the window or at your notebook lines. Find one example of each type around you and draw it.
3.2.1

The ratio test: know the answer without drawing

For a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0, compare the ratios of the coefficients.

CompareGraphSolutionsName
a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂intersecting linesexactly one (unique)consistent
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂coincident linesinfinitely manydependent (consistent)
a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂parallel linesnoneinconsistent

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q2: compare the ratios

(i) 5x − 4y + 8 = 0, 7x + 6y − 9 = 0: 5/7 ≠ −4/6 ⇒ intersect at a point.

(ii) 9x + 3y + 12 = 0, 18x + 6y + 24 = 0: 9/18 = 3/6 = 12/24 = ½ ⇒ coincident.

(iii) 6x − 3y + 10 = 0, 2x − y + 9 = 0: 6/2 = −3/−1 = 3 but 10/9 ≠ 3 ⇒ parallel.

Finding k (very common MCQ)

Unique solution: for kx + 2y = 5, 3x + y = 1: need k/3 ≠ 2/1 ⇒ k ≠ 6.

No solution: for x + 2y = 3 and 5x + ky = 7: need 1/5 = 2/k ≠ 3/7 ⇒ k = 10 (and 1/5 ≠ 3/7 ✓).

Infinitely many: for 2x + 3y = 7 and (k − 1)x + (k + 2)y = 3k: need 2/(k − 1) = 3/(k + 2) = 7/(3k). The first pair gives 2k + 4 = 3k − 3 ⇒ k = 7; check 7/21 = 1/3 = 2/6 ✓.

CBSE 2023: for kx − y = 2 and 6x − 2y = 3, infinitely many needs k/6 = −1/−2 = 2/3. But −1/−2 = ½ and 2/3 ≠ ½, so no such k exists.

⚡ Shortcuts

  • Write the equations in the same form (all terms on one side, or c on the right) before taking ratios. The ratio test works either way as long as both are in the same form.
  • If one equation is an exact multiple of the other ⇒ coincident.
  • If only the constant changes (x − y = 8 and 3x − 3y = 16) ⇒ parallel.
  • "Consistent" means at least one solution: both intersecting and coincident pairs are consistent.

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q6: given 2x + 3y − 8 = 0, write another equation so that the pair is

(i) intersecting: 3x + 2y − 7 = 0 (2/3 ≠ 3/2) (ii) parallel: 4x + 6y − 9 = 0 (2/4 = 3/6 ≠ −8/−9) (iii) coincident: 6x + 9y − 24 = 0 (all ratios ⅓).

3.3.1

Substitution method

Graphs give exact answers only when the meeting point has neat coordinates. Algebra always works. In substitution you make one variable the subject of one equation and substitute it into the other.

  1. From one equation, write y in terms of x (or x in terms of y). Pick the equation where this is easiest (coefficient 1).
  2. Substitute into the other equation. You get an equation in one variable. Solve it.
  3. Put this value back into the expression from step 1 to get the other variable.
  4. Check in both original equations.

NCERT Example: 7x − 15y = 2 and x + 2y = 3

From x + 2y = 3: x = 3 − 2y.

7(3 − 2y) − 15y = 2 ⇒ 21 − 29y = 2 ⇒ y = 19/29.

x = 3 − 38/29 = 49/29. Solution: x = 49/29, y = 19/29.

What if you get 0 = 0 or 0 = 5?

3x − y = 3 and 9x − 3y = 9 (Ex 3.2 Q1(iii)): y = 3x − 3 ⇒ 9x − 3(3x − 3) = 9 ⇒ 9 = 9, always true ⇒ infinitely many solutions (coincident lines).

x − y = 8 and 3x − 3y = 16: y = x − 8 ⇒ 3x − 3x + 24 = 16 ⇒ 24 = 16, false ⇒ no solution (parallel lines).

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q2: solve 2x + 3y = 11 and 2x − 4y = −24, then find m in y = mx + 3

From the first, 2x = 11 − 3y. Then 11 − 3y − 4y = −24 ⇒ 7y = 35 ⇒ y = 5, 2x = −4 ⇒ x = −2.

5 = m(−2) + 3 ⇒ m = −1.

🧪 Try itTwo-bag puzzle

  1. Put some marbles in a red bag (x) and a blue bag (y) without telling your friend. Tell them: "Total is 14. Red has 4 more than blue."
  2. Friend writes x + y = 14 and x = y + 4, substitutes: (y + 4) + y = 14 ⇒ y = 5, x = 9.
  3. Open the bags and check!
3.3.2

Elimination method

Make the coefficients of one variable equal (or opposite) by multiplying the equations by suitable numbers. Then add or subtract to eliminate that variable.

  1. Multiply both equations by numbers that make the coefficients of x (or y) equal. Use the LCM of the two coefficients.
  2. Subtract (if the signs are the same) or add (if opposite) to remove that variable.
  3. Solve for the remaining variable, then substitute back.
  4. If both variables vanish: a true statement (0 = 0) ⇒ infinitely many; a false one (0 = 3) ⇒ no solution.

NCERT Example: ratio of incomes 9 : 7, of expenditures 4 : 3; each saves ₹2000. Find incomes.

Incomes 9x, 7x; expenditures 4y, 3y.

9x − 4y = 2000 …(1) and 7x − 3y = 2000 …(2).

(1) × 3: 27x − 12y = 6000; (2) × 4: 28x − 12y = 8000. Subtract: x = 2000.

Incomes: ₹18,000 and ₹14,000.

CBSE 2024 (2 marks): solve 7x − 2y = 5 and 8x + 7y = 15, and verify

(1) × 7: 49x − 14y = 35; (2) × 2: 16x + 14y = 30. Add: 65x = 65 ⇒ x = 1. 1

7 − 2y = 5 ⇒ y = 1. Verify: 7 − 2 = 5 ✓, 8 + 7 = 15 ✓ 1

⚡ Which method when?

  • A variable has coefficient 1 or −1 ⇒ substitution is fastest.
  • Big or fractional coefficients ⇒ elimination.
  • Decimals or fractions? Clear them first: 0.2x + 0.3y = 1.3 ⇒ 2x + 3y = 13.
  • Symmetric pairs like 47x + 31y = 63 and 31x + 47y = 15: add (78x + 78y = 78 ⇒ x + y = 1) and subtract (16x − 16y = 48 ⇒ x − y = 3) ⇒ x = 2, y = −1.

🧪 Try itShopping receipts

  1. Ask at a shop or invent: 2 notebooks + 3 pens = ₹80, 3 notebooks + 2 pens = ₹95.
  2. Eliminate: × 3 and × 2 gives 6n + 9p = 240 and 6n + 4p = 190. Subtract: 5p = 50 ⇒ pen ₹10, notebook ₹25.
  3. Check with the receipts. You just did what a shop accountant does.

    CBSE 2024 (3 marks): ages

    Three years ago, Rashmi was thrice as old as Nazma. Ten years later, Rashmi will be twice as old as Nazma. How old are they now?

    Let Rashmi = x, Nazma = y. x − 3 = 3(y − 3) ⇒ x − 3y = −6 …(1) 1

    x + 10 = 2(y + 10) ⇒ x − 2y = 10 …(2) 1

    (2) − (1): y = 16, x = 42. Rashmi is 42, Nazma 16. 1

    CBSE 2025 (5 marks): interest

    Vijay invested some money at 8% p.a. (scheme A) and some at 9% p.a. (scheme B). Total annual interest = ₹1860. If he had interchanged the amounts, he would get ₹20 more. Find the amounts.

    Let x in A, y in B: 0.08x + 0.09y = 1860 ⇒ 8x + 9y = 186000 …(1)

    0.09x + 0.08y = 1880 ⇒ 9x + 8y = 188000 …(2)

    Add: 17(x + y) = 374000 ⇒ x + y = 22000. Subtract (2) − (1): x − y = 2000.

    ₹12,000 in A, ₹10,000 in B.

    NCERT Ex 3.3 Q2(iii): digits

    Sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. Nine times the number equals twice the number with digits reversed. Find the number.

    x + y = 9 and 9(10x + y) = 2(10y + x) ⇒ 88x = 11y ⇒ y = 8x. So x + 8x = 9 ⇒ x = 1, y = 8. Number = 18.

    CBSE 2023 (3 marks): numbers

    Half the difference between two numbers is 2. The sum of the greater number and twice the smaller is 13. Find the numbers.

    (x − y)/2 = 2 ⇒ x − y = 4; x + 2y = 13 ⇒ 3y = 9 ⇒ y = 3, x = 7. Numbers: 7 and 3.

    ⚠️ Word-problem mistakes

    • Not stating what x and y stand for ("Let the cost of a bat be ₹x"): ½ mark lost.
    • In age problems, forgetting to change both ages by the same years.
    • Leaving the answer as "x = 42, y = 16". Write the final sentence with units.
    • In digit problems, writing the number as x + y. It is 10x + y.

    SubstitutionElimination

    ⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

    1. Calling a coincident pair "inconsistent". It is consistent (infinitely many solutions).
    2. Taking ratios of equations written in different forms (one with c on the right, one with c on the left).
    3. Sign errors when subtracting: (−4y) − (−3y) = −y, not −7y.
    4. Forgetting to multiply the constant as well when scaling an equation.
    5. Graph: plotting only 2 points with no table, or not labelling the lines and the solution.
    6. Word problems: not defining x and y, or not writing the final answer in words with units.
    7. Digit problems: number = x + y (wrong). It is 10x + y.
    8. For "no solution", forgetting to check c₁/c₂ is not equal to the other ratios.

    CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

    The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Standard, 041) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 38 questions: Section A 20 × 1 (18 MCQs + 2 assertion–reason), B 5 × 2, C 6 × 3, D 4 × 5, E 3 case studies × 4 (1 + 1 + 2). Unit Algebra carries 20 marks; linear equations is one of its biggest scorers.

    2025Q2 (MCQ), Q32 (5)(1, 2) solves both ⇒ relation between a and b; investment at 8% and 9%

    ✍️ How the CBSE marking scheme splits marks

    • Word problem (3 marks): 1 for forming each equation correctly (2 marks), 1 for solving and the final answer. In 5-mark versions: 1 + 1 for equations, 2 for solving, 1 for the answer.
    • Graphical (5 marks, 2026 Q32): 1½ for each correctly drawn line (with table), 1 for the vertices, 1 for the area.
    • Solve a pair (2 marks): 1 for one variable, 1 for the other (and verification if asked).
    • Nature / value of k: MCQs; no step marks.

    🏆 Answer-writing tips for full marks

    1. Number your equations (1), (2) and say what you do: "(1) × 3 − (2) × 4".
    2. Always verify your solution in both equations; it catches 90% of sign slips.
    3. In graphs, draw the table for each line, label the lines and write the solution "x = 2, y = 2" below.
    4. End word problems with a full sentence: "The cost of a bat is ₹500 and of a ball is ₹50."

    C1A boat goes 24 km downstream in 2 hours and 16 km upstream in 2 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water and the speed of the stream.

    Let boat x km/h, stream y km/h. Downstream x + y = 24/2 = 12, upstream x − y = 16/2 = 8. 1½

    Adding: 2x = 20 ⇒ boat 10 km/h, stream 2 km/h. 1½

    C2A fraction becomes 1/2 when 1 is added to both its numerator and denominator, and becomes 1/3 when 1 is subtracted from both. Find the fraction.

    (x + 1)/(y + 1) = 1/2 ⇒ 2x − y = −1; (x − 1)/(y − 1) = 1/3 ⇒ 3x − y = 2. 2

    Subtract: x = 3, y = 7 ⇒ fraction 3/7. Check: 4/8 = 1/2 ✓, 2/6 = 1/3 ✓ 1

    C3The cost of 2 kg apples and 1 kg grapes is ₹160. After a month, 4 kg apples and 2 kg grapes cost ₹300. Represent algebraically and say whether the prices stayed the same.

    2x + y = 160 and 4x + 2y = 300. 1

    2/4 = 1/2 ≠ 160/300 ⇒ parallel lines, no solution. 1

    So the prices could not have stayed the same (they changed during the month). 1

    C4In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A = (2x + 4)°, ∠B = (y + 3)°, ∠C = (2y + 10)°, ∠D = (4x − 5)°. Find the four angles.

    ∠A + ∠C = 180 ⇒ 2x + 2y = 166 ⇒ x + y = 83; ∠B + ∠D = 180 ⇒ 4x + y = 182. 1½

    3x = 99 ⇒ x = 33, y = 50. ½

    ∠A = 70°, ∠B = 53°, ∠C = 110°, ∠D = 127°. 1

    10x + y = 13(x − y) + 2 ⇒ 3x − 14y = −2 …(2) 1½

    (i) 2x + 3y = 70. 1

    (i) x + 10y = 105, x + 15y = 155. 1

    Exercise 3.1

    1Form the pair of linear equations and find their solutions graphically: (i) 10 students of Class X took part in a Mathematics quiz. If the number of girls is 4 more than the number of boys, find the number of boys and girls. (ii) 5 pencils and 7 pens together cost ₹50, whereas 7 pencils and 5 pens together cost ₹46. Find the cost of one pencil and that of one pen.

    (i) Boys x, girls y: x + y = 10, y − x = 4. Graphs meet at (3, 7): 3 boys, 7 girls (see graph in section 3.2).

    (ii) Pencil ₹x, pen ₹y: 5x + 7y = 50, 7x + 5y = 46. Graphs meet at (3, 5): pencil ₹3, pen ₹5.

    2On comparing the ratios a₁/a₂, b₁/b₂ and c₁/c₂, find out whether the lines intersect at a point, are parallel or coincident: (i) 5x − 4y + 8 = 0, 7x + 6y − 9 = 0 (ii) 9x + 3y + 12 = 0, 18x + 6y + 24 = 0 (iii) 6x − 3y + 10 = 0, 2x − y + 9 = 0

    (i) 5/7 ≠ −4/6 ⇒ intersect at a point.

    (ii) 9/18 = 3/6 = 12/24 ⇒ coincident.

    (iii) 6/2 = −3/−1 = 3, 10/9 ≠ 3 ⇒ parallel.

    3Find out whether the following pairs are consistent or inconsistent: (i) 3x + 2y = 5; 2x − 3y = 7 (ii) 2x − 3y = 8; 4x − 6y = 9 (iii) (3/2)x + (5/3)y = 7; 9x − 10y = 14 (iv) 5x − 3y = 11; −10x + 6y = −22 (v) (4/3)x + 2y = 8; 2x + 3y = 12

    (i) 3/2 ≠ 2/(−3) ⇒ consistent (unique solution).

    (ii) 2/4 = −3/−6 = ½ ≠ 8/9 ⇒ inconsistent.

    (iii) (3/2)/9 = 1/6, (5/3)/(−10) = −1/6 ⇒ not equal ⇒ consistent.

    (iv) 5/−10 = −3/6 = 11/−22 = −½ ⇒ coincident ⇒ consistent (infinitely many).

    (v) (4/3)/2 = 2/3, 2/3, 8/12 = 2/3 ⇒ coincident ⇒ consistent.

    4Which of the following pairs are consistent/inconsistent? If consistent, obtain the solution graphically: (i) x + y = 5, 2x + 2y = 10 (ii) x − y = 8, 3x − 3y = 16 (iii) 2x + y − 6 = 0, 4x − 2y − 4 = 0 (iv) 2x − 2y − 2 = 0, 4x − 4y − 5 = 0

    (i) ½ = ½ = ½ ⇒ coincident, consistent: infinitely many solutions, every point of x + y = 5, e.g. (0, 5), (1, 4), (5, 0).

    (ii) 1/3 = 1/3 ≠ 8/16 ⇒ parallel, inconsistent.

    (iii) 2/4 ≠ 1/(−2) ⇒ consistent, unique solution (2, 2).

    (iv) 2/4 = 2/4 ≠ 2/5 ⇒ parallel, inconsistent.

    5Half the perimeter of a rectangular garden, whose length is 4 m more than its width, is 36 m. Find the dimensions of the garden.
    l + w = 36 and l − w = 4 ⇒ 2l = 40. Length 20 m, width 16 m. (Graphically the lines meet at (20, 16).)
    6Given the linear equation 2x + 3y − 8 = 0, write another linear equation in two variables such that the pair formed is: (i) intersecting lines (ii) parallel lines (iii) coincident lines
    (i) 3x + 2y − 7 = 0 (ii) 4x + 6y − 9 = 0 (iii) 6x + 9y − 24 = 0. (Many answers possible: check with the ratio test.)
    7Draw the graphs of x − y + 1 = 0 and 3x + 2y − 12 = 0. Determine the coordinates of the vertices of the triangle formed by these lines and the x-axis, and shade the triangular region.

    x − y + 1 = 0: (0, 1), (−1, 0), (2, 3). 3x + 2y − 12 = 0: (0, 6), (4, 0), (2, 3).

    The lines meet at (2, 3). Vertices: (−1, 0), (4, 0), (2, 3). (Area = ½ × 5 × 3 = 7.5 sq units.)

    Exercise 3.2 (substitution)

    1Solve by substitution: (i) x + y = 14, x − y = 4 (ii) s − t = 3, s/3 + t/2 = 6 (iii) 3x − y = 3, 9x − 3y = 9 (iv) 0.2x + 0.3y = 1.3, 0.4x + 0.5y = 2.3 (v) √2x + √3y = 0, √3x − √8y = 0 (vi) 3x/2 − 5y/3 = −2, x/3 + y/2 = 13/6

    (i) x = 14 − y ⇒ 14 − 2y = 4 ⇒ y = 5, x = 9, y = 5.

    (ii) s = t + 3 ⇒ 2s + 3t = 36 ⇒ 2t + 6 + 3t = 36 ⇒ t = 6, s = 9, t = 6.

    (iii) y = 3x − 3 ⇒ 9x − 9x + 9 = 9 ⇒ 9 = 9: infinitely many solutions (y = 3x − 3 for any x).

    (iv) 2x + 3y = 13 ⇒ x = (13 − 3y)/2 ⇒ 2(13 − 3y) + 5y = 23 ⇒ y = 3, x = 2, y = 3.

    (v) x = −√3y/√2 ⇒ √3(−√3y/√2) − 2√2 y = 0 ⇒ y(−3/√2 − 2√2) = 0 ⇒ y = 0, x = 0.

    (vi) 9x − 10y = −12 and 2x + 3y = 13 ⇒ x = (13 − 3y)/2 ⇒ 9(13 − 3y) − 20y = −24 ⇒ 47y = 141 ⇒ y = 3, x = 2.

    2Solve 2x + 3y = 11 and 2x − 4y = −24 and hence find the value of m for which y = mx + 3.
    2x = 11 − 3y ⇒ 11 − 7y = −24 ⇒ y = 5, x = −2. Then 5 = −2m + 3 ⇒ m = −1.
    3Form the pair of linear equations and solve by substitution: (i) The difference between two numbers is 26 and one number is three times the other. (ii) The larger of two supplementary angles exceeds the smaller by 18 degrees. (iii) A cricket coach buys 7 bats and 6 balls for ₹3800; later 3 bats and 5 balls for ₹1750. (iv) Taxi fare: ₹105 for 10 km, ₹155 for 15 km; fixed charge, rate, and fare for 25 km. (v) A fraction becomes 9/11 if 2 is added to both numerator and denominator; 5/6 if 3 is added to both. (vi) Five years hence, Jacob's age will be three times his son's; five years ago, Jacob's age was seven times his son's.

    (i) x − y = 26, x = 3y ⇒ 2y = 26 ⇒ 13 and 39.

    (ii) x + y = 180, x − y = 18 ⇒ 99° and 81°.

    (iii) 7x + 6y = 3800, 3x + 5y = 1750 ⇒ x = (1750 − 5y)/3 ⇒ 7(1750 − 5y) + 18y = 11400 ⇒ 17y = 850 ⇒ y = 50, x = 500. Bat ₹500, ball ₹50.

    (iv) x + 10y = 105, x + 15y = 155 ⇒ y = 10, x = 5. Fixed ₹5, ₹10 per km; 25 km: ₹255.

    (v) 11(x + 2) = 9(y + 2) ⇒ 11x − 9y = −4; 6(x + 3) = 5(y + 3) ⇒ 6x − 5y = −3 ⇒ x = 7, y = 9. Fraction 7/9.

    (vi) x + 5 = 3(y + 5) ⇒ x − 3y = 10; x − 5 = 7(y − 5) ⇒ x − 7y = −30 ⇒ 4y = 40. Jacob 40, son 10.

    Exercise 3.3 (elimination)

    1Solve by elimination and by substitution: (i) x + y = 5, 2x − 3y = 4 (ii) 3x + 4y = 10, 2x − 2y = 2 (iii) 3x − 5y − 4 = 0, 9x = 2y + 7 (iv) x/2 + 2y/3 = −1, x − y/3 = 3

    (i) 3 × (1) + (2): 5x = 19 ⇒ x = 19/5, y = 6/5.

    (ii) (1) + 2 × (2): 7x = 14 ⇒ x = 2, y = 1.

    (iii) 3 × (1): 9x − 15y = 12; minus 9x − 2y = 7 ⇒ −13y = 5 ⇒ y = −5/13, x = 9/13.

    (iv) 3x + 4y = −6 and 3x − y = 9 ⇒ 5y = −15 ⇒ y = −3, x = 2.

    Substitution gives the same answers. Try them in the Elimination stepper.

    2Form the pair of linear equations and solve by elimination: (i) If we add 1 to the numerator and subtract 1 from the denominator, a fraction reduces to 1. It becomes 1/2 if we only add 1 to the denominator. (ii) Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. (iii) The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. Nine times this number is twice the number obtained by reversing the digits. (iv) Meena withdrew ₹2000 in ₹50 and ₹100 notes, 25 notes in all. (v) A lending library has a fixed charge for the first three days and an additional charge for each day thereafter. Saritha paid ₹27 for a book kept for seven days, Susy paid ₹21 for five days.

    (i) x + 1 = y − 1 ⇒ x − y = −2; 2x = y + 1 ⇒ 2x − y = 1 ⇒ x = 3, y = 5. Fraction 3/5.

    (ii) x − 5 = 3(y − 5) ⇒ x − 3y = −10; x + 10 = 2(y + 10) ⇒ x − 2y = 10 ⇒ y = 20, x = 50. Nuri 50, Sonu 20.

    (iii) x + y = 9; 9(10x + y) = 2(10y + x) ⇒ 8x − y = 0 ⇒ 9x = 9 ⇒ x = 1, y = 8. Number 18.

    (iv) x + y = 25, 50x + 100y = 2000 ⇒ x + 2y = 40 ⇒ y = 15, x = 10. 10 notes of ₹50, 15 notes of ₹100.

    (v) x + 4y = 27, x + 2y = 21 ⇒ y = 3, x = 15. Fixed charge ₹15, ₹3 per extra day.
