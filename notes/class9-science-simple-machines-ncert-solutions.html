NCERT textbook questions (Section 7.6)

7 questions

Questions are from the Pause and Ponder and Revise, Reflect, Refine sections, slightly shortened.

1
Why are roads on hills built to wind around in gentle slopes instead of going straight up?
Key ideaInclined plane: MA = length of slope (L)height (h)
  1. Step 1
    A winding road is a long inclined plane
  2. Step 2
    For the same height h, its length L is much larger, so MA = Lh is large
  3. Step 3
    A large MA means vehicles need a much smaller force to climb
  4. Step 4
    The work done (mgh) is the same; it is only spread over a longer distance
  5. AnswerWinding roads reduce the force needed to climb
2
Why is it easier to climb an inclined ladder than a vertical ladder?
  1. Step 1
    An inclined ladder acts as an inclined plane with L > h, so MA = Lh > 1
  2. Step 2
    A vertical ladder has L = h, so MA = 1: we lift our full weight straight up
  3. AnswerThe inclined ladder needs less force at each step
3
Why is it easier to open the lid of a tin using a spoon?
Key ideaLever: MA = effort armload arm
  1. Step 1
    The spoon is a Class I lever; the rim of the tin is the fulcrum
  2. Step 2
    The load (grip of the lid) is very close to the fulcrum; the effort is at the far end of the handle
  3. Step 3
    Effort arm ≫ load arm, so MA is large
  4. AnswerA small effort gives a large force on the lid
4
Why do we push a hard object closer to the hinge (fulcrum) of a pair of scissors to cut it?
Key ideaEffort × effort arm = load × load arm
  1. Step 1
    Moving the object closer to the hinge shortens the load arm
  2. Step 2
    For the same effort and effort arm, a shorter load arm means a larger force on the object
  3. AnswerThe blades press harder near the hinge
5
Many perpetual motion machines have been designed, but none works. Why do all real machines slow down and stop?
  1. Step 1
    A machine cannot create energy
  2. Step 2
    In every real machine some work is done against friction and air resistance
  3. Step 3
    This changes mechanical energy into heat and sound, which is lost continuously
  4. AnswerBecause of friction losses, a machine stops unless energy is supplied again
6
Which factors decide the energy needed to raise a flag using a pulley? Does raising it slowly or quickly change the work done? How does the power change if the speed is doubled?
Key ideaWork W = mgh; power P = Wt
  1. Step 1
    Energy needed = mgh: depends on the mass of the flag, the height of the pole and g
  2. Step 2
    The same mgh is needed whether the flag goes up slowly or quickly
  3. Step 3
    Doubling the speed halves the time t, so P = Wt doubles
  4. AnswerMass and height decide the work; work is unchanged; power doubles
7
An adult who weighs twice as much as a child balances a seesaw with the child. Draw a figure showing their distances from the fulcrum.
GivenWeight of child = W, weight of adult = 2W
Key ideaPrinciple of lever: W₁ × d₁ = W₂ × d₂
  1. Step 1
    W × dchild = 2W × dadult
  2. Step 2
    dchild = 2 dadult
  3. Step 3
    Figure: fulcrum in the middle, child 2 m on one side, adult 1 m on the other side
  4. AnswerThe child sits twice as far from the fulcrum as the adult

1
An effort of 50 N lifts a load of 200 N using an ideal pulley system. Find the MA. How many rope segments support the movable block?
GivenLoad L = 200 N, effort E = 50 N
To findMA and number of supporting segments
Key ideaMA = LE; for an ideal pulley system MA = number of supporting segments
  1. Step 1
    MA = 20050 = 4
  2. AnswerMA = 4, so 4 segments
2
A box weighing 1200 N is pushed up a ramp 4 m long to a height of 1 m. Find the MA and the effort (ignore friction).
GivenL = 1200 N, length = 4 m, h = 1 m
Key ideaMA = lengthheight, E = LMA
  1. Step 1
    MA = 41 = 4
  2. Step 2
    E = 12004 = 300 N
  3. AnswerMA = 4, effort = 300 N
3
A child of mass 20 kg sits 1.5 m from the fulcrum of a seesaw. Where must a child of mass 30 kg sit to balance it?
Key ideam₁ × d₁ = m₂ × d₂ (g cancels)
  1. Step 1
    20 × 1.5 = 30 × d
  2. Step 2
    d = 3030 = 1 m
  3. Answer1 m from the fulcrum on the other side
4
In a lever, a load of 800 N with a load arm of 25 cm is balanced by an effort of 200 N. Find the effort arm and the MA.
Key ideaE × effort arm = L × load arm
  1. Step 1
    200 × x = 800 × 25
  2. Step 2
    x = 20000200 = 100 cm
  3. Step 3
    MA = 800200 = 4
  4. AnswerEffort arm = 100 cm, MA = 4
5
A load of 500 N is raised by 2 m using a single ideal movable pulley. Find (i) the effort (ii) the distance moved by the effort (iii) the work done by the effort.
Key ideaSingle movable pulley: MA = 2, effort moves twice the load distance
  1. Step 1
    (i) E = 5002 = 250 N
  2. Step 2
    (ii) Effort distance = 2 × 2 = 4 m
  3. Step 3
    (iii) W = 250 × 4 = 1000 J (= 500 × 2, as expected for an ideal machine)
  4. Answer(i) 250 N (ii) 4 m (iii) 1000 J
6
A machine needs 2400 J of work input to lift a 400 N load through 4.5 m. Find its efficiency.
Key ideaEfficiency = output workinput work × 100%
  1. Step 1
    Output work = 400 × 4.5 = 1800 J
  2. Step 2
    Efficiency = 18002400 × 100% = 75%
  3. AnswerEfficiency = 75%
