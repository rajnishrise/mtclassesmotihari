📌 Chapter snapshot

  • Zero of p(x): a number k with p(k) = 0. On the graph of y = p(x), the zeroes are the x-coordinates where the graph meets the x-axis.
  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes. Linear: exactly 1. Quadratic: 0, 1 or 2. Cubic: 1, 2 or 3 (never 0).
  • Quadratic ax² + bx + c: α + β = −b/a, αβ = c/a. Polynomial with zeroes α, β: k[x² − (α + β)x + αβ].
  • Cubic ax³ + bx² + cx + d: α + β + γ = −b/a, αβ + βγ + γα = c/a, αβγ = −d/a.
  • Removed from the syllabus: the division algorithm for polynomials (Ex 2.4 / long division) is not in the board exam now.
  • Most-asked in CBSE: count zeroes from a graph (MCQ), find k from a condition on zeroes, find zeroes and verify the relationship (2–3 marks), form a quadratic from sum and product, evaluate 1/α + 1/β, α² + β² etc.
  • Marks: unit Algebra (Polynomials, Linear Equations, Quadratic Equations, AP) = 20 marks. Polynomials usually brings 3 to 5 marks.
2.1

What is a polynomial?

A polynomial in x is an expression like 3x² − 5x + 2 in which every power of x is a whole number (0, 1, 2, …). The highest power is its degree. The numbers in front of the powers are the coefficients.

DegreeNameGeneral formExamples
0constantc7, −2, √3
1linearax + b, a ≠ 02x − 3, √2 y + 5, (2/3)x
2quadraticax² + bx + c, a ≠ 0x² − 4, 2y² + 3y − 1, 3 − u + u²
3cubicax³ + bx² + cx + d, a ≠ 0x³ − x, 2 − x² + x³, √2 x³

✅ Polynomials

x² + 3x + 2 · 4z³ − 2z · √3 x − 1 · (1/2)x² (coefficient ½ is fine) · 5

❌ Not polynomials

1/(x − 1) (x in the denominator) · √x + 3 (power ½) · x⁻² + 1 (negative power) · x + 1/x

Value of a polynomial and zeroes

If p(x) = x² − 3x − 4, then p(2) = 4 − 6 − 4 = −6. p(−1) = 1 + 3 − 4 = 0 and p(4) = 16 − 12 − 4 = 0.

So −1 and 4 are the zeroes of x² − 3x − 4.

Linear: the zero of ax + b is x = −b/a = −(constant term)/(coefficient of x). Zero of 2x + 3 is −3/2.

🧪 Try it nowPolynomial or not?

Sort these on a sheet into two columns: 5x³ − 2, √x + 1, x² − 1/x, 7, (x + 2)(x − 3), 2^x, πx². (Answer: polynomials are 5x³ − 2, 7, (x + 2)(x − 3) = x² − x − 6 and πx². 2^x has x in the power, not allowed.)

2.2

Geometrical meaning of the zeroes

Draw the graph of y = p(x). Where it meets the x-axis, y = 0, so p(x) = 0. So the zeroes of p(x) are the x-coordinates of the points where the graph of y = p(x) meets the x-axis. Count the meeting points and you have counted the zeroes.

Linear polynomial: a straight line, exactly one zero

Quadratic polynomial: a parabola, 0, 1 or 2 zeroes

The curve is called a parabola. Its axis of symmetry is the vertical line x = −b/(2a), exactly halfway between the two zeroes.

Cubic polynomial: at most 3 zeroes, at least 1

📏 Rule of degree

A polynomial of degree n has at most n zeroes; its graph meets the x-axis at most n times. A cubic always meets the x-axis at least once (its two ends go in opposite directions), so a cubic has 1, 2 or 3 zeroes.

⚠️ Graph MCQ trap

Count only the points on the x-axis, not where the graph crosses the y-axis, and not the turning points. A graph that touches the x-axis and turns back counts as one meeting point. A graph lying completely above or below the x-axis has 0 zeroes (CBSE 2026 Q4).

🤔 Real life

The path of a ball, a stone from a catapult or water from a fountain is a downward parabola (a < 0). Its two zeroes are where it leaves the ground and where it lands. The cables of suspension bridges and satellite dishes are parabolas too. Engineers use the vertex (highest point) to design arches.

🧪 Try itDraw a parabola point by point

  1. Make a table for y = x² − 3x − 4 with x = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
  2. You get y = 6, 0, −4, −6, −6, −4, 0, 6. Plot the points on graph paper and join them with a smooth ∪ curve.
  3. Mark where it cuts the x-axis: (−1, 0) and (4, 0). Fold the paper along x = 1.5: the two halves match. That fold line is the axis of symmetry.

2.2.1

Finding zeroes: splitting the middle term

To find the zeroes of ax² + bx + c, factorise it and set each factor equal to 0. The quickest way is to split the middle term bx into two terms whose coefficients add to b and multiply to a × c.

Find two numbers p and q with p + q = b and p × q = a × c.
Then ax² + bx + c = ax² + px + qx + c, and group in pairs.

Example 2 (NCERT): zeroes of x² + 7x + 10

a × c = 10, b = 7. Numbers: 5 and 2 (5 + 2 = 7, 5 × 2 = 10).

x² + 7x + 10 = x² + 5x + 2x + 10 = x(x + 5) + 2(x + 5) = (x + 2)(x + 5).

Zeroes: x + 2 = 0 ⇒ x = −2; x + 5 = 0 ⇒ x = −5.

Verify: sum = −7 = −b/a ✓ product = 10 = c/a ✓

Harder: zeroes of 6x² − 7x − 3 (NCERT Ex 2.2 Q1)

a × c = 6 × (−3) = −18, b = −7. Numbers: −9 and 2.

6x² − 9x + 2x − 3 = 3x(2x − 3) + 1(2x − 3) = (3x + 1)(2x − 3).

Zeroes: x = 3/2 and x = −1/3.

Special forms (no splitting needed)

x² − 3 = (x − √3)(x + √3) ⇒ zeroes ±√3 (NCERT Example 3). Here b = 0, so sum = 0.

4u² + 8u = 4u(u + 2) ⇒ zeroes 0 and −2. Here c = 0, so product = 0.

4s² − 4s + 1 = (2s − 1)² ⇒ zeroes ½, ½ (equal zeroes; graph touches the axis).

CBSE 2025 (2 marks): zeroes of x² + (4/3)x − 4/3

Multiply by 3 (zeroes do not change): 3x² + 4x − 4. a × c = −12, b = 4 ⇒ 6 and −2. ½

3x² + 6x − 2x − 4 = 3x(x + 2) − 2(x + 2) = (3x − 2)(x + 2). 1

Zeroes: 2/3 and −2. ½

⚡ Sign tricks for p and q

  • c > 0 and b > 0: both numbers positive (x² + 7x + 10 → 5, 2).
  • c > 0 and b < 0: both negative (x² − 7x + 10 → −5, −2).
  • c < 0: opposite signs; the bigger one takes the sign of b (x² − 3x − 4 → −4, +1).
  • No pair found? Then the zeroes are irrational; use the quadratic formula (Chapter 4): x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)]/(2a).

🧪 Try itAlgebra tiles on paper

  1. Cut 1 big square (x² tile), 7 long strips (x tiles) and 10 small squares (1 tiles) from cardboard.
  2. Arrange all 18 pieces into one rectangle. It is only possible one way: sides (x + 5) and (x + 2).
  3. So x² + 7x + 10 = (x + 5)(x + 2). Factorising = making a rectangle.
2.3

Relationship between zeroes and coefficients

If α and β are the zeroes of ax² + bx + c, then x − α and x − β are its factors, so ax² + bx + c = a(x − α)(x − β) = ax² − a(α + β)x + aαβ. Comparing the coefficients gives two beautiful formulas.

Sum of zeroes α + β = −b/a
= −(coefficient of x) / (coefficient of x²)
Product of zeroes αβ = c/a
= (constant term) / (coefficient of x²)

Verify for x² − 2x − 8 (NCERT Ex 2.2 Q1)

x² − 4x + 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2) ⇒ zeroes 4 and −2.

Sum = 4 + (−2) = 2 and −b/a = −(−2)/1 = 2 ✓ · Product = −8 and c/a = −8/1 = −8 ✓

Making a polynomial from its zeroes

p(x) = k[x² − (α + β)x + αβ], k any non-zero real number
"x² − (sum)x + (product)". Choose k to clear fractions.

Example 4 (NCERT): sum −3, product 2

x² − (−3)x + 2 = x² + 3x + 2 (zeroes −1, −2).

Sum 1/4, product −1 (NCERT Ex 2.2 Q2(i))

x² − (1/4)x − 1. Take k = 4: 4x² − x − 4.

Zeroes 2/3 and −2/3 (CBSE 2023)

Sum = 0, product = −4/9 ⇒ x² − 4/9. Take k = 9: 9x² − 4 (or any multiple, like 5(9x² − 4)).

⚠️ Sign mistakes are the most common error

In x² − (sum)x + (product) the middle sign is minus. Sum of zeroes is −b/a, with a minus. For 2x² − k√2 x + 1 (CBSE 2024): sum = −(−k√2)/2 = k√2/2.

🤔 Why "infinitely many" polynomials?

x² + 3x + 2, 2x² + 6x + 4 and −5x² − 15x − 10 all have zeroes −1 and −2. Multiplying by k stretches or flips the parabola but keeps the same x-axis crossings. NCERT answers usually give the simplest one (k chosen to make whole-number coefficients).

2.3.1

Expressions in α and β (without finding them)

CBSE often asks for 1/α + 1/β, α² + β² and similar values. Never solve for α and β. Write the expression in terms of α + β and αβ, then put in −b/a and c/a.

ExpressionWritten using (α + β) and αβ
1/α + 1/β(α + β)/αβ
α² + β²(α + β)² − 2αβ
(α − β)²(α + β)² − 4αβ
α/β + β/α(α² + β²)/αβ = [(α + β)² − 2αβ]/αβ
α³ + β³(α + β)³ − 3αβ(α + β)
1/α² + 1/β²(α² + β²)/(αβ)²
α²β + αβ²αβ(α + β)

CBSE 2026 (2 marks): α, β zeroes of x² − 3x − 1. Find 1/α + 1/β

α + β = −(−3)/1 = 3, αβ = −1/1 = −1. 1

1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = 3/(−1) = −3. 1

CBSE 2025 (1 mark): α, β zeroes of 3x² + 6x + k with α + β + αβ = −2/3. Find k

α + β = −6/3 = −2, αβ = k/3. So −2 + k/3 = −2/3 ⇒ k/3 = 4/3 ⇒ k = 4.

Solved: if α, β are zeroes of x² − 5x + 6, find α² + β² and α − β (α > β)

α + β = 5, αβ = 6. α² + β² = 25 − 12 = 13. (α − β)² = 25 − 24 = 1 ⇒ α − β = 1.

Solved: one zero is the negative of the other

If one zero of x² + (k − 3)x − 5 is the negative of the other, then α + β = 0 ⇒ −(k − 3) = 0 ⇒ k = 3.

If one zero is the reciprocal of the other (αβ = 1) for (k² + 4)x² + 13x + 4k: 4k/(k² + 4) = 1 ⇒ k² − 4k + 4 = 0 ⇒ k = 2.

🧠 Condition dictionary

  • Zeroes equal in size, opposite sign ⇒ sum = 0 ⇒ b = 0.
  • Reciprocal zeroes ⇒ product = 1 ⇒ c = a.
  • One zero is 0 ⇒ product = 0 ⇒ c = 0.
  • Both zeroes 0 ⇒ b = c = 0 (p(x) = ax²).
  • Zeroes equal ⇒ b² = 4ac.
  • Zeroes doubled (2α, 2β) ⇒ new sum 2(α + β), new product 4αβ (CBSE 2024 Q10).
2.3.2

Cubic polynomials

For a cubic ax³ + bx² + cx + d with zeroes α, β, γ: ax³ + bx² + cx + d = a(x − α)(x − β)(x − γ). Comparing coefficients:

α + β + γ = −b/a · αβ + βγ + γα = c/a · αβγ = −d/a
Signs alternate: −, +, −.

Example 5 (NCERT): verify that 3, −1, −1/3 are zeroes of p(x) = 3x³ − 5x² − 11x − 3, then check the relationships

p(3) = 81 − 45 − 33 − 3 = 0 · p(−1) = −3 − 5 + 11 − 3 = 0 · p(−1/3) = −1/9 − 5/9 + 11/3 − 3 = (−1 − 5 + 33 − 27)/9 = 0 ✓

a = 3, b = −5, c = −11, d = −3.

α + β + γ = 3 − 1 − 1/3 = 5/3 = −b/a ✓

αβ + βγ + γα = −3 + 1/3 − 1 = −11/3 = c/a ✓

αβγ = 3 × (−1) × (−1/3) = 1 = −d/a = 3/3 ✓

Cubic from its zeroes

k[x³ − (α + β + γ)x² + (αβ + βγ + γα)x − αβγ]. Zeroes 1, 2, 3: sum 6, pair-sum 11, product 6 ⇒ x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6.

🧪 Try itCheck with a calculator

For 2x³ − 5x² − 14x + 8, try x = 4, −2 and 1/2 on a calculator: each gives 0. Now check: sum = 5/2 = −b/a, pair-sum = −8 + (−1) + 2 = −7 = c/a, product = −4 = −d/a.

Map

Mind map, formulas and common mistakes

POLYNOMIALS

📈 Graphs

  • Zero = x where the graph meets the x-axis
  • Linear: line, 1 zero (−b/a)
  • Quadratic: parabola, 0/1/2 zeroes; a > 0 ∪, a < 0 ∩
  • Cubic: 1, 2 or 3 zeroes
  • Degree n ⇒ at most n zeroes

🔗 Quadratic

  • Split middle term: p + q = b, pq = ac
  • α + β = −b/a, αβ = c/a
  • k[x² − (sum)x + product]
  • α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ

〰️ Cubic

  • α + β + γ = −b/a
  • αβ + βγ + γα = c/a
  • αβγ = −d/a
  • Signs alternate −, +, −

⚠️ Top 8 mistakes students make

  1. Writing α + β = b/a. It is −b/a.
  2. Writing the polynomial as x² + (sum)x + product. The middle sign is minus.
  3. Counting y-axis crossings or turning points as zeroes.
  4. Saying a quadratic must have 2 zeroes. It can have 2, 1 (touching) or 0 (CBSE 2024 Q20).
  5. Forgetting to verify the relationship when the question says "find the zeroes and verify". That is half the marks.
  6. For t² − 15, writing zeroes as ±15 instead of ±√15.
  7. Dropping the coefficient a: for 3x² − x − 4, αβ = −4/3, not −4.
  8. For cubics, using −d/a for the pair sum. Pair sum is c/a; product is −d/a.

CBSE

CBSE exam pattern and how marks are given

The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics (Standard, 041) paper is 80 marks, 3 hours, 38 questions: Section A 20 × 1 (18 MCQs + 2 assertion–reason), B 5 × 2, C 6 × 3, D 4 × 5, E 3 case studies × 4 (1 + 1 + 2). Unit Algebra carries 20 marks.

✍️ How the CBSE marking scheme splits marks

  • Find zeroes and verify (2–3 marks): 1 for factorising correctly, ½ for the zeroes, ½ + ½ for verifying sum and product (with −b/a and c/a written).
  • Form a polynomial (2 marks): 1 for x² − (sum)x + product, 1 for simplifying with suitable k.
  • Expression in α, β (2 marks): 1 for α + β and αβ, 1 for the identity and the final value.
  • MCQs have no step marks. Re-check signs by putting the zeroes back.

🏆 Answer-writing tips for full marks

  1. Show the splitting: write "x² − 4x + 2x − 8", then the factors.
  2. Write "Sum of zeroes = −b/a = …" and "Product = c/a = …" as two separate lines with ✓.
  3. When forming a polynomial, write "Required polynomial = k(…), taking k = …".
  4. For graph MCQs, mark the meeting points on the question paper with a pencil before counting.

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q4 · 1 markThe graph of y = f(x) is shown: it has two humps and lies entirely above the x-axis. The number of zeroes of f(x) is (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 4

(a) 0. The graph never meets the x-axis, so there is no x with f(x) = 0.

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q20 · 1 mark (A–R)Assertion (A): The polynomial p(y) = y² + 4y + 3 has two zeroes. Reason (R): A quadratic polynomial can have at most two zeroes.

(b). A is true: y² + 4y + 3 = (y + 1)(y + 3), zeroes −1 and −3. R is true, but "at most two" does not explain why this one has exactly two.

CBSE 2026 · 30/1/1 · Q21 · 2 marksIf α and β are the zeroes of the polynomial x² − 3x − 1, find the value of 1/α + 1/β.

α + β = 3, αβ = −1. 1

1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = 3/(−1) = −3. 1

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q1 · 1 markIf α and β are the zeroes of 3x² + 6x + k such that α + β + αβ = −2/3, then the value of k is (a) −8 (b) 8 (c) −4 (d) 4

(d) 4. α + β = −2, αβ = k/3 ⇒ −2 + k/3 = −2/3 ⇒ k = 4.

CBSE 2025 · 30/1/1 · Q22 · 2 marksFind the zeroes of the polynomial p(x) = x² + (4/3)x − 4/3.

3p(x) = 3x² + 4x − 4 = 3x² + 6x − 2x − 4 = 3x(x + 2) − 2(x + 2) 1

= (3x − 2)(x + 2) ⇒ zeroes 2/3 and −2. 1

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q1 · 1 markIf the sum of the zeroes of the polynomial p(x) = 2x² − k√2 x + 1 is √2, then the value of k is (a) √2 (b) 2 (c) 2√2 (d) 1/2

(b) 2. Sum = −(−k√2)/2 = k√2/2 = √2 ⇒ k = 2.

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q10 · 1 markIf the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial x² + px + q are twice the zeroes of 4x² − 5x − 6, then p is (a) −5/2 (b) 5/2 (c) −5 (d) 10

(a) −5/2. Zeroes of 4x² − 5x − 6 have sum 5/4. Doubled zeroes have sum 5/2 = −p ⇒ p = −5/2. (Product doubles twice: 4 × (−3/2) = −6 = q.)

CBSE 2024 · 30/1/1 · Q20 · 1 mark (A–R)Assertion (A): If the graph of a polynomial touches the x-axis at only one point, then the polynomial cannot be a quadratic polynomial. Reason (R): A polynomial of degree n (n > 1) can have at most n zeroes.

(d) A is false, R is true. A quadratic like (x − 1)² touches the x-axis at exactly one point. R is a true fact.

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q7 · 1 markIf α, β are the zeroes of the polynomial p(x) = x² + x − 1, then 1/α + 1/β equals (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) −1 (d) −1/2

(a) 1. α + β = −1, αβ = −1 ⇒ (α + β)/αβ = 1.

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q14 · 1 mark (options reworded)A quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are −2/3 and 2/3 is (a) 4x² − 9 (b) 9x² + 4 (c) 3x² − 2 (d) 5(9x² − 4)

(d). Sum 0, product −4/9 ⇒ k(9x² − 4). With k = 5 we get option (d). (4x² − 9 has zeroes ±3/2.)

CBSE 2023 · 30/1/1 · Q1 · 1 mark (graph)The graph of y = p(x) is given. Find the number of zeroes of p(x). (In the paper the curve met the x-axis at exactly one point.)

1. Count only the points where the curve meets the x-axis.

📈 Pattern spotted

Every year has 2 or 3 one-mark questions here (graph count, k from sum/product, 1/α + 1/β) and often a 2-mark "find the zeroes" question. Master −b/a and c/a and the (α + β)/αβ trick: they cover most of these marks.

Exercise 2.2

1Find the zeroes of the following quadratic polynomials and verify the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients: (i) x² − 2x − 8 (ii) 4s² − 4s + 1 (iii) 6x² − 3 − 7x (iv) 4u² + 8u (v) t² − 15 (vi) 3x² − x − 4

(i) x² − 4x + 2x − 8 = (x − 4)(x + 2). Zeroes 4, −2. Sum = 2 = −(−2)/1 ✓ Product = −8 = −8/1 ✓

(ii) 4s² − 2s − 2s + 1 = (2s − 1)². Zeroes ½, ½. Sum = 1 = −(−4)/4 ✓ Product = ¼ = 1/4 ✓

(iii) 6x² − 7x − 3 = 6x² − 9x + 2x − 3 = (2x − 3)(3x + 1). Zeroes 3/2, −1/3. Sum = 7/6 = −(−7)/6 ✓ Product = −1/2 = −3/6 ✓

(iv) 4u² + 8u = 4u(u + 2). Zeroes 0, −2. Sum = −2 = −8/4 ✓ Product = 0 = 0/4 ✓

(v) t² − 15 = (t − √15)(t + √15). Zeroes √15, −√15. Sum = 0 = −0/1 ✓ Product = −15 = −15/1 ✓

(vi) 3x² − 4x + 3x − 4 = (3x − 4)(x + 1). Zeroes 4/3, −1. Sum = 1/3 = −(−1)/3 ✓ Product = −4/3 ✓

2Find a quadratic polynomial each with the given numbers as the sum and product of its zeroes respectively: (i) 1/4, −1 (ii) √2, 1/3 (iii) 0, √5 (iv) 1, 1 (v) −1/4, 1/4 (vi) 4, 1

Use k[x² − (sum)x + product].

(i) x² − x/4 − 1 ⇒ 4x² − x − 4 (k = 4)

(ii) x² − √2 x + 1/3 ⇒ 3x² − 3√2 x + 1 (k = 3)

(iii) x² − 0·x + √5 = x² + √5

(iv) x² − x + 1

(v) x² + x/4 + 1/4 ⇒ 4x² + x + 1 (k = 4)

(vi) x² − 4x + 1
